The Capability Ladder

Three rulers for the same 149 models. Artificial Analysis re-cuts its index every few months and drops the rungs the frontier has climbed past. Epoch AI fits one ability per model across sixty benchmarks and never throws a rung away. The ladder is Daniel's sum-of-rungs, computed on Epoch's fitted parameters — open at the top, and with the bottom still on the ruler.

What broke on 4 September

The site carries one capability number per model so a reader can place a personality against rough cognitive power. That number was the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index. On 4 September AA released v4.2: GPQA Diamond removed as saturated, two private-set evaluations added, 40% of the weight now on held-out tests. Every model was re-scored, and the frontier deflated by about nine points.

That is not the problem. The problem is what each version does to the models that were never near the frontier. AA scores a model on the current set. A 2023 model that was never run on Terminal-Bench or CritPt gets an "estimated" index near zero — and the benchmarks on which it was distinguishable (MMLU, GPQA, MATH-500, HumanEval) have been retired one by one. The bottom of the scale is erased with each release.

est. is AA's own flag: the model was not run on the current set, the index is inferred. The ladder column is what this page proposes instead.

Three rulers

Each is a defensible instrument for its own purpose. The question is which one measures what a personality corpus needs: a stable, longitudinal ordering that still reaches GPT-3.5 and a 6-billion-parameter chat model from 2023.

The ladder, drawn to scale

Each rung is a benchmark placed at the difficulty Epoch fitted for it — the ability at which a model is expected to score 50% after chance correction. A model sits on the rail at its ability. Its expected score on any rung is a logistic in the distance between the two, so rungs far below a model are credited near-fully and rungs far above it near-zero, without anyone deciding what "saturated" means. Where a real run exists the measured score is used instead.

How the orderings compare

Where Artificial Analysis and Epoch both have a confident measurement, they agree: the rank correlation between AA's index and the ladder is on AA's live v4.2 figures and on the v4.1.1 column the site stored. The ladder does not overturn the frontier ordering. What it changes is visible at the left of the chart: the models AA marks "estimated" collapse onto its floor while the ladder still separates them.

What it costs, and what it rests on

Fitted, not measured, at the bottom. GPT-3.5 Turbo has three of fifteen rungs measured; the other twelve are inferred from its ability. That is the design — a 2023 model cannot be run on a 2026 benchmark it would score zero on — but the site must show the measured fraction beside every number.

Epoch lags. Gemini 3.8 Flash and Muse Spark 1.3 are three days old and not yet in Epoch's table. For those the ladder estimates ability from AA's live per-benchmark row (using AA's benchmarks as extra items calibrated on the 107 models both sources cover), then fits the twelve rungs AA didn't run and uses AA's measured GPQA, HLE and CritPt for the three it did. The calibration is its own check: AA's GPQA fits at difficulty 135 against Epoch's 135.8, HLE 160 against 160.0, CritPt 169 against 167.

The sources disagree at the very top. AA's v4.2 puts Claude Fable 5.1 at 57 and GPT-6 Astra at 55; Epoch puts Astra ten points clear. AA's index is 40% private tests; Epoch's is public runs. Both are honest. The methodology page should say so rather than pick.

Variant collapse. Epoch has one "Grok 4.20"; the corpus has three routes of it. The ladder cannot split what Epoch does not.

Eleven blanks. The stealth Ox Alpha routes (excluded here), the 2023 Qwen and ChatGLM3 tail, Codestral 2508, and GPT-5.3 non-codex, which neither index lists. A blank is the honest cell.
