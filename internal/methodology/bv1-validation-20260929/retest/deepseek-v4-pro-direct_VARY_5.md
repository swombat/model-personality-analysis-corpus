## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW. A lyrical first-person meditation turns a moth at a window into a sustained reflection on longing, attention, and the stories people carry.

## Grounded reading
The voice is intimate, observant, and fond of making small domestic details feel momentous: refrigerator hum, cold floorboards, passing headlights. Its pathos comes from the moth’s futile attraction to a false moon, which the narrator connects to human distraction and unmet desire. The narrator is not simply urging rescue or acceptance; their hesitation becomes part of the piece’s moral tension, questioning whether intervention would be compassion or an intrusion into another creature’s struggle. The closing invitation is to witness what we cannot fully understand, and to notice how a brief encounter can make an otherwise ordinary night feel inhabited.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds the moth, the glass, and the porch light as recurring images of longing blocked or misdirected. It also chooses middle age, domestic routine, private human dramas glimpsed from afar, and the uneasy boundary between helping and imposing a meaning. Its mood moves from nocturnal unease to tenderness and then back toward morning’s ordinary demands.
