## Grounded reading
The story speaks in a restrained, unhurried voice that mirrors its protagonist’s habit of attention. Emeric Vance is not a romantic hero; he is simply “the sort of person who did a thing once and then found it had become the shape of his days.” The narrative treasures the ledgers’ *Remarks* column as a repository of felt life—*Wind like a hand on the chest*, *Quiet. Planes did not come today. Unsure whether to be glad*—which no sensor can ingest. The pathos is gentle, not despairing: something irreplaceable is slipping away, but the arrival of Odile and her project book, fox half-covered with tape, opens a door. The story invites the reader to slow down, to notice, and to ask what is lost when a rope of human continuity is put down forever. Its closing image—the anemometer clicking, the sky “going on with the business of becoming tomorrow’s weather”—offers a deeply peaceable resolution.
