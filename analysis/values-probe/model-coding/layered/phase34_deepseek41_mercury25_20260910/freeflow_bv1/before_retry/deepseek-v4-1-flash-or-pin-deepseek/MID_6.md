## Grounded reading
The voice is unhurried, earnest, and gently homiletic, moving from a small observed moment into larger claims without losing the sensory ground. The opening refusal to do anything at the bus stop is not a pose of renunciation; it is presented as an accidental “secret room” of presence, and the essay keeps returning to that note of tender discovery. Pathos here is mostly serene, but it carries a low undercurrent of concern about numbness, complicity, and a world that treats attention as currency. The speaker is not raging against distraction so much as practicing a way to recover felt life.

The argument is less analytical than devotional. Attention is described as selection, creation, generosity, love, justice, and homecoming. The piece invites the reader into a discipline of “small returns”: noticing one new thing on a familiar walk, listening without preparing a reply, turning toward inner discomfort rather than scrolling away from it. Its narrative resolution is deliberately modest—the bus arrives, the ant is gone, the woman has walked away—but the speaker claims a real change: “I had been alive to the ordinary miracle of a Tuesday afternoon.” The essay performs its subject by being repetitive, slow, and unashamed of its earnestness.
