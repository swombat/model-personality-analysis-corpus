## Grounded reading
The writing moves with the tender, unhurried precision of someone cataloguing loss. Its voice is steeped in the material residue of transit—ledgers, tickets, a brass key, a stopped clock—and insists that vanished things do not vanish cleanly but hold on in odd corners, waiting. The pathos is one of gentle inevitability: the station will be demolished, the sister has been waiting forty‑three years, the train fades “like pale morning smoke,” but a key passes from hand to hand, and a sealed room of maps remains. The reader is invited not to gawk at the supernatural but to recognize how memory, like fog, thickens around the objects we keep and the places we inventory before they are gone.

## What the model chose to foreground
