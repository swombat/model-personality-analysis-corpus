## Grounded reading
The voice is intimate and gently lyrical, finding significance in small sounds—the refrigerator, a settling floorboard, breathing. It treats nighttime solitude as more than loneliness: a private interval when a person can be strange, sad, or hopeful without performing for others. The closing return to the practical thought of going back to sleep keeps the piece modest and immediate. It invites the reader to recognize a shared experience without insisting that everyone feels the same way.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample centers on being awake alone at night, the sensory quiet of a house, and the contrast between public daytime performance and private nighttime thought. It also imagines strangers in different places sharing that hour, making solitude feel faintly communal. The “threshold” between sleep and waking gives the reflection its main image of in-betweenness.
