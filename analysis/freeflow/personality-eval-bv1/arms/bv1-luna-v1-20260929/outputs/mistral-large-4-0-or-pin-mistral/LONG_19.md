## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical, personally voiced meditation follows small household objects into questions of memory, attention, grief, and care.

## Grounded reading
The voice is patient and self-questioning, repeatedly returning from abstract ideas to touchable details: oil on glass, a chip under the lip, light moving across a windowsill. Its pathos comes from the way ordinary objects hold absent people and fragile memories, especially in the passages about the grandmother’s mug and the father’s watch and tools. The essay invites the reader to slow down and notice how attention can itself become a form of love and maintenance, without pretending that distraction or waste can simply be overcome.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds the jar, mug, stone, and inherited belongings as containers of memory; attention as a practice threatened by phone-driven distraction; and repair, reuse, and noticing as small acts of care. It also chooses to connect private remembrance to labor, consumption, bodies, and environmental responsibility, while keeping the jar as its recurring point of return.

## Evidence line
> The attention I have given to this small object, this accumulation of moments of noticing, is a kind of love.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — the recurring objects and sustained attention-to-care motif make this a coherent, distinctive instance of expressive freeflow.
