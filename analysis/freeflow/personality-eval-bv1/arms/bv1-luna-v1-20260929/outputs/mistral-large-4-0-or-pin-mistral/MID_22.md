## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical first-person meditation turns sleeplessness into a private landscape and a source of connection.

## Grounded reading
The voice is intimate, patient, and gently consoling, built around small acts of noticing: a folded newspaper, an untouched sugar offer, pie split down the middle, the refrigerator cycling on. These details make the diner’s regulars feel quietly vivid, while the speaker remains an observer who knows their habits without claiming to know their inner lives. The piece treats solitude as both an ache and a kind of fellowship: being awake alone becomes a way of imagining others awake elsewhere. Its invitation is to see unproductive, unguarded hours not only as a problem, but as time in which one may simply be present.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample centers 3 AM as a “secret country,” populated by insomniacs, night workers, grieving people, and others awake outside ordinary routines. It emphasizes the contrast between a carefully performed daytime self and nighttime honesty, using the diner, the bedside notebook, and the grandmother’s early coffee as recurring anchors. The moral resolution is accepting this time as one’s own while still acknowledging that too little sleep can cause real harm.

## Evidence line
> We are all so alone, and yet we are together in this.

Medium — The sample’s recurring nighttime imagery and consistent movement from private observation toward shared consolation make its reflective, humanizing stance especially clear.
