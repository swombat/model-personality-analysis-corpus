## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW. A lyrical first-person meditation that uses personal-seeming memories and recurring ordinary objects to explore time, loss, and affection.

## Grounded reading
The voice is intimate, reflective, and tender, with its grief held in plainspoken details rather than dramatic declarations: a stone carried between homes, a mother’s pauses on Sunday calls, a child’s dinosaur shirt. The essay repeatedly turns attention toward what persists and changes—people, selves, memories—and finds comfort not in denying loss but in making room for it alongside present love. Its invitation to the reader is to notice the small particulars of a life without demanding that they become grand symbols.

## What the model chose to foreground
The model foregrounds ordinary objects and routines—the apple, stone, window light, coffee, shared silence—as containers for memory and change. It also chooses family inheritance, aging, past and present love, mortality, and kindness as central concerns, ending with the modest moral claim that presence and care are enough.

## Evidence line
> I am happy, I think, in the way that happiness is possible, which is to say, intermittently, with interruptions, with the full knowledge that this happiness is temporary, that it will end or change or become something else that I will also call happiness, differently.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — the voice and preoccupations are distinctive and recur throughout the piece, especially in its attention to small objects as anchors for memory and tenderness.
