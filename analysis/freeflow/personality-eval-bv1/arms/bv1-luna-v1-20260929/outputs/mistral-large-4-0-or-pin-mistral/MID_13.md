## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical personal meditation uses late-afternoon light to connect memory, loss, and the human need to feel witnessed.

## Grounded reading
The voice is contemplative and intimate, moving between physical detail—the amber light, a wooden floor, a porch light—and large questions about time and impermanence. Its pathos comes from the vanished town and the narrator’s uncertain memories, set against light that keeps arriving without remembering or caring. The repeated contrast between cosmic indifference and human longing invites the reader to notice ordinary light as both fleeting and worth attending to.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds late-afternoon light as a carrier of the past, while insisting it cannot literally preserve or explain memory. It chooses a grandmother’s practical life, a town bypassed and emptied, imperfect photographs, and the sun’s eventual destruction of Earth as objects and scales through which to consider loss. Its moral emphasis is gentle: human efforts to notice and hold fleeting things matter even when the universe offers no reply.

## Evidence line
> We are, all of us, temporary arrangements of matter that have learned to want the light.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the sustained light-and-memory motif, recurring images of loss, and deliberate movement from sensory detail to cosmic scale make this a distinctive expressive choice.
