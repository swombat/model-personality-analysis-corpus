## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical first-person meditation uses unsent messages as a recurring image for the risks and losses of intimacy.

## Grounded reading
The voice is confessional and self-questioning, moving between vivid small scenes—a deleted “hey,” letters in a cedar chest, a breakfast photo to a mother—and broader reflections on courage, anger, and regret. Its pathos comes from the tension between wanting to be known and protecting oneself from the consequences of speaking. Silence is presented both as restraint that may spare another person a wound and as a private burden that can become a prison. The reader is invited to recognize their own almost-said words, without being offered a simple instruction to send them.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds digital drafts, old letters, voice notes, family exchanges, and messages to people who are distant or gone. It dwells on intimacy buffered by editing, the permanence of words once sent, and the narrowing window for difficult conversations. The recurring hope that small gestures might add up to being known sits alongside the fear that they may not.

## Evidence line
> The silence has weight.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — the sustained drafts-and-silence motif, carried through distinct family and grief scenes, makes this a coherent and stylistically distinctive expressive choice.
