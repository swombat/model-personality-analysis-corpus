## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A sustained, quietly magical story uses a museum of unrealized possibilities to explore grief, memory, and the ethics of consolation.

## Grounded reading
The voice is measured and observant, finding emotional weight in practical details: a missing button, a swollen window, the inconvenience of scaffolding. Its pathos comes from refusing to make the impossible reunion a simple gift. Daniel resembles Mara’s husband but does not fully know her; contact offers comfort and also draws her away from her living life. The narrator’s growing attachment to the exhibit is gently implicated, not excused. The ending invites the reader toward repair and ordinary continuation: Mara does not recover the room she imagined, but makes a place to read and mends the narrator’s sleeve.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story centers on unrealized domestic plans, the instability of memory, and the difficulty of knowing whether an apparition is a person or only a convincing possibility. Repeated objects—the doorknob, cushion, coat button, and reading room—make grief tangible without resolving it into a clean lesson. Its moral emphasis is on care with boundaries: preservation can become a form of waiting, and consolation can exact a cost.

## Evidence line
> We had learned to let practical failures acquire philosophical dignity.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — the controlled voice, recurring domestic objects, and ethically complicated resolution make this a distinctive, coherent expression rather than a generic magical-realist premise.
