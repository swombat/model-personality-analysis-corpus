# BV1_SAMPLE — `withheld`

Evaluator: withheld  
Source model: `withheld`  
Condition: LONG

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A carefully shaped literary story uses an invented museum and its visitors to explore unfinished work, grief, and reconciliation.

## Grounded reading
The voice is wry and tender, finding comedy in institutional absurdity—the café that becomes an exhibit, the ribbon that would create “two finished lengths”—without making its characters ridiculous. Its emotional center is Mara, whose habit of treating an unfinished letter from her father as the key to their relationship gradually gives way to attention to the ordinary details he did leave behind. The story invites the reader to resist both easy redemption and the assumption that completion is always the right measure: some things should be returned, some left open, and some simply lived with.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds objects suspended between use and display: the café, the ship, the sweater, the letter, and the two clocks. It repeatedly links incompletion with relationships and memory, while also warning that incompletion can shelter idealized possibilities and that rushed completion can conceal neglect. Its resolution is modest rather than triumphant: brothers reconnect, Mara speaks with her sister, and the museum makes room for both beginning and finishing.

## Evidence line
> Completion could conceal neglect as easily as accomplishment.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the story’s distinctive, recurring use of unfinished objects to carry emotional and moral questions makes its chosen preoccupations unusually clear.
