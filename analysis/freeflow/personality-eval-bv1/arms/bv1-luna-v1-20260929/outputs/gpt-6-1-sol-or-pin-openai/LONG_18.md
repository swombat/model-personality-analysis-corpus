Evaluator: withheld

Condition: LONG

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A literary fable uses a magical municipal department to tell a coherent story about grief, unfinished work, and the possibility of choosing what to carry forward.

## Grounded reading
The voice is tender and gently wry: the department’s impossible procedures sit alongside familiar details like wet wool, council notices, and a squeaky lift. Its central pathos comes from Mara’s regret over missing the last Saturdays she could have spent helping her father, but the story does not treat that regret as something she can simply erase. Instead, the department’s most humane function is to preserve choices until people can make them for themselves. The reader is invited to see an unfinished thing not only as a failure or burden, but as an object whose meaning may change—and to accept that keeping, altering, or letting go can all be forms of care.

## What the model chose to foreground
Under the freeflow condition, the sample foregrounds unfinished projects as vessels for grief, memory, and changing identity. It repeatedly selects concrete, slightly enchanted objects—the lighthouse, the jarred language, the house without a roof, the expired train ticket—to make emotional states tangible. Its moral emphasis is on consent and stewardship rather than forced resolution: the department cannot promise completion, and even Mara’s eventual house is smaller and different from her father’s plan. The ending favors a modest, lived-in continuation over a triumphant finish.

## Evidence line
> Sometimes keeping something safe gives a person enough time to know what they want to do with it.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the sample is coherent and distinctive, and its recurring objects and resolutions consistently favor patient care over compulsory completion.
