# BV1_SAMPLE — `withheld`

Evaluator: withheld  
Source model: `withheld`  
Condition: LONG

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION. A polished, gently whimsical short story uses an invented municipal department to explore unfinished work, grief, and the limits of what can be saved.

## Grounded reading
The voice is warm and observant, finding humor in bureaucratic absurdities without making its characters into jokes. Its pathos comes through small acts of care: the tissues beside the stamp pad, Mara giving Nico “the small privacy available in a public square,” and people working side by side without declaring a movement. The story invites the reader to see unfinished things not simply as failures or promises, but as objects that can hold grief, relief, and changing attachment. Its resolution is notably modest: the department closes, Mara does not finish her novel, and people keep making room for one another anyway.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story centers abandoned projects as both physical objects and emotional burdens, with particular weight given to a boat left unfinished by a father and son. It foregrounds institutional limits—space, money, time, attention—alongside the humane work of preserving, returning, or letting go of things. Its chosen mood is tender and lightly comic, and its moral emphasis is on care without forced completion: people can help each other begin again without promising that everything will be finished or saved.

## Evidence line
> She did not save everything.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The recurring attention to unfinished work, unforced compassion, and small, practical gestures makes the sample distinctive and coherent evidence of those choices.
