Evaluator: withheld

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION. A polished, gently magical short story built around an impossible lost-and-found department.

## Grounded reading
The voice is wry and tender, making room for jokes about forms and procedures without letting them undercut grief. Its central pathos is the gap between recovering a memory and being able—or wanting—to return to the life attached to it. Mara’s choice to remember a happy evening without reopening her old decision gives the story a quiet, humane resolution. The invitation to the reader is compassionate but unsentimental: loss can be named, and finding what is gone does not require reclaiming it.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story turns lost objects into lost smells, languages, courage, trust, and possible futures. It foregrounds grief and regret, but also the limits of repair: the department cannot manufacture trust or recover time in usable quantities. Its recurring doors and records give these abstractions a practical, almost bureaucratic shape; its moral emphasis is that remembering can matter without undoing the past.

## Evidence line
> For thirty years, she had confused regretting the loss with regretting the decision.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the story’s recurring lost-and-found imagery and its nuanced distinction between recovery and restoration make for a coherent, distinctive sample.
