## Grounded reading
The voice is calm, measured, and gently persuasive. It acknowledges grief, anxiety, disability, exhaustion, and the limits of walking rather than treating a stroll as a cure. Its pathos comes from small, concrete moments—the rosemary by the bus stop, the sparrow in a puddle—that make ordinary life feel more available to notice. It invites the reader to leave space between seeing and judging, and to let an experience matter without turning it into productivity or proof.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay foregrounds walking as a way to encounter detail, regain proportion, and feel a modest belonging to place. It contrasts attention with speed, instrumental habits, and quick judgment, while insisting that noticing is an opportunity rather than a virtue. Its recurring objects—streets, plants, birds, windows, weather—make ordinary surroundings the setting for that argument.
