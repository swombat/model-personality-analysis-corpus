## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION. A gently whimsical short story built around a library where unfinished things are allowed to remain unfinished.

## Grounded reading
The voice is tender and understated, finding emotional weight in small gestures rather than dramatic revelations: a man straightening a tiny deck chair, a woman resting her pen against her cheek, a librarian checking the ship. The story makes room for grief, changing interests, and uncertainty without insisting that they become lessons or achievements. Its invitation is to treat unfinished work with patience—and to let the reader sit beside it without having to resolve it.

## What the model chose to foreground
The chosen center is a library of abandoned projects: half-knitted scarves, unsent cards, an unfinished ship, and a comic left behind for another interest. The story repeatedly favors permission over pressure, culminating in a paper sail that is only being tried out, not attached. Its warmth comes from the ordinary setting and the moral claim that something can still matter even if it is never completed.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The story’s coherent return to unfinished objects, gentle acceptance, and small acts of care makes those choices a clear signal in this sample.
