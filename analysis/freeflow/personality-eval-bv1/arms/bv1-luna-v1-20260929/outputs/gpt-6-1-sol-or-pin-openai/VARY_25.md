## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION. This is a complete, gently magical short story built around a municipal repairman who tends to emotional troubles as if they were tangible objects.

## Grounded reading
The voice is tender and quietly wry, using small absurdities—a splinter of Monday, a hungry silence—to make emotional pain approachable without making it trivial. Its deepest pathos comes from what cannot be repaired: a final conversation with a dead father, the loss of joy, the hurtful things people cannot take back. Ellis offers not reversal but care: a box that makes a memory “easier to carry,” or company while the soup is made. The story invites the reader to notice small, unasked-for kindnesses and to accept that some burdens need tending rather than fixing.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds grief, honest speech, ordinary acts of care, and the limits of repair. It gives those themes a recurring material vocabulary—jars, tools, a kettle, a loose hinge—while returning to the idea that help can make pain more bearable without erasing it. Its ending extends that care to the caretaker himself: Ellis’s joy does not return, but other people gather around him.

## Evidence line
> He knew how to repair things because he had broken a great many of them.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the sustained repair motif and specific preference for consolation over miraculous resolution make this sample distinctive and coherent.
