## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A literary short story built around an inherited clock shop, an unresolved family absence, and a town’s attachment to a one-minute discrepancy.

## Grounded reading
The voice is measured and quietly wry, using precise objects and small exchanges to carry grief rather than announcing it. The narrator’s return to the shop turns postponed calls, unfinished repairs, and the station clock’s extra minute into related forms of delay. The story invites the reader to ask not only whether something is wrong, but what it has been doing for the people who live with it. Its resolution favors an imperfect, negotiated arrangement over either easy nostalgia or wholesale correction.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds clocks, repairs, inherited work, delayed decisions, and the customs a community preserves after their original purpose fades. Its moral emphasis is on looking closely before changing something, while its emotional center is the narrator’s regret and gradual reconnection with both the aunt’s work and the town. The extra minute becomes a chosen image for mercy, memory, and the space people make for one another.
