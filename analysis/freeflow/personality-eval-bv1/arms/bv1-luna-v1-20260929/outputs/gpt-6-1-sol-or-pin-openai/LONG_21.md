## Grounded reading
The voice is patient, observant, and lightly wry: people expect “a spectacular object,” while Mara’s work begins with looking carefully. Its pathos gathers around ordinary remnants—a coat with a pencil in its pocket, a mended shirt, a broken toy boat—and treats grief without making it grand or easily solved. The story invites the reader to value practical care: not restoring the past, but making things usable, sharing the work, and allowing uncertainty to remain.

## What the model chose to foreground
The chosen world is full of small mechanisms and overlooked civic spaces: loose pins, clocks, a shuttered pool, and a repair department that remains “provisional.” The story links its recurring concern with repair to a moral distinction: some damage can be fixed, some cannot, and care does not require pretending otherwise. Its mood stays humane and quietly hopeful, with companionship growing through shared work rather than dramatic revelation.
