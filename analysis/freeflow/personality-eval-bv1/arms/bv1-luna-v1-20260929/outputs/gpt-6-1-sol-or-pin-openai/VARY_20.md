## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A quiet magical-realist story uses a shop that recovers minor losses to explore grief, regret, and what memory can and cannot restore.

## Grounded reading
The voice is restrained and attentive to small physical details: a button’s scratch, a cup warming the narrator’s fingers, a strawberry seed on Elise’s wrist. The shop’s magic makes room for grief without promising to undo it. The narrator cannot change the last call with his father, but can remember what came after the words he had been stuck on. The story invites the reader to notice how regret can narrow a memory—and how recovering its fuller shape may be enough.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story centers ordinary losses rather than grand revelations: a coat button, a brief stretch of unselfconscious happiness, and a final phone call. Its moral emphasis is on accepting the irreversibility of the past while allowing memory to contain more than its most painful moment. The repeated acts of bringing, sitting, and holding objects give the magic a gentle, practical mood.

## Evidence line
> For years, I had stopped at my father’s words.
