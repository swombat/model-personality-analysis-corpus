## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A quiet, gently whimsical short story organized around a recurring motif of unfinished objects.

## Grounded reading
The voice is restrained and tender, using small comic details—the museum’s unreliable hours, the “only official stamp”—to make room for grief without turning it sentimental. Its emotional center is the boy’s unfinished wooden bird and Mara’s patient refusal to take over: care means staying close while letting someone decide what happens next. The story invites the reader to see incompletion not only as failure or loss, but as something that can remain meaningful without being neatly resolved.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds unfinished creative work, memory, and grief through a museum of abandoned or interrupted things. Its moral emphasis is gentle permission: people may reclaim what they left behind, not know what they want, and continue in their own way. Even after the boy completes the second wing, the bird’s unstarted feet preserve the story’s sense that completion is not a simple endpoint.

## Evidence line
