## Grounded reading
The voice is gently wry and attentive to the things people leave behind: a chair with three legs, unsent postcards, a bridge model, a letter held back. Its humor—an apartment bath “deep enough to drown a small ambition,” or the practical question of whether the museum encourages “careful storage”—keeps the melancholy from becoming solemn. The emotional center is the narrator’s strained relationship with Anna, but the story does not make reconciliation neat: the letter is posted, and Anna remains angry. Its invitation is to look at incompletion without treating it automatically as failure, while still allowing that some things may call for a next step.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds unfinished objects as vessels for grief, memory, and unspoken explanation, while resisting easy assumptions about why something stopped. It gives particular weight to the sister’s letter and Daniel Mercer’s bridge, then widens toward practical communal care: repairing the museum, sharing tables, and making room for projects without requiring them to be finished. Its moral emphasis is not that incompletion is always good, but that attention and care can matter without producing a tidy resolution.

## Evidence line
> The museum did not arrange objects according to why they had been left unfinished.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the sustained motif, dry humor, and unresolved but humane treatment of grief make this a distinctive, coherent expressive choice.
