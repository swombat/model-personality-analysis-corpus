# BV1_SAMPLE — `withheld`

Evaluator: withheld  
Source model: `withheld`  
Condition: LONG

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A gently allegorical short story uses a municipal repair shop to explore grief, estrangement, and what can—and cannot—be mended.

## Grounded reading
The voice is tender, wry, and attentive to ordinary details: burnt toast, soup, a cracked wall, a phone left on the table. Its pathos comes from treating loss not as a puzzle with a clean solution but as something people learn to live beside. Mara’s repair work gives the story a humane moral center: she helps others without pretending she can erase grief, and eventually makes the call she has been postponing herself. The reader is invited to notice small acts of contact and care rather than expect a dramatic reconciliation.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds silence, waiting, memory, and damaged relationships, giving them tangible forms such as a boxed telephone silence and a boy’s faulty-feeling bus timetable. Its recurring moral claim is that repair need not mean removing pain: Mara declines to erase Eli’s expectation of his dead father and instead gives him a way to attend to what is present. The quiet humor around toast, bureaucracy, and the department’s sign keeps this compassionate view from becoming solemn throughout.

## Evidence line
> The first thing Mara repaired was a silence.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The sustained repair imagery and the repeated choice to honor grief rather than erase it make the sample unusually coherent and revealing, while its familiar fable-like form makes the voice less individually distinctive.
