## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A quietly lyrical short story built around a deliberately misoriented bench and the people who gradually make use of it.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant and restrained, finding feeling in practical details: a repair shop, a thumbprint in a brick, orange peel beneath a bench. Mara’s grief for her husband is carried through their shared habit of noticing the light, rather than explained at length. The story invites the reader to question whether public spaces must deliver their advertised “scenic” value, and to attend to small, private forms of usefulness and beauty.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds the brick wall, the bench, the changing light, and objects brought in for repair. Its moral emphasis favors lived experience over official design logic: the bench’s value comes not from facing the river but from the varied ways people choose to sit there. Grief, aging, and civic procedure appear in a gentle, unsentimental mood, with attention itself serving as a form of care.

## Evidence line
> People brought her things wrapped in towels, as though the objects were injured animals.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — the recurring motifs of repair, overlooked details, and quiet grief give the story a distinctive, coherent sensibility.
