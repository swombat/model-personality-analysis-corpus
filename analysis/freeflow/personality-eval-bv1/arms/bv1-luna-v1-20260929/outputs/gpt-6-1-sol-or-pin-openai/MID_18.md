Evaluator: withheld
Source model: `withheld`
Condition: MID

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A lyrical short story uses a fantastical museum to explore unfinished objects, family grief, and the possibility of making an ending together.

## Grounded reading
The narrator’s voice is observant, gently wry, and careful with other people’s silences. The story gives unfinished things emotional weight without treating them as problems to solve: an unsent card stays available to the woman who donated it, and a child’s search for his mother’s song becomes an opening for them to make something together. Its pathos is restrained, with grief carried through small gestures—the boy going still, his mother folding her hands, the narrator reaching for a tissue “I pretended to need.” The reader is invited to see care not as preserving everything unchanged, but as making room for living people to decide what comes next.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds stalled time and unfinished things: a clock fixed at half past four, abandoned projects, unsent words, and a song that stops before its final verse. It treats silence as something that can protect or burden, and makes the narrator’s moral shift—from guarding incompletion as a rule to helping a family approach an ending—the story’s resolution. The clock moving at the end gives that change a quiet, hopeful shape.

## Evidence line

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the story is distinctive and coherent, with recurring images and moral emphasis that make its preference for patient, humane care especially legible.
