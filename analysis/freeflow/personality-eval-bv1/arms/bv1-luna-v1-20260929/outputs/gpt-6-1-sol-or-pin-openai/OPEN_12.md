GENRE_FICTION — A compact literary fable built around a museum of unfinished things.

## Grounded reading
The voice is gentle, whimsical, and quietly elegiac, finding tenderness in incompletion without treating it as failure. The curator’s careful tending and the child’s note give the story its emotional turn: unfinished work may still be alive, or finished in a way that only looks imperfect. The reader is invited to reconsider what counts as completion, with the ending offering renewal rather than a neat resolution.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story centers on abandoned plans and objects—a one-oared boat, an interrupted map, an unsigned letter, an unfinished garden—and on the people who leave or care for them. Its moral emphasis is that incompletion can have its own value, while the ending suggests that appearances are not a reliable measure of whether something is finished. The recurring acts of labeling, erasing, watering, and revising make attention itself a form of care.
