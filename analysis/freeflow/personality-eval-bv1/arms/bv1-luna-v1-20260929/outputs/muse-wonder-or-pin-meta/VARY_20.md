Evaluator: withheld

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A polished magical-realist fable about a town’s possible lives and one woman’s choice to keep them open.

## Grounded reading
The voice is whimsical and composed, with its strangeness delivered in practical, gently comic details: tea for “geography misbehaving,” maps that resent creases, and a river that declines to apologize. Beneath that lightness is real grief: Mara’s mother is “furniture” in her life, and the wind’s return of her voice makes loss newly immediate. The story invites the reader to treat memory and unrealized possibility not as errors to correct, but as forms of life worth making room for.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample centers on maps, rivers, doors, and other things that hold or redirect what might have been. It pairs mourning with curiosity, and sets the appeal of safety against a larger, stranger life. Mara’s final choice favors openness, even though the story does not promise that openness will be easy.

## Evidence line
> If Mara kept it unfolded until midnight, the town would stay as it was becoming: stranger, larger inside, haunted in useful ways.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The recurring imagery and coherent movement from grief toward possibility give the story a distinctive imaginative signature, while its tightly shaped fable form concentrates that evidence in a single mode.
