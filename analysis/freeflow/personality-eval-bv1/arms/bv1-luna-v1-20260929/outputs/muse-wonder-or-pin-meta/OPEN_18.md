## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical miniature essay built around an imagined museum of unfinished things.

## Grounded reading
The voice is gentle, observant, and quietly consoling. It turns ordinary interruptions—a cold cup of tea, an unsent letter, a half-finished scarf—into traces of attention and feeling rather than evidence of failure. The essay’s pathos is restrained: it acknowledges regret and loss, but makes room for choices and unfinished stories that have come to rest. Its invitation to the reader is to recognize their own half-done things without rushing to justify or complete them.

## What the model chose to foreground
The model chooses incompletion as its central subject, especially the small domestic objects and private moments that record interruption, hesitation, or unspoken feeling. It gives moral weight to accepting that some things can stop and still become “a different shape” of completion. The recurring museum rooms and final bench make recognition and permission—not productivity or redemption—the piece’s resolution.

## Evidence line
> Not everything is a failure because it stopped.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The sample offers a coherent, distinctive pattern of finding compassion and meaning in ordinary unfinished things, reinforced across its objects, rooms, and closing invitation.
