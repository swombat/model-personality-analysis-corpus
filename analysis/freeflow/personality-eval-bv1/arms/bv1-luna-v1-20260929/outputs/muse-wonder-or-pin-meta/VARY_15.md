## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A lyrical, gently fable-like story follows Mina as private grief becomes a shared community project.

## Grounded reading
The voice is warm, patient, and openly sentimental, finding its emotional force in small acts of attention: a repaired clock, shared tea, neighbors carrying salvaged planks. Grief is not overcome so much as given somewhere to live; imagination turns into a place where others can bring memories and be welcomed. The story invites the reader to see care and companionship as practical forms of repair, while leaving room for wonder in its moons, tide clock, and “official sister island.”

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds bereavement, imagined places, and communal care. Its recurring objects—the lighthouse, notebooks, clocks, maps, and two moons—bind Mina’s family losses to her growing connection with Tomas and the town. The central moral claim is that an imagined refuge can become real through shared work, especially when hardship damages it.
