## Sample kind
GENERIC_ESSAY — A polished, coherent public-facing essay argues that attention is both a personal discipline and a moral and political good.

## Grounded reading
The voice is reflective and gently exhortative, using familiar scenes—a rainy window, a book, an unhurried conversation—to make attention feel like a way of living rather than a productivity technique. It frames distraction as a threat not only to concentration but to love, ethical regard, and democratic judgment. The reader is invited to practice small forms of slowness and to see them as acts of returning to the world.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay foregrounds phones, feeds, books, walking, boredom, writing, and face-to-face conversation as tests or practices of attention. Its central moral claim is that what we attend to is what we value: distraction can blunt our care for other people and the wider world, while sustained attention can deepen connection and freedom. It also treats attention as resistance to a commercial environment designed to capture it.

## Evidence line
> Attention is not merely a cognitive skill.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the essay sustains a clear moral vocabulary of attention, care, and resistance, though its polished, familiar public-essay style is not especially distinctive.
