EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical first-person meditation that turns ordinary observations into a recurring argument about attention, thought, and impermanence.

## Grounded reading
The voice is contemplative and gently invitational, asking the reader to slow down and notice rather than strive to capture or control experience. Its pathos comes from the passing of time and the smallness of ordinary moments: a tree outgrowing memory, a leaf on pavement, old letters and diaries. The essay’s warmth is balanced by a quiet unease about distraction and finite attention, but it resolves in openness rather than alarm: the world offers “doors,” and the reader is invited to become quiet enough to recognize them.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds unplanned thought, attentive walking, ordinary sensory details, aging, impermanence, and the cost of giving attention to screens. It repeatedly treats receptivity as an alternative to productivity and control, using images of gardens, doors, light, cracked sidewalks, and passing blossoms to make that value concrete.
