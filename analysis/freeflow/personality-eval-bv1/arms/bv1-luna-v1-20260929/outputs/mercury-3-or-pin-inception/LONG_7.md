## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical, persuasive essay that uses a personal park-bench scene to make an argument for boredom as a condition of inner life.

## Grounded reading
The voice is warm and assured, moving between intimate observation and public argument. It treats boredom not as a void but as a space where memory, imagination, empathy, and private thought can unfold. The emotional thread is a quiet alarm about losing that space to screens and constant stimulation, balanced by an invitation to reclaim it through small acts of unhurried attention. Its strongest appeal is relational: silence and unstimulated time can make room for other people rather than pushing them away.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds the phone-less park bench, ordinary pauses, repetitive household tasks, shared silence, and the private mind. It casts attention as both a personal resource and something contested by the feed economy, while presenting boredom as useful, humane, and sometimes uncomfortable. The argument also acknowledges that leisure is unequally available, rather than treating idleness as a universal remedy.

## Evidence line
> The people we love are not supposed to be a constant stream of novelty.
