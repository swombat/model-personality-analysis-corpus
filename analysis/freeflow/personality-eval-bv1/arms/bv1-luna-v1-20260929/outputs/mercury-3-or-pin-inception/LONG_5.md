## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION. A literary, first-person ghost story whose supernatural ambiguity supports a sustained meditation on grief and memory.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant, intimate, and gently uncanny, finding emotional meaning in the house’s ordinary noises, worn surfaces, and changing light. Its central pathos is shared loneliness: narrator, house, and the dead are each figured as waiting, with attention becoming a way to keep company and bear loss. The reader is invited not so much to solve a haunting as to listen—to treat places and objects as carriers of lives that might otherwise be forgotten.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds an old house as both setting and memory-keeper, with recurring attention to creaks, dust, wallpaper, wood grain, and the pale rectangle where a photograph once hung. It links the narrator’s private sadness to the Hendersons’ bereavement and their son’s death in war, then makes listening and witnessing a moral obligation: “We are all made of the past, and we have a duty to keep it.” The ending turns the house’s waiting into a shared human condition rather than a resolved mystery.
