## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW. A reflective literary meditation built around family keepsakes, memory, and the stories people leave for themselves.

## Grounded reading
The voice is intimate, wistful, and attentive to small sensory details: rain in the gutters, cedar, fading ink, a loose ribbon. Its pathos comes from the gap between physical remnants and the feelings they can no longer fully preserve, especially in the great-uncle’s postcards, where distance is felt through brief, mundane messages. The piece invites the reader to treat objects not as reliable records of the past but as prompts for reconsidering it—and for imagining who one might become.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample centers on an attic full of inherited and personal objects: postcards, letters, photographs, a thimble, buttons, and a record. It foregrounds memory as something reconstructed rather than recovered, and presents keepsakes as “anchors” or “bottled messages” that connect past selves to the present. The mood moves from post-rain stillness and loneliness toward a gentle, open-ended invitation to answer the questions those objects raise.

## Evidence line
> The attic is a graveyard of younger selves.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the repeated linking of objects, memory, distance, and self-reflection makes the sample’s chosen preoccupations clear, though its polished, familiar literary language is less distinctive evidence.
