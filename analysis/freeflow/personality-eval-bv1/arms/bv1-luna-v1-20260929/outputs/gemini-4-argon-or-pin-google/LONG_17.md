## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical, highly structured essay whose recurring images and intimate closing invitation give it a distinctive voice beyond a conventional public-intellectual argument.

## Grounded reading
The voice moves between explanatory confidence and elegy: clocks, memory, biology, and digital archives become ways to think about how change both erases and renews. Its pathos gathers around specific losses—the unidentified man in the photograph, the dead friend in the phone clip, the grandfather and the unrecorded heron—without treating preservation as either wholly foolish or wholly comforting. The reader is invited to notice the present without converting it into another keepsake or lesson.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay repeatedly foregrounds dust, worn and broken objects, clocks, photographs, bodily change, and the tension between remembering and letting go. Its central moral claim is that impermanence is not merely damage to resist: forgetting and unkept moments can make room for renewal and attention. That set of themes and images recurs across the sections, from the abandoned house returning to the forest to the final scene of the heron disappearing.

Medium — the sustained return to decay, memory, and tender non-attachment makes the sample unusually revealing, while its carefully composed essay form offers less evidence about voice outside this mode.
