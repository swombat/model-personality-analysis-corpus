## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A polished literary short story built around a cartographer who preserves demolished places through sensory memory.

## Grounded reading
The voice is tender and meticulously observant, finding grief not only in lost people but in the rooms and routines that held a life. Its pathos comes through concrete details—the door that scrapes, the sun across the rug, the key Elena can no longer use—rather than dramatic declarations. Arthur’s gentle questions invite the reader to attend closely to ordinary spaces and treat their particulars as worth preserving.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds place as a vessel for memory, especially the intimate geography of domestic life. It sets sensory, personal knowledge—smells, sounds, worn floors—against redevelopment’s glass, steel, and wrecking balls. Its moral emphasis is that attention can honor what has been erased: Arthur cannot restore Elena’s apartment physically, but he can make its remembered shape legible and lasting.

## Evidence line
> A place was not made of drywall and joists.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The story’s recurring sensory details and consistent care for ordinary losses make its chosen preoccupations unusually clear and distinctive.
