## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION. A lyrical speculative-fantasy story whose central metaphor develops into a clear emotional resolution.

## Grounded reading
The voice is hushed and richly sensory, building loneliness through small, tangible details: tea on a brass stove, jars trembling with captured sounds, and the remembered yellow door and terrier. Its pathos comes from Julian’s fear that he has preserved other people’s losses while erasing his own. The ending invites the reader to see grief not only as something to store or escape, but as a connection to a life that can be reclaimed—even when reclaiming it hurts.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds forgotten language, grief, solitude, and the ethics of preserving what others have let go. Its recurring objects—the jars, copper net, lighthouse, and warm recovered sound—turn memory into something both collectible and intimate. The final choice is restoration over preservation: Julian stops treating the lost memory as an artifact and lets himself feel it.

## Evidence line
> A museum of things nobody wanted anymore, preserved by a man nobody remembered.
