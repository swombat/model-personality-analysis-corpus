## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW. This is a lyrical essay whose recurring sensory images and elegiac mood give it more stylistic distinctiveness than a generic public-intellectual essay.

## Grounded reading
The voice is attentive, melancholy, and deliberately tactile: it lingers over the sound of a rotary phone, the feel of a floppy disk shutter, the smell of an old book, and the hum of an empty mall. Its pathos comes from treating obsolete objects not just as lost technology but as traces of vanished habits, relationships, and versions of ourselves. The essay resists simple nostalgia, naming the cruelties and inconveniences of the past and arguing that forgetting makes room for thought and creation. It invites the reader to look at familiar things—especially the device in hand—as future ruins, and to value them without expecting them to last.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds abandoned malls, obsolete media, dead websites, fading language, and the sensory rituals technology once required. Across these subjects, it returns to impermanence: human structures are temporary, tools shape intimacy and attention, and forgetting is necessary as well as painful. Its final emphasis is not despair but tenderness toward the present, framed through recurring images of hands, rust, moss, and tides.

## Evidence line
> We are not carving the final draft of human history into diamond; we are building sandcastles between tides.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the essay sustains a distinctive sensory focus and a consistent argument about impermanence, though its polished, thesis-driven form shapes how that voice appears.
