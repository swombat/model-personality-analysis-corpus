## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A sustained first-person literary narrative follows an aging surveyor whose maps capture changing, sensory, and emotional realities that official cartography leaves out.

## Grounded reading
The voice is ornate, patient, and quietly defiant. Its central tenderness is for what cannot be fixed in place: tides, echoes, city smells, grief’s alteration of a familiar room. The narrator’s attention to materials and sensory detail gives the piece its pathos, especially when Clara’s absence makes the parlor feel vast and hostile. The ending invites the reader to notice their own surroundings as lived, shifting places rather than finished diagrams.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample repeatedly elevates movement and perception over stable measurement: shifting sandbars, sound-shaped terrain, smells that divide a city, and rooms reshaped by bereavement. It also gives practical and moral weight to neglected knowledge—the cockle-gatherer’s safety, the doctor’s disease map, the fisherman’s hazards—while setting that knowledge against bureaucratic standardization. The final gesture of leaving the last map on a public bench makes attention something to be shared, not merely archived.
