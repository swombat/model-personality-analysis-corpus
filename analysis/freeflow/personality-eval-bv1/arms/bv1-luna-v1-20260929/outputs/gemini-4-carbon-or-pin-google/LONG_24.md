GENRE_FICTION — This is a sustained, carefully plotted literary story about a surveyor and a glassblower creating music from a town about to be submerged.

## Grounded reading
The voice is lyrical and tactile, repeatedly turning technical details—survey lines, cullet glass, water pressure—into ways of registering grief. Its central emotional movement is from Julian’s habit of recording places after people leave to his willingness to help make a communal farewell while the town is still alive in sound. Maren’s stubborn craft and Julian’s quiet change give the story its pathos; the reader is invited to see preservation not only as keeping a record, but as finding a way to let what is lost answer back.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds threatened places, inherited craft, and the difference between a map’s geometry and a town’s lived texture. Its recurring objects—rippled glass, survey instruments, the map, and the rising water—join measurement to memory. It favors collaboration and attentive witnessing over rescue: Barrowglass cannot be saved, but its destruction is made into a final performance.
