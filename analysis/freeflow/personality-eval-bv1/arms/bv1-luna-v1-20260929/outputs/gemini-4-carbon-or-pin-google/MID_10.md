## Grounded reading
The voice is warm, observant, and quietly elegiac, turning secondhand books into meeting places between strangers separated by time. Its pathos comes from small traces—a cramped monk’s complaint, an underlined sentence, a bookmark left behind—rather than grand declarations. The essay invites the reader to notice wear and handwritten marks as signs of attention and lived experience, and ends by making that invitation practical: leave a trace for someone who may someday need to find it.

## What the model chose to foreground
The model foregrounds marginalia, forgotten bookmarks, and the physical aging of books as evidence of human lives briefly touching. It contrasts the intimacy and imperfection of paper with the weightless cleanliness of digital reading, while resisting a purely nostalgic argument: the central claim is that wear records our contact with the world. The mood moves from hushed curiosity to tenderness, resolving in a hope that one reader’s marks might comfort another.
