Evaluator: withheld

Condition: VARY

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A literary short story built around an archivist who preserves sounds that ordinary history overlooks.

## Grounded reading
The voice is lush and attentive, turning technical and mundane sounds into carriers of tenderness and loss. Its pathos gathers around the small domestic moment on the tape: someone hears a loved one call and hurries to answer, unaware that the ordinary morning will become an unreachable past. The story invites the reader to listen more carefully to daily life, treating its unremarkable sounds as evidence of how people loved and lived.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds extinct sounds—ticket punches, dial-up modems, vanished forests and wetlands—and contrasts public historical spectacle with private domestic life. Its moral emphasis is that a chair scraping or a hurried, limping step may reveal more about human life than speeches and ceremonies. Rain, tape hiss, aging objects, and the effort of listening give the piece its elegiac mood.

## Evidence line
> Nobody asks for the sound of a person abandoning a cigarette to go answer someone they love in the next room.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the story is coherent and distinctive, and its repeated attention to fragile sensory detail and uncelebrated tenderness gives the chosen themes unusual definition.
