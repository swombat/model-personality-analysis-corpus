It makes room for very different kinds of people—the baker, nurse, new parent, and insomniac—without flattening their reasons for being awake. The passage’s pathos lies in the fragile relief of a world temporarily free of demands, and in knowing that the respite will end. Its closing reassurance offers the reader a place to return to, not a solution to daytime pressures.

It casts the pre-dawn hour as a reprieve from productivity and social obligation, while giving attention to labor, infant care, and anxious thought. The patient traffic lights and the recurring hallway or parenthesis imagery make this pause feel both impersonal and quietly welcoming.
