## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A literary short story built around the last day of a ferry service and its operator’s quiet transition out of a long-held role.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant, dryly funny, and tender without insisting on sentiment. Ruth notices how grief and care hide inside habits and practical gestures: Mr. Teague’s nickels, Cal’s awkward offers, and the careful work of teaching him the ferry line. The story invites the reader to attend to ordinary objects and routines as records of attachment, while letting the ending remain modest rather than triumphant.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story centers on a bridge replacing the ferry, and on the mixed feelings of someone whose useful work is ending even though the change is also welcome. It foregrounds small objects—the refrigerator, nickels, eggs, keys, and schedule—as carriers of memory and connection. Its moral emphasis is on the value of unshowy labor and the difficulty of asking directly for care; its resolution comes through shared practical work, not a grand declaration.

## Evidence line
> Still, it was one thing to want the bridge and another to be told how happy she must be about it.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the story is highly coherent and distinctive in how it lets recurring objects and understated humor carry its themes of grief, work, and connection.
