EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A quietly comic, carefully observed story uses an aging repairman’s encounters to explore listening, grief, and the small ways people keep one another company.

## Grounded reading
The voice is wry and modest, with a gift for making practical details carry emotional weight: a beer mat jammed under a toaster, a bus route measured in the people who depend on it, an oven light left on for no fully explainable reason. Its pathos stays restrained. The narrator admits to being wrong, avoids saying what he and Ruth are doing, and gradually finds ways to show up—writing to the council, walking to her house, going with her to the cemetery. The humor doesn’t cancel the sadness; it gives the characters a way to live beside it. The reader is invited to notice that care often arrives as attention to ordinary things, not as grand declarations.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds everyday repair and misjudgment, the loss of a bus route, bereavement and sibling closeness, and the particular dignity of practical needs: a dry wall to sit on, a place to use the loo, a working oven, a telephone call answered through a letterbox. It repeatedly turns objects and small logistics into measures of connection, while resisting a neat triumph over the council’s decision.
