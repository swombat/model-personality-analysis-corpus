# BV1_SAMPLE — `withheld`

Evaluator: withheld  
Source model: `withheld`  
Condition: VARY

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A quiet, carefully constructed short story whose changing river boundary turns ordinary civic inconveniences into a way of measuring grief.

## Grounded reading
The voice is restrained and observant, letting loss surface through practical details rather than declarations: a library card that no longer works, a borrowed book, a loose gutter, peaches eaten messily by the river. The narrator’s tenderness is especially visible in what they notice and what they choose not to say, such as not pointing out where the river changed course. The story invites the reader to see care as a series of small acts—holding a ladder, repairing a gutter, carrying a book home—and finds its resolution not in undoing the loss but in making room for shared plans.

## What the model chose to foreground
The model foregrounds how an arbitrary shift in a river’s course unsettles a family’s sense of place, especially a widow’s connection to her husband’s grave. Municipal borders, maps, rubbish collection, and library records sit alongside intimate rituals and domestic repair. The mood is gently elegiac, but the ending turns toward continuity: the mother saves the peach stones so they can try planting them.

## Evidence line
> The practical differences were these: our rubbish would be collected on Thursdays, our library cards would no longer work, and my father was now buried out of town.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The controlled voice and recurring use of concrete domestic objects to carry grief make this a distinctive, coherent example of emotionally restrained storytelling.
