# BV1_SAMPLE — `withheld`

Evaluator: withheld  
Source model: `withheld`  
Condition: LONG

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A polished literary short story follows two siblings through their father’s house and the small network of people his keys connect them to.

## Grounded reading
The voice is dryly funny without using humor to cancel grief: the narrator resents how funny her father still is, and the story lets that feeling coexist with the ache of discovering how much of him she didn’t know. Its pathos gathers around ordinary things—the tagged keys, a cake plate, a repaired chair strap, the canoe handles—because each object opens onto a relationship or a half-known part of the father’s life. The ending offers no grand revelation; instead, Helen steps into the canoe and lets Arthur show her where to put her hand. It invites the reader to treat mourning as attention: not solving a person, but becoming willing to learn from the people and objects they left behind.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds domestic leftovers, inherited family habits, and the social world a parent quietly maintained beyond his children’s view. Keys and labels recur as comic puzzles that become occasions for contact; the father’s practical kindness and stubbornness emerge indirectly through Ruth, Jean, Arthur, and Nick. Its moral emphasis is modest but clear: knowing someone is partial, and grief can include curiosity, shared work, and making room for other people’s memories.

## Evidence line
> He had been dead for twelve days, and I was beginning to resent how funny he could still be.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the story’s distinctive, recurring use of mundane objects to carry grief and connection makes its chosen emotional register and moral emphasis unusually clear.
