## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A quiet, carefully shaped family story uses ordinary objects and small acts of care to carry grief without explaining it away.

## Grounded reading
The voice is wry, observant, and emotionally restrained. It lets grief surface through the carpenter’s pencils, the colander, the dishwasher, and the sister’s peas rather than naming or interpreting every feeling. The humor—sour cream as “a breach of the arrangement,” the sister’s “TWO IF YOU LIKE”—keeps the tenderness from becoming solemn. The piece invites the reader to recognize how practical habits and unfinished conversations can become forms of intimacy, and how continuing those habits can be a way of staying connected.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds care expressed through useful things: a father sharpening pencils, a sister sorting possessions, and both siblings attending to the details of a dishwasher or a planting depth. It gives weight to bodily vulnerability and small losses—the father’s hand, the narrator’s cut thumb, the unspoken wish to tell him the dishwasher works—while ending not with a neat resolution but with the narrator going outside to tend the peas and confront the squirrel.

## Evidence line
> The pencils belonged to a separate correspondence.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the sustained attention to ordinary objects, understated humor, and practical forms of care makes this a distinctly shaped piece rather than a generic reflection on grief.
