## Grounded reading

Its pathos gathers around what the family has not asked or said: a father’s absence, a brother’s childhood memory, and the narrator’s reluctance to release their mother’s hand. The story invites the reader to notice how objects can preserve mismatched versions of a past, and how a conversation with a stranger can make room for a new one.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds ordinary objects—the potato masher, keys, chairs, and a zipper striking the dryer—as containers for memory and family connection. It pairs death and estrangement with dry humor, and gives weight to the moral choice to leave the father’s name on the label rather than erase or excuse him. Its ending turns toward patient companionship: the narrator sits with a stranger, asks about her husband, and lets the moment remain unresolved.
