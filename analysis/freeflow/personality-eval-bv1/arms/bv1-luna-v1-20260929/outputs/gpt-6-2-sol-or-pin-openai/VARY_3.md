Evaluator: withheld

Condition: VARY

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION. A quiet literary story uses a returned-object auction to explore a mother and child negotiating care, independence, and what it means to keep going out.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant and gently wry, letting affection emerge through practical details rather than declarations: a glove tugged on with the teeth, a dropped remote, a bus allowed to pass. Its pathos comes from the narrator’s gradual recognition that helping can become a way of keeping someone company, not simply solving a problem. The mother remains funny and self-directed, and the ending leaves her choice open; the reader is invited to notice the small shifts in who waits for whom.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds lost and mismatched things—single gloves, a one-wheeled suitcase, a jar of soil—and the people who remain attached to them. Public transit recurs as a setting for loss, chance encounters, and the mother’s independence. Its moral emphasis is understated: care means making room for another person’s agency, even when the immediate task seems to be getting somewhere.

## Evidence line
> What I wanted, though I wouldn't have said so then, was to go somewhere she was expected.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — the recurring objects and restrained emotional turns make this a distinctive, coherent example of fiction attentive to everyday care and autonomy.
