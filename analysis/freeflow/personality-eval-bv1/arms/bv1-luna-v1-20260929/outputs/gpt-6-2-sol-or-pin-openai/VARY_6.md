## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A restrained family story makes grief felt through ordinary objects, repeated visits, and small acts of care.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant and understated, attentive to what people ask for indirectly and what they cannot quite say. Its pathos comes from the mother’s grief and the narrator’s gradual recognition that showing up matters more than solving a practical problem. Rice, scissors, a walnut, a lost button, and the father’s coat carry affection without turning it into a speech. The reader is invited to notice how care can mean staying beside someone while they manage a task themselves.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds bereavement as a change in everyday routines rather than a single dramatic event: a mother calling about rice, a child coming over, and both finding new ways to share ordinary work. It gives particular weight to objects that preserve traces of the dead and to the delicate balance between helping and allowing someone to do things for herself. Its mood is tender, gently funny, and patient.

## Evidence line
> The second time, I understood that she wanted me to come over.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The recurring household objects and quiet reversals make this a distinctive, coherent example of emotionally restrained storytelling.
