## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW. A quiet, carefully shaped story turns ordinary acts of listening and letting go into an understated meditation on memory.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant and restrained, finding feeling in small physical details rather than explaining what to feel: a brass key, a heel-marked draining board, pages stopping mid-turn, a bicycle bell worn smooth but never heard. The pathos gathers across two losses—the closing archive and the siblings’ decision about their mother’s piano—without making either scene grand or sentimental. Repeatedly, the narrator encounters uncertainty about what was heard or remembered, then makes room for more than one version: “Put both.” The reader is invited to attend closely, and to see care as something done through listening, preserving, and waiting.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds sound and its imperfect transmission: recordings, a remembered tune, a disputed transcript, and the bell that remains silent. It pairs preservation with relinquishment, from the pool files and the piano to the narrator’s red sticker placed on an apple. Its moral emphasis is modest but clear: people and memories deserve attention, including when the record cannot settle on one definitive account.
