## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A carefully shaped literary short story built around a family relationship and an archive of everyday sounds.

## Grounded reading
The narrator’s voice is observant and gently comic, with feeling held in small details rather than explained outright: a drawer that sounds like it is about to disagree, a mother photographing her knees, a hand taking the bigger slice. The story’s pathos comes from the mismatch between what can be recorded and what matters in being together; the narrator fails to find the old drawer but recognizes the familiar sound of the cup. Its invitation is to listen closely to ordinary life without assuming that preservation gives us ownership or full understanding of it.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds domestic sounds, family memory, and the work of an archive, alongside the boundaries people set around what should be recorded or cataloged. Repeatedly, sounds matter because of the relationships around them: the mother’s cup, Nadia’s father peeling an orange, and the shoe repairer’s preferred distance from the pool recording. The ending gives priority to an unplanned, imperfect exchange over the narrator’s original search for a sound.

## Evidence line
> I knew the cup by the sound.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the story’s recurring attention to ordinary sounds and restrained tenderness is distinctive and coherently carried through its resolution.
