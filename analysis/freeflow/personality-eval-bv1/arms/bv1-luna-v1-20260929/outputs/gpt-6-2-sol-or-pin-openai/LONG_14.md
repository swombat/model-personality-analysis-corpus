## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION. This is a carefully shaped literary story, using a family inheritance and a room’s history to bring grief and sibling misunderstanding into focus.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant, restrained, and quietly funny, with emotional force arriving through practical details rather than declarations. The narrator notices chairs, keys, socks, oranges, and the awkward ways people try to speak; these objects make grief feel lived-in rather than grand. Its central pathos is the siblings’ uncertainty about what their mother wanted and what each of them owed the other. The story invites the reader to sit with truths that do not cancel one another out: the narrator wanted solitude, Daniel wanted reassurance, and neither can know exactly what their mother’s silences meant.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds rehearsal and speech, especially the gap between preparing to say something and actually saying it. It also dwells on inherited habits, unfinished grief, and small acts of care: peeling an orange, leaving a dollar under a chair leg, looking away to give a stranger privacy. Its moral emphasis is on allowing ambiguity to remain, and on the dignity of not forcing another person to perform their feelings.
