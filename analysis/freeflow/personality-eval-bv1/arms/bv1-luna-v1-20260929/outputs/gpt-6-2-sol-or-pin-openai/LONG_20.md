# BV1_SAMPLE — `withheld`

Evaluator: withheld  
Source model: `withheld`  
Condition: LONG

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A quietly comic, elegiac short story built around a municipal lost-property office and the people who pass through it.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant and restrained, finding feeling in practical details rather than announcing it. A door’s worn handle and pencilled height marks, a featureless stuffed rabbit, and a borrowed glove become small records of family life, care, and separation. The narrator’s own memories surface obliquely, especially in the remembered sound of their father’s thumbnail on a table. The story invites the reader to notice how people care for one another through modest acts—saving a card from the recycling, leaving a rabbit with a newsagent, opening a drawer after the office has closed—and to accept that not every loss needs to be explained or resolved.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds the ordinary infrastructure of loss: registers, depot calls, drawers of gloves, closing signs, and the gradual outsourcing of a civic service. Against that bureaucratic backdrop, it selects objects marked by use and affection, and gives particular weight to attention: remembering a detail, making room, or noticing when someone is about to leave something behind. Its mood balances dry humor with tenderness, while its ending leaves the narrator riding on rather than offering a grand resolution.

## Evidence line
> I thought I might ask Jo, and then remembered Jo hadn’t been looking.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the carefully recurring lost objects, understated humor, and repeated gestures of practical care make this a distinctive, coherent expression of the story’s values.
