## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A quietly crafted short story whose recurring clock and understated encounters carry its emotional weight.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant and gently wry, letting grief emerge through ordinary habits rather than declarations. Daniel’s coffees and Ruth’s municipal work make the story’s tenderness practical: people show care by waiting, noticing, and making room for a conversation. The clock’s small inaccuracy becomes a way to linger with the past without being trapped in it. The reader is invited to notice how everyday objects and routines hold private meanings, and how connection can continue in modest, imperfect forms.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds timekeeping, memory, and the tiny accommodations people make for one another: a slow clock, a bus that runs late, coffee with the wrong amount of sugar or milk, and a repair order changed from “REMOVE” to “ADJUSTED.” It gives grief a light touch, while suggesting that old rituals can make space for new companionship.

## Evidence line
> The clock at the end of Mercy Street was four minutes slow.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The repeated clock and coffee motifs, along with the story’s consistent preference for understated care over dramatic resolution, make its emotional and stylistic choices distinct.
