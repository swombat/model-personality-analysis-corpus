## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A finely observed family story uses the act of recording household sounds to explore memory, loss, and the difference between preserving a place and sharing it with someone.

## Grounded reading
The voice is affectionate but unsentimental: the narrator stands in doorways and tries to keep sounds, while the mother is brisk, funny, and increasingly at home with the move. Grief arrives through ordinary details—the bench scrape, a missing drawerful of teaspoons, the father’s tiny screwdriver—rather than through speeches. The story gently invites the reader to notice how memory is less like an archive than a conversation: the recordings preserve noises, but the mother’s stories and the sounds happening now matter more.

## What the model chose to foreground
The model chose domestic sounds and objects as vessels for family history: a sticky drawer, a bench, a table leveled with a matchbook, a kettle, and a snowplow heard over the phone. It foregrounds a daughter’s urge to rescue and preserve, set against a mother’s practical pleasure in leaving the house—and the eventual recognition that connection matters more than keeping its artifacts.

## Evidence line
> It turned out that the main thing I wanted, when I missed the house, was to tell my mother I missed it.

Medium: the sample’s coherent, recurring attention to ordinary objects as carriers of affection and loss makes its emotional and stylistic choices unusually clear.
