## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A restrained literary short story built around a ferry’s final crossing and a daughter’s uncertain memories of her father.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant and dryly funny, letting affection emerge through practical gestures rather than declarations: a jumper pulled onto a child, a fare dispute, a chair offered while they wait. Its pathos comes from what the narrator cannot know—whether her father saw her stranded as a child, and what he was willing to tell about his past. The closing image invites the reader to hold tenderness and uncertainty together: the father’s raised hand may echo that old signal, though the story refuses to explain it away.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds a vanishing local routine, the ferry and its small rituals, alongside changing infrastructure that lets people cross the river “without noticing it.” A spirit level, a cooker, a yellow jumper, and an old shelf carry family history without being made into grand symbols. The moral emphasis is on attention and imperfect care: people may miss one another, yet still make room, keep a shelf sturdy, or hold a hand up.

## Evidence line
> I’d always pictured my father looking straight at me and being unable to turn the boat.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The carefully repeated objects, understated humor, and unresolved final gesture make this a distinctive, coherent sample rather than a generic sentimental family story.
