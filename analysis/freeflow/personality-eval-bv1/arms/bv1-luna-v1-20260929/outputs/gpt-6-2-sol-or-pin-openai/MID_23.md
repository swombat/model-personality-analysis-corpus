Evaluator: withheld

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION. A quiet, character-centered story uses linked lost objects to build an understated emotional arc.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant and gently wry, attentive to the small awkwardness of claiming, returning, and letting go. Ada’s work gives the story a way to notice how people reveal attachment indirectly: a bit of cheese on a dish, a boy’s repeated checks of his trumpet case, the old keys she has kept. The emotional pathos stays restrained; the ending offers recognition rather than a dramatic reunion, as Ada remembers the smell of bread and lingers beside a place she once lived. It invites the reader to take seemingly trivial objects seriously without making their meaning grandiose.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds lost property, keys, food, and the uncertain line between keeping something and returning it. Its moral attention falls on ordinary acts of care and embarrassment, and on the way memories can persist in physical details even after a person has moved on. The recurring keys and the pear connect separate lives while allowing Ada to relinquish what she cannot claim.

## Evidence line
> She could have thrown that one out, but she liked the definiteness of it.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the story’s distinctive restraint and recurring attention to objects as carriers of memory and attachment make those choices especially clear within the sample.
