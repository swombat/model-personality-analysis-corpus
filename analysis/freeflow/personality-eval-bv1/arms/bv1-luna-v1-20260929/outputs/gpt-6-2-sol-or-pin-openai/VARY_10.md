## Grounded reading
The voice is restrained and attentive: it lets the father’s confusion emerge through small, concrete moments rather than explaining his decline. The “good key,” the pears, the toaster, and the workbench hold memories of his habits and usefulness, while the narrator’s practical acts—buying pears, clearing the house, returning to visit—show care without sentimentality. The ending invites the reader to notice how love can persist in gestures and objects even when shared memory is changing.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds a father’s memory loss and death, but gives equal weight to ordinary routines: fixing doors, turning bread, trading tea, measuring a workbench, eating a pear. Keys recur as symbols of access and usefulness, though the narrative never settles on a single explanation for the good key. Its moral emphasis is quiet: attention to small things can become a form of remembrance.
