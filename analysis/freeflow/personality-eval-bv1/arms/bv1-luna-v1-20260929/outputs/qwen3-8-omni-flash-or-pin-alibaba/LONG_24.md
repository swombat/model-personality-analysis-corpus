## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical, essayistic meditation whose recurring images and intimate first-person reflections give it a distinctive voice.

## Grounded reading
The voice is gentle and reflective, repeatedly turning ordinary experiences—dust in window light, dishes, a commute, a cup of tea—into occasions for moral attention. Its pathos comes through most clearly in its treatment of grief, loneliness, and the wish to be heard without being hurried toward resolution. The piece invites the reader to slow down and regard ordinary life not as a distraction from meaning but as where meaning is made. Its warmth is tempered by a civic and ethical insistence: attention should extend beyond the self, toward strangers, relationships, and shared responsibilities.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample centers attention as a practice of resistance to urgency and distraction, returning to windows, dust, water, trees, books, silence, and daily chores as images of patience and presence. It gives substantial space to listening, forgiveness, grief, joy, and the ordinary work of maintaining relationships. It also connects inward reflection to public life, framing attention and small acts of care as forms of citizenship and resistance to helplessness.

## Evidence line
> Recovering attention, then, becomes a political act as much as a spiritual one.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the sample has a coherent, recurring lyrical style and unusually sustained attention to ordinary objects as moral symbols.
