## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical, first-person day-in-the-life narrative uses ordinary routines and objects to explore avoidance, solitude, and quiet courage.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant and gently metaphorical, finding emotional weight in kettles, rain, books, and transit without turning the day into a dramatic crisis. Its pathos comes from the unopened letter: avoidance has become “architecture,” yet the narrator gradually recasts the envelope from accusation into invitation. The reader is invited to slow down and notice how ordinary surroundings can hold uncertainty, connection, and small acts of courage.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds morning rituals, urban weather and sounds, the parallel lives of strangers, and water as an image of shaping and connection. Its mood is reflective and mildly lonely, but not despairing; the narrator finds a kind of belonging in an “indifferent machine” and ends with the resolve simply to remain.

## Evidence line
> Sometimes avoidance feels like wisdom until it becomes architecture: rooms build around a single decision, hallways lengthen, and the door we refused opens inward onto years.
