## Grounded reading
The voice is solemn and tender, turning the lighthouse keeper’s isolation into a form of care: he watches, rescues, listens, and preserves the names of the dead. Its pathos gathers around Elian’s guilt and the keeper’s loss of his son, then softens into a legacy carried through the compass, the maps, and the final lamp-lighting. The story invites the reader to see witness and remembrance as meaningful acts, even in the face of a cruel, indifferent sea.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds a lighthouse, shipwreck, storm, and rescue, but its lasting focus is on loneliness, survivor’s guilt, and the duty to remember. Light recurs as warning, witness, and inheritance; names, journals, maps, and the returned compass make memory tangible. The moral claim is explicit: grief can be carried without being allowed to rule, and a life saved can continue to matter through attention and care.
