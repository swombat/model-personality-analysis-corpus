## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION. A lyrical, allegorical story builds around a lighthouse keeper’s grief and a letter that unsettles his sense of the past.

## Grounded reading
The voice is gentle and solemn, drawn to soft images of light, weather, food, and small keepsakes. Its pathos comes from Eli’s uncertain memories and the possibility that he was the child lost in the shipwreck; the small shoe makes that uncertainty intimate rather than abstract. The story invites the reader to sit with grief without demanding a clean explanation, then offers relief when the daughter is found alive. Its repeated quiet similes and reflective maxims give the ending a parable-like warmth.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds tending the light as a form of care, the persistence of grief and memory, and the power of ordinary comforts—warm bread, soup, repaired nets—to anchor a person. It also chooses a mystery about lost family and uncertain identity, resolving it not through proof of Eli’s past but through news of the living daughter.

## Evidence line
> Eli said yes because someone must always tend the light.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the sample’s recurring light, sea, and keepsake imagery gives its meditation on grief and care a coherent, distinctive shape, though its aphoristic style and redemptive resolution are familiar features of parable-like fiction.
