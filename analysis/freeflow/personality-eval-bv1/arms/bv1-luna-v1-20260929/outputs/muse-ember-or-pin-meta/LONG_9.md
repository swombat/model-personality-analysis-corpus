## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A polished essay, but its first-person scenes, recurring images, and conversational voice make it more personally textured than a generic public-intellectual argument.

## Grounded reading
The voice is warm, observant, and gently persuasive, with self-deprecating humor in the opening account of getting lost and a more earnest note when it turns to grief, solitude, and the design of cities. Its central pathos is a longing to recover attention from efficiency: the bakery, blue doors, wet steps, and changing weather become evidence that a familiar place can still surprise us. The reader is invited not just to agree with an argument, but to try a small act of wandering and let the world set the pace.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds walking as a way of knowing a place and oneself, and getting lost as a potentially generative rather than shameful experience. It repeatedly returns to local details—the cat, bakery, footpaths, shopfronts, weather—and contrasts them with cars, deadlines, and frictionless environments. It also makes room for walking as companionship and solitude, while acknowledging that safety and the freedom to wander are not equally available to everyone.
