## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW. A reflective first-person meditation gives the idea of attention a recurring set of sensory scenes and a personal history.

## Grounded reading
The voice is calm and gently instructive, but it makes its case through small, tactile moments rather than grand claims: a chipped mug, soap rainbows, an ant on the porch, a friend’s delayed laugh. The childhood memory of waiting gives the essay’s central idea a quiet pathos: attention can turn imposed emptiness into an inhabited world. The reader is invited to slow down, notice restlessness without immediately obeying it, and offer other people the respect of unhurried listening.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds attention as a practice amid distraction, anxiety, and pressure to make time productive. Ordinary objects and activities—chores, walking, reading, waiting—become occasions for noticing; listening to another person extends that noticing into an ethic of respect and accompaniment. Its mood is tender and reassuring, with a closing image of attention as imperfect garden-tending rather than a trait one either possesses or lacks.
