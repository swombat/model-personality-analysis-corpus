## Grounded reading
The voice is precise and tender, finding feeling in the small procedures and objects of office life: striped light, a thermos, an unofficial notebook, a swatch held to a window. Walter’s color-blindness is not treated as a deficit to overcome; it becomes part of his way of attending to what colors mean to people and the memories they carry. The story invites the reader to regard naming as a form of care and preservation. Its closing gesture gives that practice to Priya without making her understanding feel like a grand revelation.

## What the model chose to foreground
Under the open prompt, the model chose to center names, color, memory, and ordinary acts of keeping. The contrast between official two-word labels and Walter’s private, intimate descriptions carries the story’s moral claim: exact classification can record a thing, while attention can preserve what it means. The recurring domestic details—tea, a kettle, Elsie’s thermos—give the melancholy a quiet, lived-in warmth.
