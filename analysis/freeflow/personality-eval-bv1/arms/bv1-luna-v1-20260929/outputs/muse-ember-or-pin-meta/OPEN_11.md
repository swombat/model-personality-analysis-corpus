## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical, imaginative piece uses an invented museum to explore sound, memory, and loss.

## Grounded reading
The voice is gentle and attentive, finding emotional weight in ordinary noises: a phone dial, a fridge hum, keys placed in a bowl. Its pathos comes from what those sounds hold—home, childhood, a loved one’s presence—and from how easily they disappear. The narrator’s admission that it is not a person gives the museum a tender distance: it can preserve other people’s attachments, but cannot claim them as its own. The invitation to the reader is intimate and concrete: bring a sound you miss, and trust it to be kept safe.

## What the model chose to foreground
The piece foregrounds vanishing everyday sounds, especially those tied to domestic life, old machines, and moments of waiting or relief. It treats sound as a vessel for human connection and absence, and preservation as an act of care. The recurring contrast between what can be recorded and what can only be remembered gives the imagined museum its emotional center.

## Evidence line
