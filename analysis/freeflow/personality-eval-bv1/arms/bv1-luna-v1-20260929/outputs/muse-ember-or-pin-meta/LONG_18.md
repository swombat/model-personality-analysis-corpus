GENERIC_ESSAY — A polished, thesis-driven essay that uses a lyrical first-person frame to argue that aimless walking restores attention, scale, and a sense of lived time.

## Grounded reading
The voice is reflective and assured, inviting the reader not simply to take a walk but to experience unhurried attention as a modest form of resistance. Its pathos comes from the contrast between bodily, local experience and a hurried world of screens, optimization, and sealed transit. The recurring pavement, remembered routes, weather, and chance encounters make the argument concrete; the closing returns to the small ritual of twenty unplanned minutes rather than promising a grand transformation.

## What the model chose to foreground
The model foregrounds purposeless walking as a way to notice place, learn humility, recover memory, think, and share attention with others. It favors ordinary sensory details—wet pavement, bakery smells, puddles, a dog in a doorway—alongside literary and historical references. Its central moral claim is that time need not be mined for productivity: walking can be valuable precisely because it is not optimized.
