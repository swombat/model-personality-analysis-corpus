## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical personal essay that turns ordinary acts of noticing into a practice of care.

## Grounded reading
The voice is patient and intimate, building its reflection through tactile, modest particulars: a chipped mug, dust in library light, rain on different surfaces, a grandmother’s letter. Its pathos comes less from dramatic loss than from the tenderness of recognizing how affection and memory inhabit everyday things. The narrator admits distraction and imperfection rather than presenting attention as a perfected virtue; the invitation is to slow down, return, and let the familiar become more fully visible.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample repeatedly returns to attention as generosity, reverence, and embodied care. Kitchens, libraries, trains, weather, gardens, music, letters, hands, photographs, silence, and seasons become occasions to notice presence and accept limits. Its moral emphasis is that ordinary life already has depth, and that attention reveals rather than decorates it; the grandmother’s habits and letter give this idea an intergenerational tenderness.

## Evidence line
> Attention does not add to life from the outside, like a decoration.
