## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A lyrical, gently magical short story built around an invented lighthouse and its keeper.

## Grounded reading
The voice is tender and wistful, finding wonder in small, ordinary losses: a glove, a remembered tune, the exact weight of a parent’s hand. Its central sorrow is not dramatic estrangement but the quieter ache of distance and change; Wren still speaks with her brother, yet misses the particular laughter that belonged to their childhood. The story invites the reader to treat memory with care and to believe that what seems gone may still be held somewhere, waiting to be found.

## What the model chose to foreground
The model foregrounds lost objects and fleeting sensations as emotionally substantial, then turns that idea into a metaphor of patient care: Wren labels, preserves, and illuminates what others have misplaced. The story’s moral emphasis is gentle rather than corrective; the box marked “NOT YET” gives regret a place to wait instead of demanding that it be resolved. Its recurring images of light, water, jars, and home make recovery feel like an act of welcome.

## Evidence line
> She winds the light every night at dusk, not to warn anyone away from the rocks, but to make the Lost findable.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the story is coherent and stylistically distinctive, with recurring attention to memory, tenderness, and the possibility of return.
