## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical personal essay that uses a narrated walk to explore uncertainty, attention, and the value of wandering.

## Grounded reading
The voice is reflective and gently self-deprecating, turning ordinary details—a cracked sidewalk, burned toast, wet shoes—into openings for memory and thought. Its pathos lies in the pressure to have a plan and the relief of temporarily stepping outside that pressure. The essay invites the reader to try noticing familiar places without a destination, not as self-improvement but as a way to encounter life with less instruction and more curiosity.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds aimlessness as a form of attention, and contrasts childhood wandering and urban exploration with phones, efficiency, and route-following. It repeatedly returns to small, tactile details—cornfields, a brass flood plaque, a radio, a garden—as evidence that the familiar can become newly vivid. Its moral claim is modest but clear: not every hour needs a plot or a purpose to matter.

## Evidence line
> For an hour, my life had no plot, and I had never felt more like its author.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
