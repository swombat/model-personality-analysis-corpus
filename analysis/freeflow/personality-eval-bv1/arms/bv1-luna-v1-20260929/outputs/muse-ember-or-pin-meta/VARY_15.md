## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW. A lyrical first-person meditation turns a thousand-word limit into a story about attention, memory, and the ethics of choosing what to say.

## Grounded reading
The voice is intimate, reflective, and gently melancholic, with its images kept close to ordinary life: coffee cooling, rain on a window, a mother humming, a library book returned unread. Its pathos comes from the sense that time and language are finite, and from the speaker’s reluctance to spend words carelessly on other people or on an unsent letter. The piece invites the reader to notice how naming can preserve an experience without possessing it, and ends by easing its initial fear of waste into quiet acceptance.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds words as both scarce currency and a form of attention, using that conceit to connect small sensory moments with love, memory, and restraint. It gives particular weight to the mother’s remembered presence, the neighbor the speaker hesitates to describe, and the letter left unmailed. The chosen mood is tender and wistful rather than despairing; its moral emphasis is that language can either diminish another person or hold a moment more fully.

## Evidence line
> Words are lighter and heavier at once.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — the sustained metaphor, recurring attention to ordinary sensory details, and repeated preference for tenderness and restraint make this a distinctive sample.
