## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical first-person walk becomes a reflective argument for aimlessness, with recurring sensory detail and a distinctive, gently humorous voice.

## Grounded reading
The voice is companionable and observant, finding significance in ordinary things without making them grand: wet pavements, a bruised apple, a fisherman’s stories, a bird glimpsed for an instant. Its pathos comes from the contrast between “busy or tired” life and the quiet happiness of having nowhere to be. The narrator admits to missed obligations and imperfect thoughts, which keeps the reflection from sounding like a sermon. The invitation is modest: put the phone away for a while, tolerate uncertainty and boredom, and let attention return to the world at walking pace.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds unoptimized time, walking without a destination, and the way phones and schedules turn movement into efficient transit. It repeatedly attends to small encounters and sensory particulars—rain, trees, river water, birds, a stranger’s local knowledge—and treats these as forms of attention worth protecting. Its moral claim is proportionate rather than anti-modern: ordinary obligations matter, but so does time that accomplishes nothing measurable.

## Evidence line
> Wandering is about consenting to it.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the sample sustains a coherent, distinctive voice and returns throughout to sensory attention, gentle humor, and a defense of unhurried time.
