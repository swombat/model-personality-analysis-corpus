## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW. A lyrical first-person meditation turns waiting rooms and transit spaces into a lens on memory, grief, and patience.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant and gently self-questioning, finding emotional weight in ordinary details: carpet patterns, vending-machine hum, plastic chairs, and a borrowed coat. Its pathos gathers around the father’s death and the experience of sitting with a question that has no answer coming. The piece invites the reader to treat waiting not as wasted time but as a form of attention—without pretending that grief or delay is cheerful or easily redeemed.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample repeatedly returns to thresholds—stations, terminals, depots, and hospital waiting rooms—and to the strangers briefly sharing them. It emphasizes suspended time, small sensory details that outlast major events in memory, and the quiet tenderness that can emerge when ordinary expectations of productivity fall away. The moral claim is modest but clear: arrival does not justify the hours before it; those hours are part of life in their own right.

## Evidence line
> I once spent six hours in the overnight terminal in Chicago because of snow, and I can still remember the exact pattern of the carpet, the hum of the vending machine, the way a man's shoes squeaked every time he paced past the help desk.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the sample is coherent and distinctive, with recurring images and an unusually revealing turn from travel delays to bereavement.
