## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical first-person reflection turns a personal habit into an argument for wandering and attentive, unoptimized time.

## Grounded reading
The voice is intimate, wry, and gently persuasive: it admits the inconvenience of being late, then finds dignity and pleasure in what the detour makes possible. The pathos is quiet rather than grand, grounded in childhood avoidance, the pressure to optimize adult life, and the fear of moving through places without really experiencing them. The reader is invited to treat wandering not as failure but as a practice of attention—one that leaves memory, beauty, and a richer sense of having been somewhere.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds the long way through city streets: parks, canals, shop windows, pigeons, a flooded underpass, damp plums, a bicycle chain, and repeated piano bars. It sets these acts of noticing against schedules, efficiency, and the demand to optimize, making purposelessness a skill worth protecting. The recurring moral claim is that a slower route may be less efficient in minutes but more substantial in memory and lived experience.

## Evidence line
> It is a minor rebellion, mostly harmless, and it has taught me more about the cities I live in than any map ever has.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The essay sustains a distinctive, coherent motif through specific urban details and a consistent defense of wandering, though its argument resolves in a polished, neatly stated conclusion.
