## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical fantasy about preserving lost sounds becomes a gentle reflection on memory, grief, and what may be better left unrecovered.

## Grounded reading
The voice is tender and quietly elegiac, but it makes room for ordinary, even comic details: toast popping up, a bicycle chain slipping, a kettle whistling. Those sounds give grief a domestic scale rather than turning it into grand tragedy. Wren’s sealed jar of her childhood voice calling for her mother holds the story’s most private ache; the piece treats not listening as a form of care, not failure. Its closing invitation is to trust that memory can live within us without being perfectly retrieved.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds jars, sound, the sea, and a lighthouse that keeps working while its keeper tends to something less visible. It dwells on small human noises as vessels for connection and loss, and suggests that remembering does not always require reopening what hurts. The recurring contrast between a lonely island and sounds that briefly fill it gives the piece its gentle, haunted mood.

## Evidence line
> The sea is very good at collecting human noise and very bad at keeping it.
