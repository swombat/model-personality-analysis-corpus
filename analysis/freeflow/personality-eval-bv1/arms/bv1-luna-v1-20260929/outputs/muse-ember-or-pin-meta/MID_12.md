## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A sensory, first-person meditation uses breadmaking to connect family memory, history, and the value of patience.

## Grounded reading
The voice is warm, reflective, and attentive to ordinary physical details: the cracking crust, the chipped blue crock, cool flour-dusted hands. Its pathos comes from the grandmother’s remembered advice and the effort to recover her loaf, which gradually becomes a meditation on care and inheritance. The piece invites the reader to slow down and see bread not merely as food, but as shared labor and continuity across generations.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds bread as nourishment and social bond, the grandmother’s practical wisdom, and the persistence of tradition through hardship. It repeatedly contrasts slow, attentive processes with modern demands for speed, and treats patience, restraint, and not wasting what one has as moral lessons emerging from breadmaking.

## Evidence line
> Bread is not just food; it is a social contract made edible.
