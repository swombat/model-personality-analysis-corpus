## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical reflection that develops a personal ethic of attention through ordinary scenes and sensory details.

## Grounded reading
The voice is gentle and self-correcting: it values the “second look” not as a way to uncover grand secrets, but as a courtesy that resists the laziness of first impressions. Its pathos is modest—wanting to be “a little slower” and more available, without turning attentiveness into solemnity or demanding an epiphany. The reader is invited to notice alongside the speaker: a worn sweater, rain on roofs, a pause in conversation. The piece makes presence feel like a reasonable beginning rather than a cure for everything.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds everyday objects and small acts of attention: a tree growing around a fence, a stranger shielding a paper bag, an ant negotiating a breadcrumb, steam from a drain. It repeatedly contrasts efficiency with attentiveness, and treats looking closely as courtesy rather than extraction. Its chosen mood is unhurried and appreciative, tempered by the claim that noticing does not make every day good.

## Evidence line
> Looking twice is not a way of forcing meaning out of things.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the recurring attention-to-ordinary-detail motif and consistent, distinctive voice make this a clear expressive choice within the sample.
