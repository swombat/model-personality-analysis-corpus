# BV1_SAMPLE — `withheld`

Evaluator: withheld  
Source model: `withheld`  
Condition: VARY

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A compact, gently magical story uses a sound-repair shop to frame a widower’s first movement back toward ordinary life.

## Grounded reading
The voice is tender and restrained, letting grief appear through concrete absences—the missing second cup, the cold kitchen, the silence in the biscuit tin—rather than explaining it at length. Its central pathos is that Mara cannot restore Alice or the old kitchen, but can help Harold let sound back in. The story invites the reader to see small, imperfect acts—a bad tune, a laugh, tea made together—as meaningful without pretending they undo loss.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds grief, domestic ritual, memory, and the limits of repair. Sound is both its imaginative premise and its emotional language: silence is treated as something that can occupy a room, while laughter and kettle noise make space for life to resume. The moral emphasis is modest and humane: care does not require fixing what cannot be restored; sometimes it means interrupting the silence and staying with someone.

## Evidence line
> The laugh seemed to hurt him.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The story’s recurring sound imagery and carefully understated resolution make its humane attention to grief a distinctive, coherent choice.
