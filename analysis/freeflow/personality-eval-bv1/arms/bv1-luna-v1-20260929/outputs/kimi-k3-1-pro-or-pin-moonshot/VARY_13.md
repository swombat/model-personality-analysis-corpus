## Grounded reading
The voice is gentle, observant, and lightly wry, finding emotional weight in concrete details: jars of bent nails, a crooked curtain, a cup left on the draining board. Its grief is direct but controlled; the narrator explicitly resists turning the dead into a supernatural answer, yet still chooses to get up when the wind might be knocking. The reader is invited to sit with the gap between what we wish a memory could give us and what it actually gives.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample centers on a blue door as an image of childhood wonder, delayed return, and the desire for one more impossible thing from a lost loved one. It foregrounds ordinary acts of care—especially the grandfather’s ability to sit beside unhappiness without demanding an explanation—and treats grief as something that remains, even as speaking to the dead can still matter. The ending preserves uncertainty rather than converting it into proof or comfort.
