## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A literary fable uses a small domestic god and restrained magic to frame a story about grief and moving forward.

## Grounded reading
The voice is gently comic without making light of loss: the god’s “profound administrative disappointment,” tiny favors, and complaints about tea sit beside the narrator’s preserved cup, unfinished book, and habit of waiting in doorways. The story’s pathos comes from how ordinary objects hold grief in place, while the god’s limited powers keep the resolution from becoming a miraculous cure. It invites the reader to see recovery not as a triumph, but as returning to small acts—washing a cup, answering the phone, going for a walk—and letting the future become open again.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds thresholds and waiting: doorways, dawn, the pause before an answer, and the kettle’s brief interval before boiling. It connects those moments to contemporary distraction (“Now you scroll”), domestic rituals, and the narrator’s grief for Mara. Its moral emphasis is on respecting another person’s will and finding help rather than a magical undoing of loss. The movement from kettle to sea gives the story’s recurring images of steam, weather, and water a quiet sense of release.
