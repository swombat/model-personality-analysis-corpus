## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A polished, emotionally shaped short story built around a supernatural encounter in an ordinary home.

## Grounded reading
The voice is wry and tactile, letting grief arrive through radiator knocks, a leaking kettle, old tools, and family sayings rather than through extended confession. Its pathos rests on regret over a missed chance to visit and the comfort of being loved without having to prove usefulness. The story invites the reader to accept ambiguity—the grandfather may be speaking through the pipes or memory may be giving them meaning—while still treating the connection as emotionally real.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds bereavement, a marriage ending, inherited domestic space, and the small acts of repair that keep family ties alive. Its recurring objects—the radiator, toolbox, hair grip, kettle, bread, and cups—make care practical and imperfect. The moral claim is gently stated: some broken things call for repair, while others call for company.

## Evidence line
> “Some things you mend,” he said at last.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the coherent voice, recurring repair imagery, and understated resolution make this a distinctive expressive choice rather than a generic grief vignette.
