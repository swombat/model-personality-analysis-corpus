## Grounded reading
The voice is measured and reassuring, but not simply permissive: it makes room for incompletion while insisting that obligations to others still matter. Its pathos comes from ordinary objects—a half-read book, a missing rung, a draft—and from the pressure of feeling that unfinished work is a verdict on the self. The recurring carpenter’s shop gives the essay a steady image for revision, inheritance, and release. It invites the reader to distinguish what deserves another step from what can be let go, without treating either choice as failure.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay foregrounds unfinishedness as a condition of making, learning, living, and relating to other people. It repeatedly returns to craft and repair, then extends the idea through landscapes, cities, knowledge, relationships, grief, and daily attention. Its central moral qualification is that incompletion can be humane or evasive: an imperfect sketch may preserve possibility, while an unfinished promise can transfer burdens to someone else.
