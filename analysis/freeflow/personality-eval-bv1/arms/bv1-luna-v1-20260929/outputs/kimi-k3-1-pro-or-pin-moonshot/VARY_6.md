# BV1_SAMPLE — `withheld`

Evaluator: withheld
Source model: `withheld`
Condition: VARY

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A polished literary fable uses an impossible lighthouse and its keeper to tell a quiet story about care, loneliness, and community.

## Grounded reading
The voice is whimsical without making light of its loneliness: the lighthouse stands absurdly far from the sea, while Mina’s steady routine and the strangers who arrive after midnight give the absurdity a tender purpose. The turning point is not the lamp’s repair but the neighbors’ response to its failure, which lets Mina see that her solitary work has been witnessed. The closing answer, “At somebody’s window,” offers the reader an intimate, modest image of where care reaches—not a grand rescue, but a light arriving somewhere in the dark.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds obsolete or seemingly purposeless work, small rituals, and ordinary objects—a second cup, a drawer of found things, a thermos, improvised lamps—as forms of hospitality and connection. Its moral emphasis is that persistence can sustain a place for others, and that care may circulate back toward the person who has quietly offered it.

## Evidence line
> It was astonishing, she thought, how long a thing could survive if everyone assumed someone else had decided it should.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the story is coherent and distinctive, and its recurring light-and-hospitality imagery gives its humane moral emphasis unusual clarity.
