# BV1_SAMPLE — `withheld`

Evaluator: withheld
Source model: `withheld`
Condition: VARY

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A quietly whimsical village fable uses letters from the future to explore uncertainty and ordinary human care.

## Grounded reading
The voice is warm, gently comic, and reflective, with its strongest feeling arriving in modest turns rather than grand declarations. The narrator’s unrequited love, the town’s anxious dependence on forecasts, and its eventual practical response to a real disaster give the story emotional weight without making it melodramatic. The invitation is to accept uncertainty as part of living—and to meet it together, with soup, brooms, and room for children to be wrong.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds brief warnings, small-town rituals, seedlings, the post box, and the oaks planted for shade. Its moral emphasis falls on the difference between being uninformed and being helpless: forecasts can prevent harm, but they cannot answer every human question or replace people’s capacity to respond. The final pleasure in hearing children argue about the future makes uncertainty feel not only tolerable but alive.

## Evidence line
> We were not helpless, as we had feared. We were only uninformed, which is a different thing.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The recurring contrast between prediction and lived response gives the piece a coherent, distinctive moral center, while its familiar fable form makes the evidence less individually revealing.
