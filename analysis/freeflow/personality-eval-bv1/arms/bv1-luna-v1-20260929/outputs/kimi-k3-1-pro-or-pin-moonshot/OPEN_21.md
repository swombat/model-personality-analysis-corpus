## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A carefully shaped, lyrical miniature uses an imagined museum to explore regret, possibility, and the small acts that can still be chosen.

## Grounded reading
The voice is tender but wary of easy consolation. Its humor—the “wet Tuesday,” “cowardice with good tailoring”—keeps the museum from turning regret into a shrine. The piece invites readers to recognize their unrealized selves without idealizing them, then asks whether anything can be carried back into the present. Its final hope is deliberately modest: not a promised happy ending, but someone walking an unfamiliar street or making an almost “done.”

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample centers on missed journeys, unsaid words, abandoned possible selves, and the objects that stand in for them: a softened train ticket, unused walking shoes, a sentence in a glass case, an empty plinth. It pairs compassion for hesitation with a moral insistence that possibility should not become an excuse for inaction; the curator distinguishes mercies from tragedies and cowardice, then offers a pencil or an open door rather than absolution.

## Evidence line
> There should be a museum for the lives we nearly lived.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The sample’s recurring objects, dry humor, and restrained turn toward action make its treatment of regret notably coherent and distinctive.
