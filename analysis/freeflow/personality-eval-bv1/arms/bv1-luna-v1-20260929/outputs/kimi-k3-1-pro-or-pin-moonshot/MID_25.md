## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical first-person meditation that develops the window as a changing boundary between shelter, attention, and contact with the world.

## Grounded reading
The voice is contemplative and attentive to ordinary particulars: a cat crossing a sill, laundry in the wind, the smells and sounds of a street. Its pathos lies in wanting both safety and connection, while recognizing that looking at other lives is not the same as knowing them. The reader is invited to notice familiar thresholds more closely, and ultimately to move from observation toward participation.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample chooses the window as its central object and repeatedly treats it as a boundary that can edit, admit, conceal, or return attention to the world. It weighs the comforts of shelter against the costs of sealed-off living, including the way screens offer distant access without sensory contact. Its moral emphasis is gentle but clear: other people remain partly unknowable, and attention should not become a substitute for entering life.

## Evidence line
> A window is a wall that has changed its mind.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the sample is coherent and stylistically distinctive, with recurring attention to sensory detail, partial knowledge, and the tension between shelter and openness.
