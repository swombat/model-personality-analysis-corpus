## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A literary short story built around a curator’s changing understanding of sound, silence, and what an archive should preserve.

## Grounded reading
The voice is tender and contemplative, finding emotional weight in ordinary noises without making them grand. Its pathos comes from loss and the wish to leave traces, then turns toward connection: the boy’s recording reveals a community becoming audible to itself during a blackout. The story invites the reader to listen more patiently to the incidental sounds and people usually edited out.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story centers obsolete and everyday sounds—typewriters, bottles, bus doors, chairs, footsteps—and the memories they carry. It foregrounds the ethics of preservation: clean recordings can erase the rooms, recordists, and other people that give a sound meaning. Its resolution favors attention and shared presence over nostalgia or monument-making, ending with an ordinary recording offered to future listeners.

## Evidence line
> At best, she was making it harder for them to vanish without witnesses.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The story is coherent and unusually consistent in its attention to ordinary sounds, memory, loss, and the value of listening.
