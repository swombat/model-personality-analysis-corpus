# BV1_SAMPLE — `withheld`

Evaluator: withheld  
Source model: `withheld`  
Condition: LONG

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION. A polished literary short story uses a museum of unfinished objects to frame grief, avoidance, and the possibility of continuing without erasing what came before.

## Grounded reading
The voice is gentle, observant, and quietly wry: creaking floors, over-optimistic pencils, shortbread, and a chair that “creaked, then held” keep the emotional register grounded in ordinary things. Its central pathos is Mara’s attempt to preserve a moment with her dead father by leaving their chair unfinished; the story lets her recognize this without treating grief as a problem that can simply be solved. Abel’s questions are often pointed, but the narrative also gives him limits and unfinished work of his own. The reader is invited to see incompletion less as failure than as something that can carry grief, avoidance, rest, or a new beginning—and to notice which it is in a particular case.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds unfinished objects and their makers’ reasons for stopping, especially a jointly made chair that holds Mara’s memories of her father. It repeatedly links making and finishing to grief, inheritance, and the difference between preserving the past and participating in the present. Its moral emphasis is modest rather than triumphant: continuing does not undo loss, and completion does not make an object safe from time.

## Evidence line
> Completion had not made the chair safe from time.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the story’s recurring objects, restrained humor, and careful resolution make its interest in unfinished work and ordinary acts of continuation unusually coherent within the sample.
