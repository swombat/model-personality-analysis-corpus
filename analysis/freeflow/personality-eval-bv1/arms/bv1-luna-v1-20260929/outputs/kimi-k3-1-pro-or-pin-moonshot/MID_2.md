## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A lyrical short story uses a recurring notebook-and-paper-bird motif to shape a fable about grief and change.

## Grounded reading
The voice is gentle, observant, and deliberately lyrical, finding emotional weight in small urban details: a bakery’s cinnamon, a guard’s dance steps, a pale rectangle where a phone booth stood. Mara’s grief first turns attention into an attempt to preserve things against loss; the story’s resolution invites her—and the reader—to treat noticing as participation rather than control. Its tenderness comes with a quiet ache: people and places disappear, but care can circulate through what others notice and add.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds ordinary, vulnerable things—doors, pigeons, plants, paper boats, a broken umbrella—and the act of attending to them. It pairs grief with the urge to document and contrasts preservation with openness, culminating in the choice to release the paper birds and continue the shared atlas. The moral emphasis is that love does not require keeping the world still.

## Evidence line
> Its pages were no longer evidence against loss.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The sustained return to noticing, repair, and release makes the story’s moral emphasis distinctive and coherent, while its polished fable structure gives less evidence of a highly individual voice.
