# BV1_SAMPLE — `withheld`

Evaluator: withheld  
Source model: `withheld`  
Condition: LONG

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A carefully structured literary story uses the museum of “almosts” to explore regret, family, and the lives that unfold after plans are abandoned.

## Grounded reading
The voice is patient and gently wry, attentive to small objects and the stories people attach to them: a key, a ticket, a teaspoon, two cups. Its pathos comes from losses that are neither erased nor made into secret blessings—Mara’s abandoned art-school place matters, as do the years of caregiving and the life she built instead. The story invites readers to look beyond the clean, painful outline of what did not happen and notice the mixed, imperfect substance of what did. Its resolution is modest: not transformation, but a Thursday visit, a sketchbook, and a door left slightly open.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds regret, caregiving, sibling estrangement, and the way obligation and fear can coexist with love. It repeatedly gives emotional weight to ordinary objects and acts of attention: cataloguing, making soup, drawing a cup, offering tea. Its moral emphasis is that honoring a missed possibility need not diminish the life that followed; the story resists both blaming Mara for staying and pretending that every loss was secretly a gift.

## Evidence line
> But beside the lives that had not happened, she made room for evidence of the ones that had: inconsistent, unphotogenic, often absurd, and rarely visible from the angle at which regret preferred to look.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The story is coherent and distinctive, with recurring objects and a sustained, humane moral emphasis on regret without easy consolation.
