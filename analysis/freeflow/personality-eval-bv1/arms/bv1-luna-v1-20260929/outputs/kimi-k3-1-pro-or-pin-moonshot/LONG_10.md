## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A literary short story uses a planning dispute to dramatize the gap between measurable impacts and the ordinary pleasures that are harder to count.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant, gently wry, and attentive to material particulars: a paint tin of rosemary, a chess clock, a sunlit patch on a table. Its pathos comes from the narrator’s growing recognition that accurate models can still leave out what matters to people, without making measurement or new housing the villain. Ada’s insistence that her loss be taken seriously—and her refusal to become a sentimental emblem—keeps the story’s tenderness unsugared. The reader is invited to hold competing needs together and notice small, transient things without pretending that noticing them resolves the conflict.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds sunlight as both a measurable resource and an unoptimized pleasure, alongside housing need, public decision-making, and the limits of technical accuracy. Its moral emphasis falls on asking what a loss is measured against and on making room for lives that a calculation can represent only incompletely. The repeated movement between figures and lived details, ending with the narrator pausing to watch a brief reflection, makes attention itself a chosen value.

## Evidence line
> The useful work was less romantic: finding which losses could be reduced, which could be moved, and which somebody had quietly assumed did not count.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the story’s recurring contrast between accurate figures and uncounted human meaning is distinctive and carried coherently from the opening complaint through the closing image.
