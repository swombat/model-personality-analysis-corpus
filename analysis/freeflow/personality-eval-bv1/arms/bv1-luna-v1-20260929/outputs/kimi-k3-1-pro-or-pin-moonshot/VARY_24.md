## Grounded reading
The voice is restrained, gently wry, and attentive to small physical details: rain on eyelashes, a loose thread, a yellow duster left behind. Its pathos comes from the gap between imagined encounters and actual life, especially the siblings’ long estrangement. The story invites the reader to recognize that “almost” can become a refuge from the risks of acting, then offers no grand reconciliation—only a meal, an umbrella, and one evening spent together.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds unchosen lives through objects that preserve a moment just before action: an unpunched ticket, an unanswered telephone, and Mara’s card marked “ALMOST KNOCKED.” It joins that motif to grief, family distance, and the quiet cost of staying in one place. Its moral turn is modest but clear: Mr. Iven leaves the museum and accepts the imperfect chance of being with his sister.
