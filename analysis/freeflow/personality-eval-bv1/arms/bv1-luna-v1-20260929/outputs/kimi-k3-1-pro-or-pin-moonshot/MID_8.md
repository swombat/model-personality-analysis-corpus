Evaluator: withheld

Condition: MID

## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW. A lyrical first-person reflection uses a morning walk to explore attention, memory, and the lives unfolding around the narrator.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant and self-correcting: it takes pleasure in small urban details, then checks that pleasure against the realities of work, grief, and homelessness. The narrator resists turning the city into scenery, noting that the bakery workers’ labor is “heavy, timed, and probably underpaid” and that dawn does not make the world innocent. Its pathos is quiet rather than sentimental, especially in the old man’s bread and remembered wife. The reader is invited to slow down and notice without expecting attention to solve anything or make hardship beautiful.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds urban dawn, ordinary labor, traces of lives past, grief carried through habit, and the contrast between wandering and digital efficiency. It repeatedly treats attention as a modest courtesy rather than a virtue or cure: walking gives worries “a slightly larger room,” while the closing image of the street breathing gathers the essay’s recurring sounds and its invitation to leave part of the day unclaimed.

## Evidence line
> Dawn does not make the world innocent, and I should not use its beauty to varnish over that fact.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the essay sustains a distinctive, humane emphasis on attention while repeatedly resisting easy consolation and grand conclusions.
