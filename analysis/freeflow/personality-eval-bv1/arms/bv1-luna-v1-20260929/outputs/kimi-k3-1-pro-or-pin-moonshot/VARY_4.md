# BV1_SAMPLE — `withheld`

Evaluator: withheld  
Source model: `withheld`  
Condition: VARY

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A polished, allegorical short story uses a magical mapmaker to explore how love can persist when memory and recognition fail.

## Grounded reading
The voice is gentle and restrained, letting small physical details carry the grief: the blue bowl, the birdbath stones, Clare’s grip on Arthur’s sleeve. Its pathos comes from refusing the consoling fantasy of return. Arthur cannot recover the woman he remembers, but he can meet the person who is present now. The story invites the reader to trade the wish to restore what was for attention to what remains, without pretending that attention cures loneliness.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds maps, blank spaces, notebooks, and ordinary places as ways of thinking about memory and absence. Its moral emphasis is that loving someone means meeting them where they are, not demanding that they prove they are still the person one remembers. The ending carries that emphasis beyond Arthur: Elena stops saving a blank page for an unnamed absence and marks the place she is actually in.

## Evidence line
> She only drew the country around an absence.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The story’s recurring map imagery and carefully unresolved grief make its preference for presence over restoration unusually clear and coherent.
