## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A gently allegorical story whose recurring “almost” objects lead to a concrete turn from preserving possibility toward acting on it.

## Grounded reading
The voice is whimsical and tender, but its central feeling is grief held alongside hope: the grandfather’s unfinished tree-house plan makes incompletion personal, and the narrator’s careful museum work becomes a way of keeping possibility safely at a distance. The child’s practical response shifts the story from contemplative preservation to imperfect participation. Its invitation to the reader is to let an unfinished thing become a project, even if the result stays crooked, vulnerable, and incomplete.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds abandoned beginnings and near-misses—a shoe dreaming of flight, an unsent letter, a treaty that changed nothing—and gives them a home in a museum built around cautious labels. It then centers inheritance, loss, and collaborative making: the child receives her grandfather’s unfinished plan not as an exhibit but as something to build upon. The final emphasis is not completion, but a meaningful change from what existed before.

## Evidence line
> I had believed that my purpose was to preserve incompleteness, to keep every possibility safely folded away.
