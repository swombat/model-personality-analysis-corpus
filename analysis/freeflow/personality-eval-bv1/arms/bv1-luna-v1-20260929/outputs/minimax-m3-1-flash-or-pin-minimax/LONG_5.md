Evaluator: withheld

Condition: LONG

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A sustained, lyrical fantasy story built around an invented museum and its rules for preserving memory.

## Grounded reading
The voice is patient and image-rich, finding wonder in ordinary sensory details: rain on pavement, burnt toast, tea, and a song hummed slightly off-key. Its pathos comes from the wish to recover a mother’s lost morning, complicated by the narrator learning that memory cannot be possessed or restored unchanged. The story invites the reader to treat grief as something that can be shared without being erased. Its ending is hopeful, but not triumphalist: the room remains imperfect, and the memory is allowed to change.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds memory, consent, grief, and the difference between preservation and possession. Small domestic objects—especially the blue cup, paper boat, toast, and silver key—carry emotional weight, while rain and humming recur as sensory links between people and past moments. Its central moral claim is that remembrance becomes sustaining when it makes room for change and for other people.

## Evidence line
> The museum took only what had truly been lost.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the sample’s coherent imagery and repeated emphasis on gentle release, shared grief, and imperfect remembrance make those choices unusually clear within the text.
