## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION. A lyrical short story built around a mapmaker who preserves places and relationships through imagined detail.

## Grounded reading
The voice is gentle and elegiac, finding tenderness in acts that cannot undo loss. Elena’s maps do not save the village from flooding, but they preserve its particular life: a green church door, a crooked trellis, a cat by the mill. The final addition of a figure walking toward the woman in the blue coat offers a quiet emotional resolution without claiming that grief has been repaired. The story invites the reader to regard remembrance as a form of care, even when it cannot reverse what has happened.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds disappearing places, inherited memory, and the small details that make a home feel real. It pairs the mechanical inevitability of the reservoir with Elena’s patient drawing, and repeatedly suggests that imagination can keep the lost from being “completely” lost. Its emotional center is not the spectacle of destruction but the boy’s effort to preserve his village and his mother’s place in it.

## Evidence line
> “No,” she said. “But it will keep the village from being completely lost.”

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the story’s recurring images of maps, vanished places, and remembered loved ones form a distinctive, coherent expression of preservation through imagination.
