# BV1_SAMPLE — `withheld`

Evaluator: withheld
Source model: `withheld`
Condition: LONG

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A sustained, literary fable uses a museum of unfinished objects to turn an unusual premise into a story about value, memory, and accepting incompletion.

## Grounded reading
The voice is patient and observant, finding emotional weight in modest things: a folded railway ticket, a worn key, an unfinished painting, a chair that remembers its owner. Its pathos comes less from dramatic loss than from the quiet persistence of what people meant to do, and could not do. The narrator begins as a municipal assessor who sorts objects by formal criteria, then finds those criteria inadequate to the human attachments they leave out. The story invites the reader to consider that an unfinished life or effort need not be treated as a failed version of a finished one. Its repeated reflections make that invitation explicit, sometimes nearly aphoristic, while the accumulating objects give it warmth and texture.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds unfinished work, private memory, and the tension between bureaucratic measures of cultural value and people’s attachments to ordinary objects. Rain, blank spaces, abandoned journeys, letters, keys, and broken or incomplete things recur as emblems of possibility rather than mere loss. The moral emphasis is that preserving what remains unresolved can be a form of care; the ending offers continuation and breathing room instead of tidy completion.

## Evidence line
> The museum does not complete people. It gives them permission to stop without being erased.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The story’s distinctive, recurring choice to treat incompletion as worthy of tenderness gives this sample more signal than a generic inspirational fable.
