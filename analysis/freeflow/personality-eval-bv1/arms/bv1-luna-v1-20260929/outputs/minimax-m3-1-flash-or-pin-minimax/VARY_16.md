## Grounded reading
The voice is elegiac but not resigned: precise civic details and tactile objects—bells, a red paint line, a missing clapper—give shape to grief over a vanished landscape. Mara’s insistence on ringing the bells makes hope feel less like confidence in a happy outcome than a practice of remembering together. The story invites the reader to see beauty and communal attention as meaningful even when they cannot reverse loss.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds environmental change, inherited memory, and the tension between adapting to present reality and keeping faith with what has been lost. Its central objects—the bells, the dry harbor, the red tide mark, and blue-painted lampposts—turn remembrance into a public ritual. The ending favors collective hope and listening, while acknowledging that neither can restore the water.
