Evaluator: withheld  
Source model: `withheld`  
Condition: LONG

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A lyrical magical-realist story uses an impossible museum to explore grief, memory, and the cost of trying to preserve the past perfectly.

## Grounded reading
The voice is tender and elegiac, but it lets wonder and gentle humor soften the sadness: a museum opens “by appointment or forecast,” and weather becomes a way for people to enter moments they never got to live. The story’s emotional center is not recovering the past intact, but accepting its incompleteness. Its invitation to the reader is to share grief without turning memory into a prison—to let love survive as changing, partial recollection rather than a perfect scene held forever.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds weather as a tangible form of interrupted possibility: rain for a farewell that did not happen, snow for a dead sister’s birthday, and a summer afternoon tied to June’s mother. Repeated objects—the ledger, jars, key, globe, and ordinary umbrella—make memory feel collectible but also costly. The moral claim that emerges is that loss cannot be undone, and that letting memory change can be an act of love rather than betrayal.

## Evidence line
> The museum did not undo loss.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The sample’s recurring weather-and-memory imagery and its consistent movement from longing for perfect recovery toward acceptance make its emotional and moral choices unusually clear.
