## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A quiet, gently uncanny story uses the sea’s returned objects to bring grief into ordinary life.

## Grounded reading
The voice is restrained and tender, letting small actions carry the weight of loss: Maeve keeps the perfume stopper, dials her brother’s old number, and watches his handmade boat float in the sink. The strange premise stays understated; the returned objects matter less as spectacle than as private links to people who are gone. The ending offers connection without claiming that grief is resolved: Maeve remembers a detail about the boat, laughs with a friend, and puts it back on the shelf. The reader is invited to notice how memory can return through objects and conversation, and how reaching out can make room for it.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds grief for Maeve’s mother and brother, the sea’s uncanny return of lost belongings, and the intimate meanings objects acquire through use and memory. Its mood is melancholy but not bleak, with a modest moral emphasis on companionship and renewed contact rather than public display or spectacle.

## Evidence line
> Her brother had always been good at making things.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the story is coherent and distinctive in its recurring objects and understated movement from private loss toward human connection.
