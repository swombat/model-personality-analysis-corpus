## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION. A carefully shaped short story uses a recurring sound-collection motif to connect intimate attention with loss and community.

## Grounded reading
The voice is gentle and observant, finding emotional weight in small sensory details rather than dramatic declarations. The museum’s ruined recordings give the story real grief, while the neighbors’ efforts to help keep that loss from becoming a simple lesson about resilience. The reader is invited to listen more carefully—to ordinary sounds, to what has disappeared, and to other people’s attachments.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds humble sounds and the objects that hold them: a radiator, an orange being peeled, a ferry bell, and the snow’s soft *whuff*. It treats preservation as both fragile and communal: recordings can be lost, but the people who remember them can gather, name what is gone, and make room for absence. The final sound is not a grand spectacle but flakes brushing a coat, heard because Tomas and Ada wait together.

## Evidence line
> Not everything could be replaced.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — the story is coherent and distinctive, with recurring attention to small sensory traces, loss, and communal care.
