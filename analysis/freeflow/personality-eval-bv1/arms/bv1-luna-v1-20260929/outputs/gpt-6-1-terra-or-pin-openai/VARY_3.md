## Grounded reading
The voice is dry, gentle, and observant: the strange event is met less with panic than with questions about permits, opening hours, and whether the rolls will sell. Its pathos arrives through the elderly woman’s memory of her cheerful husband, and through Mara’s small, practical kindnesses. The story invites the reader to notice how people carry on when systems fail—not by solving the impossible, but by helping one another where they can.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds time, civic bureaucracy, and the friction between rigid software and human judgment. Its recurring objects—the clocks, permit, walking stick, piano recording, and rolls—keep the surreal premise grounded in daily life. The moral emphasis is modest but clear: useful, compassionate action can matter even when certainty is unavailable.
