Mara’s grief is bound up with a familiar regret: she was in a hurry during a visit that seemed ordinary at the time. The story invites the reader to notice how love can live in unremarkable exchanges—a tomato sandwich, a complaint about mustard, watching a neighbor struggle with a rug—and how being present may matter more than finding the perfect thing to say.

## What the model chose to foreground

Its recurring objects—the handless clock, teacups, paper bag, and simple food—make time feel both magical and domestic. The resolution favors renewed attention to the living: Mara leaves slowly and calls her mother, not with urgent news but to ask what she is doing.
