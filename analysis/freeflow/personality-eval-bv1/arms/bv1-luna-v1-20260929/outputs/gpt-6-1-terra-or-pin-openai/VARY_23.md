## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A quiet, gently comic short story about a town preserving sounds that might otherwise disappear.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant and unhurried, finding tenderness in small noises and in the disagreements they keep alive. The story’s pathos comes not from a grand loss but from people missing ordinary details they may not even have liked. It invites the reader to listen more closely, and to see shared memory as something people make together—sometimes by arguing over whether the clock was two minutes slow.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds everyday sounds—the squeak of new shoes, a kettle nearly boiled, the rattle of a bus—and the communal work of recording and remembering them. Its mood is affectionate and lightly wry: the broken clock becomes an archive entry, and its final legacy is renewed conversation among neighbors.

## Evidence line
> Nora learned that the things people missed were not always things they had liked.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the story’s coherent arc repeatedly favors small sensory details, gentle humor, and communal memory as its emotional center.
