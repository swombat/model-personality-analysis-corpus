## Grounded reading
The voice is calm and matter-of-fact, letting strange objects—a jar of “TUESDAY AFTERNOON,” a bag of a remembered smell, a box that rattles near forgotten names—sit alongside umbrellas and mittens. The boy’s lost courage turns out not to be something Mrs. Vale can retrieve for him: he finds it by admitting he broke the kite and returning to his friend. The story invites the reader to see courage as taking responsibility and making repair, with kindness rather than punishment as the resolution.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds lost memories and feelings, small acts of care, and honesty between friends. Its fantasy is built around ordinary civic rituals—claim tickets, a ledger, a library book—and its moral emphasis is on courage expressed through accountability.
