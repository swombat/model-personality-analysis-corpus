# BV1_SAMPLE — `withheld`

Evaluator: withheld
Source model: `withheld`
Condition: VARY

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A quiet, gently wry short story in which a library’s closure draws strangers into making an improvised place for books and one another.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant and understated, finding humor in small incongruities—the “unnecessarily quiet day for a disaster,” the missing second volume—without making light of the loss. Mara’s missed appointment about her mother’s house gives the communal book-rescue a private undertow: she is stalled in one part of her life while finding a modest, useful thing to do in another. The story invites the reader to see care as practical and shared: carrying books inside, making a shelf, recording titles, waving someone over.

## What the model chose to foreground
The chosen objects are ordinary but charged: library notices, books left in the rain, a penciled atlas, a photograph, and a paper-bagged roll. The mood is melancholy softened by dry humor and neighborly warmth. The moral emphasis falls on preservation and participation rather than ownership or expertise: the retired librarian is told to leave the children’s markings, and Mara is told that knowing to bring the books in is enough.

## Evidence line
> “You knew to bring them in.”

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the story’s coherent, recurring movement from threatened books to improvised communal care gives its gentle, practical humanism a clear shape.
