# BV1_SAMPLE — `withheld`

Evaluator: withheld  
Source model: `withheld`  
Condition: LONG

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A polished, sustained magical-realist story whose invented library gives shape to grief, missed chances, and the limits of knowing another person.

## Grounded reading
The voice is gentle and patient, finding emotional weight in modest details: a dead fern, lentil soup, tomatoes, a call that lasts eleven minutes. Its pathos comes from resisting the fantasy that one different choice would have repaired everything. Mara’s grief is acknowledged without being cured; the almost-call with her mother leads not to a revelation but to an ordinary afternoon she cannot recover. The library’s rules also invite the reader to distinguish curiosity from entitlement: a possible story may offer perspective, but it cannot settle what someone meant or owed. The ending turns that restraint into a small act of connection, as Mara makes a call in the present rather than trying to rewrite the past.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds missed opportunities, bereavement, and the comfort—and danger—of imagining alternate versions of a life. Repeated ordinary objects and acts of care—tea, plants, food, catalog cards, phone calls—keep the supernatural premise grounded in daily life. Its moral emphasis is that stories about what almost happened are not proof of what people felt, and that some boundaries remain important even when someone is grieving or afraid.

## Evidence line

Medium — The story’s recurring attention to ordinary grief, respectful boundaries, and modest acts of connection makes its moral and emotional choices unusually coherent and distinctive.
