## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical, carefully structured essay that uses phantom islands to explore how errors persist and how difficult it is to establish absence.

## Grounded reading
The voice is curious and humane, with a taste for memorable turns of phrase and a steady concern for the people caught inside systems of inherited belief. It resists treating mistaken witnesses as foolish: sailors, chartmakers, and Arctic explorers are shown acting on partial evidence and understandable incentives. The essay’s pathos gathers around the costly pursuit of Crocker Land and the institutional reluctance to remove doubtful marks. Its invitation is to look closely at how knowledge is copied and protected—and to recognize that checking an absence can be an act of discovery in its own right.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay foregrounds phantom islands, charts, mirages, and the challenge of proving that something is not there. It links these objects to a broader moral and epistemic claim: cautious habits can preserve errors when they prevent disconfirming evidence from arriving. It also chooses to make that claim personal near the end, comparing the narrator’s own compilation of reports to a chart assembled from inherited sources.

## Evidence line
