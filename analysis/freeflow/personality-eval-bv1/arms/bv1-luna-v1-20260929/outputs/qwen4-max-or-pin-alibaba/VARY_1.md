## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A reflective essay turns an ordinary drawer of obsolete cables into an account of grief, memory, and the self we leave behind.

## Grounded reading
The voice is witty and conversational, but its humor keeps giving way to tenderness: obsolete plugs become “surviving halves of pairs,” and the drawer becomes a discreet place to grieve for former versions of oneself. The piece is careful not to sentimentalize everything; its admission that most clutter is simply friction makes the more charged objects feel earned. It invites the reader to inspect an everyday habit without shame, and to notice what a seemingly useless object might be preserving.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds obsolete technology, household clutter, and the quiet persistence of memory. Its central moral claim is that keeping things can be a small refusal to accept that a period of life is over, though the essay also insists that laziness accounts for most of the drawer. The mood moves from dry comedy toward elegy, then ends with a modest, unresolved act of keeping the cable.
