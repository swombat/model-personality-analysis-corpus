## Grounded reading
The voice moves between scientific explanation and reflective wonder, repeatedly turning invisible processes into legible signals: a smell becomes evidence of feeding, a plume becomes a map, and plastic becomes a counterfeit message. The essay’s pathos comes from holding beauty and violence together—calling the scent “the exhaust of a massacre,” then finding wonder in how seabirds read it. Its invitation to the reader is not to abandon familiar pleasure in the sea air, but to deepen it by learning what the sensation carries.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay foregrounds hidden ecological activity beneath a familiar sensory experience: plankton being consumed, seabirds tracking chemical traces, and atmospheric effects that complicate a once-beautiful climate hypothesis. It also chooses to dwell on human-made deception, as plastic emits a signal animals interpret as food. The moral emphasis is that knowledge need not spoil wonder; it can make the world more vivid while sharpening attention to the harm humans introduce.
