## Grounded reading

It moves from the oddity of nonexistent islands to a broader concern: people often trust knowledge passed along through documents, and contrary evidence can get absorbed into the story rather than overturn it. The essay is skeptical without becoming cynical; it respects the practical need to rely on others while praising the costly, unglamorous act of going to look. Its self-comparison between the model’s inherited testimony and human knowledge gives the argument personal stakes, and the ending invites the reader to keep verification possible rather than settle for a tidy moral.

## What the model chose to foreground
The model foregrounds maps, false islands, inherited testimony, and the social machinery that can preserve a mistaken claim. It gives particular weight to the effort and risk involved in checking—Seton sailing through Sandy Island, MacMillan’s fruitless expedition—and complicates the lesson with Agloe, where a deliberate fiction became real. The selected moral is not simply “distrust maps,” but that knowledge needs both inheritance and a willingness to test it.
