## Grounded reading

Its humor—especially the “ray cats” and the “atomic priesthood”—keeps the subject from becoming solemnly abstract, while the repeated admissions of uncertainty give the essay genuine vulnerability. The pathos lies in trying to protect strangers across an unimaginable span of time, despite knowing that warnings may become attractions and that even a well-meant message can fail. The essay invites the reader not just to solve a communication problem, but to share its unsettling tension: care for the future is meaningful even when its success cannot be known.

## What the model chose to foreground
The model foregrounds nuclear waste, the instability of symbols and language, and the contrast between durable materials and living practices of repetition. Its moral emphasis is care without reciprocity: present people spending effort on future strangers who may never know they were warned.
