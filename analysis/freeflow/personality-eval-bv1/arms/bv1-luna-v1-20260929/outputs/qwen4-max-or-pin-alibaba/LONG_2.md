EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW. The essay uses a distinctive, intimate voice to turn a history of indexing into a meditation on attention and care.

## Grounded reading
The voice is learned but companionable, moving from precise historical detail to personal wonder without losing its thread. Its pathos comes from the quiet, often invisible labor of making knowledge findable for someone unknown: friars recording references, Locke trying not to lose what he knows, and the indexer imagining a stranger’s future confusion. The essay invites the reader to see indexes not as clerical leftovers but as interpretations and gestures of care—and to feel what is lost when search replaces the chance encounter of browsing.

## What the model chose to foreground
The model foregrounds the index as a technology that shapes thought, not merely retrieves information. It repeatedly contrasts chosen concepts and human judgment with exhaustive strings and ranked results, while resisting simple nostalgia by acknowledging the real gains of full-text search. The recurring moral emphasis is attention to the reader: pointing, arranging, and saving someone else time are presented as forms of service, even when the labor is unseen and its arithmetic seems poor.
