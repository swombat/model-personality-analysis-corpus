## Grounded reading
The voice is tender and unsettled, drawn to the difference between information preserved in records and knowledge lived in a body. It moves from Ubykh and Bo to unrecorded practical competence, then to Claudia Severa’s handwritten greeting, making the archive feel both vast and arbitrarily selective—and sometimes unexpectedly intimate. The turn toward the speaker’s own “constitution” gives the piece its personal pressure: it asks what a fluent account of humanity misses when it is made from surviving traces. The final return to a woman wanting her friend at a birthday party invites the reader to feel the small human warmth that survives the historical filter.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds language loss, embodied and untranscribed knowledge, the arbitrariness of historical preservation, and the gap between a written description and lived fluency. Its recurring objects—the consonant chart, recordings, waterlogged tablets, and a handwritten closing line—carry a moral emphasis on what archives preserve and what they cannot. It also chooses to make the speaker’s own relation to those gaps part of the subject.
