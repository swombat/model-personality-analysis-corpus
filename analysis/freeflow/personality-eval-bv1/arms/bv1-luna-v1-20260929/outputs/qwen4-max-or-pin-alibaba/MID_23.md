## Grounded reading
The voice is lucid and warmly skeptical, finding wonder rather than merely exposing error in stories about false words and invented places. Its central unease is that trust is unavoidable, while its counterweight is that copying can create as well as distort: Agloe becomes a store, and a fabricated word can briefly acquire authority. The first-person passages make the essay’s abstraction more intimate, especially when the narrator imagines its own confident errors taking on a life in the world. The reader is invited to hold knowledge neither with blanket suspicion nor easy certainty, but with attention to what a chain of transmission does when it lands.

## What the model chose to foreground
It foregrounds dictionaries, maps, copied errors, trap streets, and the paper town of Agloe as ways to consider how facts gain authority and how fictions can become real through belief and use. The mood is amused, reflective, and mildly unsettled; the moral emphasis is on discernment without impossible demands for total verification.
