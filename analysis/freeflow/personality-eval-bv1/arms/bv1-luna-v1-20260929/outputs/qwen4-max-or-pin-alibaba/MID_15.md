## Grounded reading
The voice is learned but not showy, turning archival history into a meditation on contingency and loss. The essay’s pathos comes from the contrast between what survives and what disappears: poems reused as mummy-case packaging, a landfill yielding literature, and mathematics hidden beneath a prayer book. The first-person reflection makes the reader share the unease of mistaking a filtered remnant for the whole past; the ending invites a different response, finding meaning in fragments without pretending they are complete.

## What the model chose to foreground
It foregrounds the accidental and often mundane conditions of cultural survival: copying choices, school curricula, reuse, neglect, and chance discoveries. Against the dramatic story of a library burned, it emphasizes slow attrition, then connects ancient material fragility to digital records that may not last. Fragments are presented not only as losses but as invitations to imagine and attend more closely.
