## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A literary nonfiction essay that turns the material accidents of preservation into a meditation on what survives and what gets lost.

## Grounded reading
The voice is curious, lucid, and quietly elegiac, but it resists treating the past as a beautiful archive waiting intact for discovery. Again and again, the essay shows that what survives is shaped by mundane systems—accounting, rubbish, religious rules, fire, reuse—and that this leaves us with a badly skewed picture of human life. The personal turns (“I find this consoling” and “I keep turning that over”) invite the reader to share not just the historical puzzle but the essay’s unease about how knowledge travels. Its resolution is hopeful but demanding: the essential inheritance is not a durable object, but people teaching other people how to read.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay foregrounds clay tablets, rubbish heaps, reused papyrus and parchment, burned archives, and obsolete digital media as accidental filters on memory. It gives particular weight to ordinary records—grain accounts, shopping lists, letters, bureaucratic paperwork—because they complicate the idea that literary or elite texts represent a whole civilization. Its central moral claim is that preservation depends not only on materials but on a continuing human community of readers.
