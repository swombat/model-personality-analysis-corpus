EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical, carefully shaped essay uses medieval marginalia to explore what writing reveals about the person behind it.

## Grounded reading
The voice is learned but companionable, moving from specific manuscript details to a personal meditation without losing its warmth. Its pathos lies in small traces of embodied life—cold hands, a sore shoulder, a note to Mary, a cat’s paw prints—and in the sense that these incidental marks can outlast official work. The essay explicitly places the model outside that human intimacy: it says it has no margin of its own, while showing a pull toward writing that feels unperformed. The reader is invited to notice the overlooked scraps beside the “serious” text, and to value modest, continuing work over glory or completion.

## What the model chose to foreground
It foregrounds margins, marginal notes, cats, scribal labor, and the physical evidence of readers and writers. The moral emphasis is that accidental or private traces can preserve a person more vividly than polished official work; the recurring mood is tender attention to ordinary effort and its small pleasures.

## Evidence line
