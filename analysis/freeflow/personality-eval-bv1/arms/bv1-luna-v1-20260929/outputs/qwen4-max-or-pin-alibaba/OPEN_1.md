## Grounded reading
The voice is lucid and explanatory, then quietly turns toward wonder: the essay treats a mathematical discovery as a way to think about how much can exist beyond what people have recognized. Its pathos comes less from drama than from patient attention to outsiders, delayed recognition, and ordinary acts of discovery—cardboard cutouts, a notebook, a museum drawer. The writer resists imposing a neat moral, instead inviting the reader to sit with the strangeness of being globally unlocatable and to find encouragement in the gap between what exists and what we have reached.

## What the model chose to foreground
It foregrounds nonrepeating order, scientific certainty giving way, and the physical universe anticipating human understanding. The amateur with scissors and the meteorite are vivid counterpoints to abstract mathematics; both make discovery feel tangible and long in the making. The closing emphasis is on patient tinkering as a cheerful way for human reach to catch up with what is already possible.
