## Grounded reading
The voice is learned without being merely explanatory: it moves from papyrus scraps and crocodile wrappings to the uneasy thought that what survives can pass for the whole world. Its pathos comes from the tension between loving Sappho’s fragment and recognizing that the damage which makes the fragment moving also obscures the complete work she made. The essay invites the reader to feel both the beauty of what remains and the limits of treating absence as meaningful shape.

## What the model chose to foreground
The model foregrounds accidental survival: discarded papyrus, damaged poems, school editions, and the unmarked gaps that disappear into our idea of a canon. It repeatedly turns physical remnants into a moral and epistemic claim—that inherited knowledge is a partial sample whose missing parts may be invisible. The closing return to Sappho keeps that claim from becoming a debunking exercise; the poem’s beauty survives the analysis.
