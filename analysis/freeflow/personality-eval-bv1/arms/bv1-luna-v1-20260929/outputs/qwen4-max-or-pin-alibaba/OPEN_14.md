## Grounded reading
The voice is nimble and assured, turning word histories into a way of unsettling familiar assumptions. Its pathos comes from the gap between knowing that old theories were wrong and recognizing that their vocabulary remains useful, even indispensable. The essay invites the reader to notice the metaphors embedded in ordinary speech, then to apply the same suspicion to present-day terms such as *hardwired*, *processing*, and *trauma*. Its ending accepts this inheritance without pretending it can be purified away.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay foregrounds language as a storehouse of abandoned theories: stars and humors, miasma and medicine, spirits and inspiration, machinery and mental life. It repeatedly returns to the tension between error and usefulness, culminating in the claim that inherited metaphors can be “inaccurate, indispensable, and somehow accurate anyway.” The choice to end by implicating its own account of “processing” gives the argument a self-questioning quality rather than a simple debunking posture.
