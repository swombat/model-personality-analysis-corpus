## Grounded reading
The voice is learned and inviting, using vivid examples to make an abstract claim feel tactile: notation is a “mesh” that helps some things become legible while letting others disappear. Its pathos lies in that double-edged bargain—what a system preserves, it may also flatten—and in the trust implied by leaving something for another person to complete. The repeated “I keep circling” and “I think” make the essay feel like an inquiry the writer is pursuing alongside the reader, rather than a verdict delivered from above.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay foregrounds musical notation, embodied craft, and the politics of legibility: St. Gall’s neumes, the staff, tablature, knitting charts, Braille music, and dance notation all become evidence for how formats shape what people can notice and transmit. It gives particular weight to cultural flattening—especially the way European notation can recast other musical traditions as inaccurate—and to a countervailing value in incompleteness: a score’s omissions can invite interpretation and participation. Its closing image returns to the “bird tracks” as a human-sized gap between writer and reader.
