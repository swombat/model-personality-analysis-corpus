## Grounded reading
The voice is curious and lightly theatrical, moving from the image of an ox-headed letter to the grand history of reference works without losing its conversational intimacy. Its pathos arrives late: the alphabet’s arbitrary order becomes consoling because it lets people coordinate without agreeing about what matters. The reader is invited to see familiar systems as both historical accidents and shared places to stand.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds the alphabet as an inherited, unexplained sequence; the material traces of its history, especially the Ugarit tablet and the meanings behind the first letter names; and alphabetical order as a neutral tool for organizing knowledge. Its central moral claim is that some durable human structures endure not because they are true, but because their emptiness makes cooperation possible.
