## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A polished, gently uncanny domestic story that turns house repairs into a conversation across generations.

## Grounded reading
The voice is attentive and tender, finding emotional weight in ordinary details: a breaker box, a dripping tap, a groaning stair. Its melancholy comes from Mara’s fear of trespassing on a life already embedded in the house, while its resolution offers permission rather than erasure: she can honor what came before and still make the place hers. The reader is invited to see care as a kind of conversation—with a house, with its former occupants, and with the future.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds inheritance, domestic knowledge, and the difference between preserving a home and living in it. Its recurring objects are practical notes, switches, paint, and small signs of a family’s past; its moral claim is that belonging comes not from leaving everything untouched, but from tending a place and adding one’s own life to it. The mood moves from anxious trespass to warm permission.

## Evidence line
> Arthur had not merely occupied this house; he had been in a half-century conversation with it.
