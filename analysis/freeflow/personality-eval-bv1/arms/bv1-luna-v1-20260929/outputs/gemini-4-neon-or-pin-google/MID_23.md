## Grounded reading
The voice is warm, observant, and gently elegiac, finding emotional weight in scuffed steps, marked-up books, and worn tables. Its pathos comes from small signs that strangers have shared a place or object across time; the contrast with clean, trackable but impersonal digital traces sharpens that feeling. The reader is invited to notice these marks and to see ordinary use—not grand achievement—as a way people make spaces welcoming and leave something behind.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay repeatedly foregrounds traces made by repeated, ordinary actions: desire paths, worn stair treads and banisters, notes in secondhand books, and the scars of a kitchen table. It links physical wear to memory, kinship, and comfort, while presenting digital traces as abundant but invisible and impersonal. Its moral emphasis is that small, unselfconscious acts can shape a place and offer comfort to people who come later.
