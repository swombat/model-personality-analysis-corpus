## Grounded reading
The voice is patient and sensory, lingering over craft details—the tuning fork’s vibration, the pin’s resistance, the piano’s wobble—so that skilled attention becomes a form of care. The loss is acknowledged without being overcome: the piano will be destroyed, but Clara gets one last chance to hear and play it as something alive. The story invites the reader to value this brief restoration not as a rescue, but as a gentler way to say goodbye.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds an old piano, the small objects preserved inside it, and the mother’s remembered music as vessels of family memory. It links repair to listening and emotional attunement, then makes the impending demolition a test of whether something temporary can still matter. The closing moral favors a meaningful farewell over permanence.

## Evidence line
