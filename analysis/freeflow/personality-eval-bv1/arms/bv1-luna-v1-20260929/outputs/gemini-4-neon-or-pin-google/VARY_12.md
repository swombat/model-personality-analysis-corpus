## Grounded reading
The voice is patient and sensory, lingering over textures and sounds rather than rushing toward plot. Its pathos comes from the gap between what Mara can preserve and what she has already lost: the station’s clattering board can be recorded, but the kettle, newspaper pages, and childhood floorboards survive only in memory. The ending offers a gentle release from preservation rather than a triumph over loss: Mara leaves the recorder packed away and listens without trying to keep anything. The reader is invited to notice ordinary sounds as temporary, and to feel both affection for what is passing and curiosity about what will replace it.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds obsolete machinery, the labor of listening closely, and the tension between recording a moment and letting it pass. Its recurring objects—split-flaps, headphones, hard drives, a microphone—make preservation concrete, while the closing electric bus suggests that every era’s new sounds may eventually become someone else’s nostalgia. The moral emphasis is tender rather than anti-progress: change is real, but it need not mean the world is becoming quieter or poorer.
