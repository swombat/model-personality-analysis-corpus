GENRE_FICTION — A literary short story about a drought exposing a drowned town and a former resident’s changed understanding of what water preserved.

## Grounded reading
The voice is elegiac and tactile, building grief through precise remnants: the loose flagstone, the empty belfry, the rusted bicycle wheel. Julian expects the exposed town to unlock his childhood sorrow, but instead feels he has intruded on a sleeping place; that quiet embarrassment makes his attachment feel intimate rather than grand. The story invites the reader to look past spectacle and consider how memory can enlarge a lost home, while also seeing its preservation as something material and fragile.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds drought, displacement, and the difference between a remembered home and its exposed remains. Water is both the force that submerged Aldermere and the medium that kept it intact; the ending’s hope for prolonged rain favors concealment and preservation over a dramatic recovery.
