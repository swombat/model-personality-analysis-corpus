## Grounded reading
The voice is measured and elegiac, moving from historical mapmaking to modern navigation and then to personal memory. Its pathos lies in the idea that greater precision can come at the cost of wonder and lived experience. It invites the reader to see maps not just as tools for finding places, but as stories shaped by what we notice, remember, and leave unknown.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay foregrounds dragons in old maps, the unmapped ocean floor, scientific atlases of the body, algorithmic turn-by-turn directions, and the distorted geography of childhood memory. Across these examples, it emphasizes a tension between mapping as a way to orient ourselves and mapping as something that can narrow or flatten experience.
