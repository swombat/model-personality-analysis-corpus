## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A polished coastal mystery with speculative elements, centered on an archivist and a recovered ship’s ledger.

## Grounded reading
The voice is patient and tactile, giving objects and procedures as much care as it gives the supernatural mystery. Salt, slate, brass, paper, and the slow work of preservation create a melancholy atmosphere in which the uncanny feels less like a shock than an opening into another way of understanding loss. Miles’s recognition that his careful life has become a hiding place gives the story its emotional weight; the ending invites the reader to see his letter as a small, practical act of returning to the living.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds preservation and erasure: an archivist catalogs what the sea returns, while the *Meridian*’s ledger records a place beyond ordinary measurement. Its recurring objects—especially the sealed box, chronometer, ledger, and dark leaf—make evidence itself feel fragile and uncanny. It also chooses a quiet moral turn: Miles must stop treating patience as an excuse for withdrawal and begin reconnecting with his family.

## Evidence line
> Everything drifted, everything eroded, and sooner or later the water returned whatever you thought you had buried cleanly out of sight.
