## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW. A sensory narrative gradually turns a greenhouse cleanup into a meditation on inherited care and the value of maintaining things that wear out.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant and unhurried, finding intimacy in practical details: a chewed pencil, a tin of saved screws, rain caught in a basin. Its pathos comes from imagining the previous owner’s hands and declining strength without pretending to know their story. The narrator begins with a plan to clear things away, then learns to see repair and reuse as forms of care. The reader is invited to slow down and notice the quiet labor that makes a life hold together.

## What the model chose to foreground
The model foregrounds a leaning greenhouse, weather, worn tools, and objects saved for another use. Decay is present in cracked glass, rust, and rot, but the moral emphasis falls on tending rather than giving up: maintenance may not stop things from falling apart, yet it can make today’s door open smoothly and tomorrow’s work possible.

## Evidence line
> Nothing in this shed was ever discarded.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the sample’s distinctive sensory detail and recurring movement from decay toward care make its chosen preoccupation unusually clear.
