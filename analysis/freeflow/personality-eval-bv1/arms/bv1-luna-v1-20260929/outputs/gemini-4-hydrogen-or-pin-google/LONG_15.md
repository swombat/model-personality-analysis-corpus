GENRE_FICTION — A polished literary mystery with speculative elements, built around a remote listening station and a decades-old shipwreck.

## Grounded reading
The voice is patient, tactile, and atmospheric, turning technical details—hydrophones, tape reels, bearings, and resonant frequencies—into a way of attending to grief. Its central pleasure is listening closely: first to the sea’s unfamiliar sounds, then to the human loss hidden in them. The story lets scientific explanation and emotional meaning coexist rather than treating one as a trick that cancels the other. It invites the reader to share Mara’s shift from detached measurement toward care, while keeping the mystery grounded in the material world.

## What the model chose to foreground
It foregrounds cold, remote maritime landscapes; obsolete instruments and carefully kept records; the deep sea as a world of persistent sound; and the moral value of staying with what others have abandoned. The Salt-Clock becomes both a physical phenomenon and a memorial to Henrik’s brother, while Mara’s work of decommissioning the station is complicated by the human history its equipment has preserved. The resolution favors intimate recognition and continued listening over spectacle or rescue.
