Evaluator: withheld

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A carefully paced literary story uses a sound recordist’s work in a soon-to-be-flooded town to explore what preservation can and cannot save.

## Grounded reading
The voice is patient and richly sensory, especially attuned to how sound changes with distance, rooms, water, and listeners. Its pathos comes less from dramatic confrontation than from the slow inventory of a place being emptied: the removed mail slot, the gutted church, Hollis’s tools, the train heard through the valley. The story gives real weight to Hollis’s objection that recordings may sharpen loss rather than ease it, then lets Julian’s quiet conviction coexist with that objection instead of defeating it. It invites the reader to practice the same attention Julian does—and to feel the tenderness and inadequacy of trying to keep what is disappearing.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds a community displaced by a dam, the textures of ordinary work and local sound, and the ethics of turning a place into an archive. Recurring objects—the bell, walnut shells, train, river, and recording equipment—make listening a form of witness while keeping the question of whether witness is enough unresolved. The closing image of the steeple tip above the mist gives the loss a visual counterpart to the sounds Julian has tried to preserve.

## Evidence line
> Somebody had to stand still in the dark long enough to notice what was leaving.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The sample’s coherent, distinctive attention to sensory detail and its sustained moral tension make it revealing, while its polished literary-fiction form shapes that evidence.
