## Grounded reading
The voice is contemplative and tender, drawn to objects that turn uncertainty into orientation: annotated maps, a lighthouse’s coded flashes, glass softened by the sea, a familiar mark on a staircase. Its emotional center is loss made more bearable with time. The essay doesn’t claim that damage disappears; it suggests that ordinary life can wear down its sharpness until memory becomes something one can hold. The reader is invited to notice small, private landmarks and to see writing as a generous signal sent into darkness, without knowing who will receive it.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds the limits of knowledge, the comfort of honest uncertainty, and acts of orientation offered without expectation of return. Its recurring objects—maps, beacons, shards, handwritten marks—give material form to memory and connection. The mood is elegiac but not bleak: time softens pain, and modest traces of other lives can still feel companionable.
