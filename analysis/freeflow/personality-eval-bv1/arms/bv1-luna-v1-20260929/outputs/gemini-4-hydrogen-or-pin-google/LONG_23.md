# BV1_SAMPLE — `withheld`

Evaluator: withheld  
Source model: `withheld`  
Condition: LONG

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A carefully paced literary story uses an encounter between a county assessor and a retired observatory keeper to turn a demolition visit into a preservation choice.

## Grounded reading
The voice is atmospheric and patient, building feeling through material particulars: the salt-stained station, its clockwork instruments, the storm, and the recordings of the seabed. Its pathos rests less on Arthur’s loneliness than on the threatened loss of what he has spent a life listening to and maintaining. Clara’s shift from inventorying objects as scrap to finding a bureaucratic way to protect them invites the reader to reconsider what counts as useful or worth saving. The story’s moral turn is warm, but practical rather than grand: care takes the form of an accurate report and a delay.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds listening, mechanical timekeeping, and the hidden life of places. Brass gauges, paper scrolls, aluminum disks, and the clockwork’s “tock-shhh” give the ocean a recordable presence; the contrast with satellite measurements and tidy spreadsheets makes efficiency feel like a kind of erasure. It also chooses intergenerational stewardship and ordinary institutional leverage as its resolution: Clara protects the archive not by defeating the system, but by finding a provision within it.

## Evidence line
> This place wasn't a warehouse or a bunker. It was an ear, shaped out of granite and brass, pressed patiently against the edge of the world.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The story’s distinctive sensory focus on sound and its recurring concern with preservation are sustained across the setting, objects, and resolution.
