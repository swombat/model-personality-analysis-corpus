## Grounded reading
The voice is patient and tactile, lingering over brass teeth, ink, salt, weather, and the sounds of machinery. Its affection for Arthur is expressed through competence rather than sentimentality: he reads the water closely, risks himself to free the jammed float, and keeps the record because someone must still be looking. The pathos comes from a world losing its human-scale practices, but the ending avoids simple triumph over technology. Mara’s respect and wish to calibrate the new instrument against the old one offer continuity instead of a reprieve from change. The reader is invited to value not only the old machine, but the knowledge and care made visible through its use.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds mechanical complexity, local observation, long-maintained records, and the difference between a prediction and the unruly sea. Its moral emphasis is that neither abstract systems nor hands-on experience tells the whole story alone; understanding comes from attending to both. The storm rescue makes that claim concrete, while the closing image of Arthur teaching Mara how to read the Engine makes succession, rather than preservation at any cost, the resolution.
