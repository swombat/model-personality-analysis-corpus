## Grounded reading
The voice is lyrical and patient, attentive to material texture: salt, rust, wool, wet stone, and objects worn down by the sea. Its pathos comes less from overt grief than from Mara’s careful acts of recording and her recognition that keeping things safe is impossible. The story invites the reader to regard discarded objects—and the lives and labor they imply—as worthy of attention, while resisting an easy romance of isolation: Mara knows solitude can become dangerous, and the ocean’s indifference is both frightening and relieving.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story centers on collecting, loss, and the transformation of objects by time and weather. Its recurring objects—the ledger, insulator, ruined compass, plastic debris, and ship timber—make attention a form of care, while the threatened weather station gives that care a practical stake. It also foregrounds a tension between human systems of preservation and the sea’s ongoing alteration of what people try to keep.
