## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A carefully plotted coastal literary story uses an abandoned town and a surveyor’s work to give shape to its themes of loss and memory.

## Grounded reading
The voice is patient and sensuous, attentive to the textures of a changing place: shingle under tires, black peat, waterlogged ledgers, a skiff that will never float again. Its pathos is restrained rather than dramatic. Julian’s survey turns measurement into an act of witness, while Walter’s quiet care for the skiff gives that idea a human counterpart. The story invites the reader to notice what official records flatten or leave behind—not just a coastline’s changing position, but the rhubarb bed, drowned oaks, and vanished homes that made it matter.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds erosion, retreat, and the limits of maps as records of a living world. It repeatedly pairs precise instruments and documents—charts, tide tables, GPS coordinates—with intimate remnants that exceed their neat boundaries. Its moral emphasis is that documenting loss cannot stop it, but can still honor what stood there. The mood is elegiac and atmospheric, with a brief turn toward suspense as the tide returns.

## Evidence line
> You measured them because there was a terrible, quiet dignity in knowing precisely what was being lost—in saying, at least once, *this stood here, and I saw it before the water closed back over.*

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The sample makes its preoccupation with witnessing loss unusually clear, recurring through maps, ledgers, field notes, and the final return of the tide.
