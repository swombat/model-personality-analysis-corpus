## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A polished literary vignette follows a newcomer’s late-night laundromat encounter toward a small, earned sense of belonging.

## Grounded reading
The voice is sensory and patient, turning ordinary details—the dryer’s rhythm, the orange’s sharpness, warm laundry—into emotional bearings. Its pathos is quiet: the narrator feels exposed among boxes and unfamiliar walls, then finds relief not through a grand revelation but through another person’s local knowledge and unforced kindness. The scene invites the reader to notice how a brief, low-pressure exchange can make a place feel more inhabitable.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds relocation, loneliness, and the provisional feeling of a new home, set against the tactile warmth and smells of a laundromat at night. It gives moral weight to modest hospitality: sharing half an orange and offering a practical detail about the building become acts of welcome. Rain, fluorescent light, warm clothes, and the older woman’s calm presence shape a movement from unease toward borrowed warmth.

## Evidence line
> When you live among taped boxes, your life starts to look like an inventory rather than a home, and by midnight the quiet of an unfamiliar room can begin to feel less like peace and more like an accusation.
