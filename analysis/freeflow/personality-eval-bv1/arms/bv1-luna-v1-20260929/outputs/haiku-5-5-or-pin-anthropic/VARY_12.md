## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A compact literary meditation on grief, memory, and the meanings attached to ordinary household objects.

## Grounded reading
The voice is tender and observant, letting grief surface through concrete details rather than grand declarations. The drawer becomes a way to encounter the grandmother’s care indirectly: the saved note, worn pencil, and accumulated odds and ends suggest affection that was present but not always explained. The ending invites the reader to see keeping and interpreting small things as a form of inherited love.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample centers an inherited drawer, its clutter, and the narrator’s gradual recognition of what the grandmother preserved. Its mood moves from reluctance and quiet sadness toward warmth, while holding onto the ambiguity of objects whose original reasons for being kept are partly lost. The narrator’s choice to keep the keys, thimble, pencil, and note emphasizes that uncertainty itself can be worth preserving.

## Evidence line
> I hope they feel, as I did, that they were loved.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The sustained attention to domestic objects and the restrained turn from loss toward tenderness make this sample notably distinctive and coherent.
