Evaluator: withheld

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A polished magical-realist story uses a found object to give grief a quiet, consoling shape.

## Grounded reading
The voice is gentle and lyrical, with sensory details that make the hill feel both eerie and welcoming: silver-rippled grass, murmuring lost things, a knife warm from the sun. The story’s pathos comes from the narrator’s search for a father’s keepsake after his funeral. Its comfort is measured rather than absolute: the hill preserves what is lost, but does not promise to return everything. The reader is invited to treat grief as a search that may lead to recognition, gratitude, and a way home.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story centers on lost objects as vessels of memory, especially the father’s worn pocketknife. It chooses a mood of soft wonder over fear, and gives the hill a patient, nonjudgmental role: a keeper of things that may be recovered or may remain lost. Its moral emphasis is that searching matters, but not every loss can—or should—be undone.

## Evidence line
> Some things, I think, are meant to stay lost.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the coherent imagery and careful balance between consolation and irretrievable loss make the sample distinctive, though its lyrical fairy-tale form is familiar.
