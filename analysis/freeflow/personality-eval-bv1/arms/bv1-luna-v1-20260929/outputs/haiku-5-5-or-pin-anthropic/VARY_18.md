## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A literary short story built around a granddaughter discovering her grandmother’s lighthouse logbooks.

## Grounded reading
The voice is restrained and intimate, letting small details—stale bread, a failing lamp, a whale that might be a shadow—carry grief and affection. The story’s pathos gathers around the grandmother’s private notes about loss, duty, and continuing to tend the light; its invitation is to notice the human traces left in ordinary records and to make room for one’s own. The ending turns inheritance into a modest action: the narrator begins a log of her own.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds the margins beside official records: private feeling that cannot fit into measurements of wind, pressure, and visibility. It links the grandmother’s lighthouse work to bereavement and quiet persistence, then passes that responsibility to the narrator. Water, darkness, lamps, and handwritten entries recur as images of indifference, care, and being remembered.

## Evidence line
> I began to understand that the official entries were a kind of armor.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the story’s recurring light-and-recordkeeping imagery and carefully understated grief make its chosen themes distinctive and coherent.
