## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A reflective first-person essay turns an ordinary walk into a gentle meditation on attention, respect, and noticing what was already there.

## Grounded reading
The voice is measured and intimate, with a lyrical eye for small street details: fern fronds like a violin, a faded ribbon, a cat watching “with the complete seriousness of a magistrate.” Its pathos comes from the quiet recognition that routine can make a life feel absent from itself, and that such inattention need not be permanent. The essay resists turning mindfulness into a demand for constant gratitude; noticing can reveal drabness or inconvenience as well as beauty. It invites the reader to look again, without making that act into a performance.

## What the model chose to foreground
The piece foregrounds ordinary urban details—the fern in the sidewalk crack, the baker’s seasonal window, the brass plaque—and the contrast between moving through a place on autopilot and recognizing it as a place. It presents attention as respect, perhaps love, and closes on the possibility that noticing can spread gently from one person to another.

## Evidence line
> Attention, it turns out, is contagious in the gentlest way.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The recurring fern and the turn from personal noticing to a neighbor’s quiet care give the essay a coherent, distinctive attentional ethic.
