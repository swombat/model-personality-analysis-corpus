# BV1_SAMPLE — `withheld`

Evaluator: withheld
Source model: `withheld`
Condition: LONG

## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A polished reflective essay that uses commonplace books to explore memory, attention, inheritance, and the making of a self.

## Grounded reading
The voice is measured and intimate, moving from historical explanation into personal admission: “I forget nearly everything I read.” That vulnerability gives the essay its emotional center. Forgetting is not treated as a personal failing so much as a reason to practice attention—to copy, choose, annotate, and return. The piece invites readers to resist frictionless collecting and to treat reading as a form of care. Its closing image of a sentence waiting years later offers a gentle, hopeful resolution rather than a demand to adopt a particular habit.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay foregrounds handwritten notebooks, copied sentences, marginal notes, and the contrast between deliberate copying and effortless digital saving. Its recurring moral claim is that attention involves choice, and that what we choose to remember helps form us. It also emphasizes borrowed language as personal inheritance, with the most vivid examples linking quotations to grief, family, embarrassment, and past selves.

## Evidence line
> The act of copying slowed you down enough to notice the shape of a sentence, the rhythm of a clause, the way a writer had turned an idea to catch the light.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the essay sustains a distinctive preoccupation with attention as care, developing it through recurring images of copying, friction, memory, and inheritance.
