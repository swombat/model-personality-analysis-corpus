EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical personal meditation turns an ordinary kitchen drawer into an image of memory, loss, and the stories people make from fragments.

## Grounded reading
The voice is quiet, reflective, and gently elegiac. It finds emotional weight in mundane leftovers—brittle rubber bands, uncertain batteries, unidentified keys—and treats their disorder as a record of lives that cannot be fully recovered. The grandmother’s house gives the meditation personal stakes, while the closing image of things waiting in the dark offers comfort without claiming that their meanings can be restored. The reader is invited to regard clutter not just as failed housekeeping, but as traces worth approaching with curiosity and tenderness.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds household residue, fading memory, unreliable recollection, inheritance, and the imaginative work of whoever encounters an object later. Its recurring keys and drawer make a specific moral emphasis: letting things remain unresolved can preserve a place for both tenderness and invention.

## Evidence line
