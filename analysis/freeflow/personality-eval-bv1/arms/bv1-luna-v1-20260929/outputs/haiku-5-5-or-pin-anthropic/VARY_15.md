## Grounded reading
The voice is tender and observant, making grief palpable through silences, worn books, and the radiator’s small knocks rather than dramatic declarations. Marguerite’s pathos comes from a life narrowed by loss that slowly opens through attention to another person’s ordinary details. The story invites the reader to see small acts of recognition—leaving a note, asking a question, showing up—as real forms of companionship and care.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds bereavement, books and marginalia, anonymous connection, and the private histories carried inside seemingly ordinary lives. Its moral emphasis is that people may need one another before they know each other, and that letting someone in can be a modest but meaningful act. The breathing streetlights and softly recurring radiator give the ending a restrained, hopeful mood.
