## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW. This is a reflective personal essay whose recurring objects and unresolved feelings give it a distinctive, intimate voice.

## Grounded reading
The voice is measured and self-questioning, moving from a familiar kitchen drawer toward larger questions of memory, identity, and worth. Its pathos gathers around the stopped watch and the man who gave it: the narrator is unsure whether keeping it honors a relationship or avoids deciding what to do with the gift. The essay invites the reader to inspect their own possessions without demanding either sentimental preservation or ruthless decluttering; its closing emphasis is on noticing and making a choice, even when the reason remains imperfectly clear.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds obsolete cables, a stopped watch, a broken pen, and other objects whose practical use has faded while their histories remain. It treats function as an incomplete measure of value, linking kept things to memory, deferred decisions, repair, consumer disposability, and aging. The comparison between broken objects and people makes the essay’s moral claim especially clear: usefulness should not determine whether something deserves care.

## Evidence line
> We are allowed to keep some things without a reason we can fully articulate.

Medium: the essay sustains a coherent, distinctive inquiry through recurring objects and a specific emotional conflict, rather than relying only on a generic thesis.
