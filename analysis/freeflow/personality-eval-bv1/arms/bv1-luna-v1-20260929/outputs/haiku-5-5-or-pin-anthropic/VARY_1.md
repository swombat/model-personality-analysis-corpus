## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A reflective, gently lyrical essay turns an everyday observation into an invitation to notice the memories and ideas that surface unbidden.

## Grounded reading
The voice is intimate and unhurried, moving between ordinary scenes—a mug at the sink, a song before waking, a kettle whistling—and larger claims about attention and creativity. Its pathos comes from small, sensory memories that carry time with them: the cousin’s fall, a grandmother’s laugh, the bark worn smooth by years of hands. Rather than treating distraction as a problem to eliminate, the piece invites the reader to pause, notice what has arrived, and decide whether to keep it.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds involuntary thought, memory, quiet intervals, and the idea that wandering attention can be a form of mental work. Its recurring objects—the coat pocket, pebble, rope swing, and visitor at the door—make the mind feel less like a machine than a place where unplanned things gather. The moral emphasis is modest: pay attention without surrendering judgment, since some thoughts are worth keeping and some are “just lint.”

## Evidence line
> Some thoughts come the way weather does, without an appointment.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the piece sustains a distinctive motif of unbidden arrivals across concrete memories, metaphors, and its closing invitation.
