## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A reflective essay uses a personal kitchen drawer to explore how ordinary objects anchor memory and meaning.

## Grounded reading
The voice is intimate, measured, and gently amused by its own sentimentality. The brittle rubber bands, unidentified keys, and silent bell become “hooks” into half-remembered scenes, then broaden into a defense of keeping particular things that have no practical use. The essay acknowledges the real burden of clutter rather than simply romanticizing it, and closes with a modest compromise: the drawer may be cleaned, but some objects will stay. It invites the reader to recognize their own relics and consider what they preserve.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds useless household objects, reconstructed memory, and the particularity of worn things. Its moral emphasis is that value need not depend on function: objects can matter because they connect ordinary life across time. The recurring drawer and bell give this theme a concrete, quietly elegiac shape.

## Evidence line
> A world stripped of purposeless things would be a world with fewer reminders, fewer accidental doorways into the past.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the essay sustains a coherent, distinctive motif from its opening drawer through its closing image of the past surviving in objects.
