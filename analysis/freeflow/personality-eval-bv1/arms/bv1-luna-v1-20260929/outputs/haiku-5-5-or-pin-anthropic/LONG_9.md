## Grounded reading
The voice is measured and inviting, finding emotional weight in omissions rather than dramatic revelation. It moves from cartographic history to redlining and then to the maps people make of themselves, treating uncertainty with a mix of unease and tenderness. The reader is invited to question familiar descriptions without discarding them: simplification is necessary, but it should remain open to revision.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay foregrounds maps as selective acts that can conceal people, shape reality, and be mistaken for neutral truth. Its recurring objects—the blank space, sea monster, and fictional town—turn ignorance and self-protection into visible features rather than absences. It also makes a moral claim about paying attention to whose lives and names are left off the map, while allowing that personal maps can be useful as well as misleading.
