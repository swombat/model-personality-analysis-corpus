## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW. The essay uses a distinctive first-person voice to turn a familiar object’s material history into reflection on dependence, thought, and mortality.

## Grounded reading
The voice is curious and measured, moving from the pencil’s construction to what its qualities suggest about living and thinking. Its pathos is gentle rather than dramatic: the writer finds comfort in dependence on strangers, tenderness in faded notes from a grandmother, and dignity in an object that shortens itself through use. The essay invites the reader to slow down and look closely at an ordinary thing, then consider how provisional marks, shared labor, and personal lives take shape.

## What the model chose to foreground
The model foregrounds the yellow pencil and its graphite, cedar, eraser, and gradual wear; the unseen labor and knowledge behind everyday objects; and the contrast between self-sufficiency and dependence. It also gives special weight to pencil marks as provisional, forgiving forms of thought, and to the pencil’s diminishment as a quiet image of service and mortality.

## Evidence line
> A pencil is a small, humbling lesson in dependence.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the essay’s coherent, distinctive reflections repeatedly connect concrete material details to humility, interdependence, and the willingness to revise.
