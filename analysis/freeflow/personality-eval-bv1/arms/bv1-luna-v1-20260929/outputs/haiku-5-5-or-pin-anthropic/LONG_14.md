## Grounded reading
The voice is contemplative and gently elegiac, finding pathos in ordinary survivals: a street sign outlasting its original reason, a child carrying a grandmother’s name, a family word disappearing with its last users. It invites the reader to treat names not as fixed definitions but as shared inheritances—sometimes tender, sometimes tied to colonial claims—and to notice the histories they carry without imagining that changing a label can erase the past.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay foregrounds names as traces of past lives and changing meanings, moving from street names and word origins to colonial renaming, family names, and private household language. Its moral emphasis is on attending to inherited histories and handling words carefully, while accepting that each generation receives and alters them.
