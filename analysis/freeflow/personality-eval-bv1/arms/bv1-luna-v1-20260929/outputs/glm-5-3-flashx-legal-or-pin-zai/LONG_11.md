## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical essay whose recurring images and carefully modulated emotional turns give it a distinctive voice beyond a general argument about mortality.

## Grounded reading
The voice is intimate and gently insistent, moving from the concrete scene of a father carrying a child to a wider meditation on how ordinary moments become endings without announcing themselves. Its pathos comes less from dramatic loss than from belated recognition: the missed significance of a Tuesday, a routine phone call, or a familiar room. The essay acknowledges the guilt and searching that grief can bring, then offers attention—not constant solemnity—as a modest way to meet ordinary life. It invites the reader to notice and reach back without pretending that awareness can prevent loss.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay foregrounds unmarked “last times”: childhood care, play, grandparents gathered together, a childhood home, calls with loved ones, and even fleeting encounters with strangers. Its repeated Tuesdays and ordinary objects make impermanence feel domestic rather than abstract. It also chooses a consoling but unresolved moral claim: we cannot know which moment is the last, and that ignorance is both painful and merciful; still, we can sometimes answer a call, look up, or reach back.
