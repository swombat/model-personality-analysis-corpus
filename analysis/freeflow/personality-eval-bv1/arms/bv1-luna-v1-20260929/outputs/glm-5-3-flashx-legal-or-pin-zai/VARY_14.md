## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A literary short story uses an invented language and a final speaker to explore loss through intimate domestic scenes.

## Grounded reading
The voice is elegiac but restrained, finding its deepest feeling in ordinary life rather than grand tragedy: an argument about salt, a kettle, rain, and a sister’s hand on Maren’s wrist. The story treats language as a lived place, not merely a store of words to be archived. Its invitation is to notice what disappears in small, almost unremarkable choices—and to care about what remains present while it can still be spoken.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds language loss, intergenerational pressure to assimilate, and the tension between preserving words and continuing to live them. It gives special weight to domestic objects and routines, especially the unresolved soup argument, as the setting where cultural loss becomes personal. Its moral emphasis is that recording a language matters, but does not replace speaking it with someone who shares its everyday uses.

## Evidence line
> People think a language dies when the last speaker dies.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the recurring images of rooms, kitchens, and words unsaid make the piece distinctive and coherent, with a sustained emphasis on ordinary intimacy as the measure of loss.
