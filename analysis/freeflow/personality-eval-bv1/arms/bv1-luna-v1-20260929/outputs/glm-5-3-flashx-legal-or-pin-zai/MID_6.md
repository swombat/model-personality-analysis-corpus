## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW. A lyrical personal meditation uses specific memories and a recurring image of unnoticed endings to explore grief and attention.

## Grounded reading
The voice is intimate, rueful, and gently self-deprecating: it admits impatience with a grandmother’s calls, then lets that regret deepen without turning it into a scolding. Its pathos comes from ordinary sensory details—the tomatoes, the yellow kitchen, the dog’s absent weight on the bed—rather than grand declarations. The piece invites the reader not to monitor every moment for its ending, but to show up more fully for the ordinary moments that may later matter.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds unnoticed endings in family life, childhood, friendship, home, speech, and the slow decline of a beloved dog. Its central moral claim is that not knowing which moment is last can be a kindness, while attention in the present is still worth choosing. The repeated ordinary objects and rituals—Sunday calls, soup, a phone that may stop ringing—make its reflections on grief concrete.

## Evidence line
> The call about the tomatoes is boring right up until it becomes sacred, and it can only be boring first.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the recurring motif, sensory specificity, and balanced movement from regret toward a practical ethic of attention make this a distinctive sample.
