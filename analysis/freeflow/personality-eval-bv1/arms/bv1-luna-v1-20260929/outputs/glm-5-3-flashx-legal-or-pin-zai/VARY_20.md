EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical first-person memory turns a childhood lesson in whistling into a meditation on grief, inheritance, and connection.

## Grounded reading
The voice is tender and attentive to small physical details: the porch boards give “like it had a pulse,” a first note sounds like a bicycle tire, and even the neighbor’s dog “said one word.” Grief is present but handled obliquely, through family euphemisms, pauses, and the remembered tune rather than grand declarations. The story invites the reader to feel how a practical gesture can carry affection across generations, while leaving room for uncertainty about whether the dead can hear us.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds a grandfather’s teaching, a tune passed along through family and strangers, and whistling as a way of checking that someone is still there. Its mood is elegiac but not bleak: play, humor, and sensory warmth coexist with loss. The closing return to the grandfather’s words makes the central claim—that connection can persist through a small repeated act—feel chosen and deliberate.
