## Grounded reading
The voice is intimate and assured, moving between historical detail and reflective address to the reader. Its central pathos is not a simple wish to return to a pre-electric past: it acknowledges light’s practical goods while mourning what constant illumination may have displaced—night as a shared environment, a different rhythm of sleep, and time for inward attention. The recurring image of night as a country makes the essay feel like an invitation to cross a border: spend time in real darkness, reconsider waking at 3 a.m., and recover some sense of scale and quiet.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds the history of artificial light, segmented sleep, folklore, starless skies, and the psychological meaning of darkness. It gives particular moral weight to lost forms of attention and to the possibility that modern discomfort with night has turned an old human interval into something to medicalize or fill with screens. Its qualified stance matters: it praises electric light while arguing that darkness remains valuable and worth choosing.
