## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical first-person story gives a station clerk’s cataloguing work an intimate, elegiac meaning.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant and quietly tender, treating lost objects as openings onto the lives around them. Its pathos comes from restrained acts of attention: the clerk imagines the story behind a child’s drawing, helps an old man recover a watch tied to his dead wife, and preserves a predecessor’s notebook. The reader is invited to see remembering—not simply recovering—as a kind of care, while the closing image of trains carrying people onward leaves that care tinged with transience.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds ordinary lost things—umbrellas, gloves, a shoe, a watch, a notebook—and the human stories that official categories cannot hold. It favors patient observation, private imagination, and small rule-bending acts of compassion over the efficiency of a digital replacement. Its recurring moral claim is that some things matter because someone remembers them, even when they cannot be returned.

## Evidence line
> But the objects arrive already talking, and someone has to listen.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the recurring objects, restrained compassion, and explicit contrast between inventory and memory make this a distinctive, coherent expressive choice.
