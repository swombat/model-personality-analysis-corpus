## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical, tightly structured essay turns a case for protecting darkness into a meditation on memory, imagination, and ecological loss.

## Grounded reading
The voice is elegiac but not merely nostalgic: it treats darkness as a living part of the world that modern life has narrowed, and connects that loss to human sleep, inwardness, myth, and animal navigation. Vivid comparisons—the Milky Way as a river, civilization as a permanently lit half-room—give the argument emotional force. The closing invitation is practical and contemplative: go somewhere dark, wait, and let the stars return to view.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds light pollution as both environmental damage and cultural forgetting. It links night to “first sleep,” private reflection, shared stories, and the lives of sea turtles, moths, and migrating birds, then resolves toward a small act of attention rather than a demand to abandon modern lighting.

## Evidence line
> The night is not gone.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the essay’s recurring imagery and deliberate movement from loss to invitation make its chosen mood and moral emphasis especially clear.
