It moves from a mother’s arms and childhood summers to Boa Sr, a vanished bird’s duet, and the moon’s distant future, giving small personal losses and immense disappearances a shared shape. The reader is invited to notice the ordinary present—not because it can be preserved, but because it will become a loss.

Its recurring objects—doors, photographs, songs, light, Tuesdays—make disappearance feel quiet and commonplace, while its moral claim is that awareness of impermanence can sharpen attention to what remains.
