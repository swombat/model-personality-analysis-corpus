## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW. This is a lyrical, carefully structured essay whose recurring images and emotional invitation make it more distinctive than a merely thesis-driven public essay.

## Grounded reading
The voice is learned but intimate, moving from sleep history and physiology toward a defense of quiet attention. Its pathos rests on a tension: the fourth watch can intensify fear and loneliness, yet it also holds births, work, animal life, and a different kind of perception. The essay does not simply romanticize sleeplessness; it explicitly acknowledges chronic insomnia as painful, then invites the reader to meet an occasional waking hour with curiosity rather than panic. Its closing image makes that invitation feel like entry into a neglected room, not an obligation to keep vigil.

## What the model chose to foreground
The model foregrounds the erased “watch” between two sleeps; the night workers, laboring mothers, monks, birds, and migrating sea life who remain awake; and the costs and comforts of electric light. It repeatedly links darkness with vulnerability and insight, making the night’s hidden population a counterweight to the modern idea that wakefulness at four a.m. is simply a defect. The moral emphasis is restorative: recognize what convenience has displaced, without denying what it has made possible.

## Evidence line
> The sea, in other words, never accepted the invention of the electric light.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — the sustained imagery of watches, thresholds, and hidden life, together with the humane distinction between insomnia and occasional wakefulness, makes this a notably revealing piece of writing.
