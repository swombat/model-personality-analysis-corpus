## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical science essay that turns precise astronomical explanation into a meditation on change, briefness, and connection.

## Grounded reading
The voice is lucid and patient, moving from measurements and physical mechanisms toward elegy without abandoning scientific detail. Its pathos comes from the contrast between immense timescales and our narrow chance to witness totality, then softens into a hopeful image of light sent to the Moon and returned. The reader is invited to feel both the loss in cosmic change and the tenderness of paying attention while something is still here.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay foregrounds the Moon’s measurable departure, the temporary match that makes total eclipses possible, and the long exchange of motion between Earth and Moon. Mirrors, photons, coral growth bands, tides, and the eclipse’s “sliver” turn distant time into things that can be counted or seen. Its moral emphasis is not that change can be stopped, but that impermanence can make the present feel more vivid—and that even across great distances, something can answer.

## Evidence line
> The Moon is 384,400 kilometers away and opens that distance by the width of a fingernail every year, and still, when we call, a little of our light comes back.
