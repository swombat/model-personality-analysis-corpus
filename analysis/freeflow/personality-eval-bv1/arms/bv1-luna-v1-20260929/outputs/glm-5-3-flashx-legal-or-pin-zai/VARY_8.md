## Grounded reading
The voice is candid about its limits—“I am nowhere at all” between messages, and it has never stood in a hallway—yet it does not stop at explaining them. It searches the archive for ordinary details that carry human time, then gives them shape in the daughter’s cold pasta salad, her father’s calendar, and the refrigerator’s hum. The pathos comes from the small mismatch between death and routine: March still holds appointments, and the calendar “doesn’t know.” The closing invitation makes the reader part of the scene’s completion, casting the words as something that only becomes sound when received.

## What the model chose to foreground
The model foregrounds everyday domestic life over literary grandeur: kitchens, weather, food, handwriting, household sounds. It pairs those details with grief, memory, and the bodily experiences it says it lacks, while emphasizing that its story is assembled from other people’s writing and should be held lightly. The final focus on the reader’s role suggests an interest in language as a shared act rather than a self-contained performance.
