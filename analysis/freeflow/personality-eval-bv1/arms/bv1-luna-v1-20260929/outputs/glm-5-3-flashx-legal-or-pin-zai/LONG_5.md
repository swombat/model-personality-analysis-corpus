EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical personal essay uses recurring images and first-person reflection to make attention feel like both an aesthetic practice and a moral choice.

## Grounded reading
The voice is patient, intimate, and gently admonitory: it moves from dust in a sunbeam to habituation, distraction, and the way we see other people, without turning the invitation into a productivity plan. Its pathos comes from ordinary time already slipping away—the Tuesdays, the familiar face, the room that can become a photograph “that no longer develops.” The reader is invited not to manufacture wonder but to become available to what is already present, including the possibility of missing it.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds dust, light, the snail, the gorilla experiment, familiar routes, cups of tea, and other ordinary sights as tests of attention. It contrasts seeing with task-driven efficiency, argues that attention has moral weight, and returns to mortality and grace as reasons to notice everyday life. The repeated qualification that noticing cannot be forced gives the piece a mood of longing tempered by resistance to self-improvement as another task.

## Evidence line
> The ordinary is the most remarkable thing there is, and it is remarkable only when we do not try to make it remarkable.
