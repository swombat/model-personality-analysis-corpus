## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical essay that uses historical and scientific material to reframe waking at night as a human inheritance rather than simply a failure of sleep.

## Grounded reading
The voice is intimate but assured, inviting the reader into a shared nocturnal experience before widening it into history, science, and city life. Its pathos comes from turning private anxiety into a loss of cultural permission: the awake person is not defective, but stranded in a once-inhabited interval. The recurring image of the watch as a country gives the essay warmth and coherence, while the ending offers the reader a gentler identity—as keeper of the watch, briefly keeping the sleeping world company.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds the three-o’clock waking, historical “first sleep” and “second sleep,” the disruption of electric light, and the people and animals who inhabit the night. It favors thresholds, quiet companionship, and the possibility that a distressing experience changes meaning when viewed through history. Its moral emphasis is not to dismiss insomnia, but to resist treating every ordinary night-waking as personal failure.

## Evidence line
