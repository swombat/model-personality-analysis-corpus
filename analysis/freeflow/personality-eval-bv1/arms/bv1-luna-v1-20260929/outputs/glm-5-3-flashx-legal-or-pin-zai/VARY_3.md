## Grounded reading
The voice is intimate and self-questioning, moving between concrete details—the cracked dictionary, the changing ink, the copied words—and lyrical interpretation. Its pathos comes from a life glimpsed only through marks, and from the narrator’s recognition that making a story about Ruth risks making Ruth into a mirror. The reader is invited to slow down and notice what a repeated act might mean, while the ending leaves that meaning open: the words become both Ruth’s trace and the narrator’s own voice.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds patient attention against skimming, and the small choices that might give shape to a life. It chooses words, underlines, handwriting, and a found book as its central objects; its mood is tender and elegiac, but not conclusive. The moral emphasis falls on both the value and danger of interpretation: the narrator is changed by Ruth’s marks, yet refuses to claim certainty about the woman behind them.
