## Grounded reading
The voice is assured, image-rich, and gently admonishing without becoming scolding. It invites the reader to see darkness not as deprivation but as access: the Milky Way, animal navigation, ancestral knowledge, and a sense of human scale have been obscured by habits that can be changed. Its pathos comes from pairing losses that are intimate and concrete—turtles, birds, fireflies—with the immense, patient presence of the galaxy.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay foregrounds artificial light as a veil between people and the universe, and darkness as a condition for recognition rather than emptiness. It links astronomy to practical knowledge, cultural memory, ecological harm, and experiences of awe, then resolves toward modest collective action: shielding lights, using less, and looking up again. The recurring contrast between what remains present and what people can no longer see is the piece’s central moral claim.
