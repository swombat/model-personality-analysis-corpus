## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A polished literary short story uses a paint stylist’s work to explore memory, grief, and how an ordinary domestic scene can outlast the person who made it.

## Grounded reading
The voice is lyrical and precise, attentive to the small sensory details that make memory feel lived: cold on the wrists, kettle steam, radio talk, a window left open. Its pathos comes from the narrator’s attempt to reproduce a mother’s kitchen in a paint formula, then realizing the remembered yellow belonged to a whole morning and relationship, not simply a wall. The story invites the reader to see naming and making as forms of care—and, finally, to accept that a loved one may be carried forward imperfectly, in strangers’ rooms and ordinary routines.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds colour, domestic light, a mother’s kitchen, and the tension between commercial naming and private meaning. It gives special weight to accidental details—sun, steam, an open window—and treats them as inseparable from memory. Its resolution turns the narrator’s grief into a quiet act of giving: the mother’s “colour of the morning” reaches other homes, even though they do not know her name.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the sustained sensory imagery and repeated movement from objects toward emotional meaning make this a distinctive, coherent sample, especially in its choice to resolve grief through an everyday act of sharing.
