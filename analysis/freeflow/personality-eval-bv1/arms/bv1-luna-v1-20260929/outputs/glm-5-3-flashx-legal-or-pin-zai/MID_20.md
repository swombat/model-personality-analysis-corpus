## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A literary short story built around an archivist whose collection of disappearing everyday sounds becomes a meditation on memory, loss, and what cannot be preserved.

## Grounded reading
The voice is patient and attentive to texture: sounds are described as physical traces of the machines, rooms, and relationships that made them. Its pathos gathers around a tension Marian cannot solve: she preserves what people forget to value, yet the sound that matters most to her is the one she failed to record. The ending invites the reader to listen not only for vanishing things, but for the living moment before it becomes an artifact.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds ordinary obsolete sounds—busy signals, card catalogs, classroom murmur—and treats their disappearance as a form of cultural and intimate loss. It also emphasizes the limits of archives: Marian can save a recording, but not recover her mother calling her in for dinner. The final image, of strangers settling into a theater together, gives that attention a quiet, communal warmth rather than resolving the grief.

## Evidence line

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the story’s distinctive attention to overlooked sounds recurs throughout, and its emotional resolution is closely tied to that choice.
