## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical personal essay whose sustained imagery and intimate memories make its meditation on attention more distinctive than a generic public-intellectual argument.

## Grounded reading
The voice is patient and observant, repeatedly inviting the reader to slow down and look again at ordinary things: a kitchen before dawn, a familiar street, flour falling through a sieve. Its pathos builds from the fear of unlived time and the pain of being overlooked, then turns toward the consolation of being steadily attended to, especially in hospitals and near death. The essay asks the reader not merely to agree that attention matters, but to practice it as a form of generosity toward other people and the world.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay foregrounds attention as a form of love and moral presence, using recurring images of dawn, streets, birds, water, and small acts of looking. It contrasts deliberate attention with distraction and “leaking,” but resists a simple anti-phone message by distinguishing purposive daydreaming from aimless consumption. Its emphasis on being seen—in friendship, family, care work, and the presence offered to the dying—makes attention both an everyday discipline and a way of affirming another person’s humanity.

## Evidence line
