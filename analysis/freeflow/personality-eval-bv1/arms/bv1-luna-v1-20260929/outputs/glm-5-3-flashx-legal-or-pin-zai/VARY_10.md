## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A quiet, carefully shaped story about an older man whose ritual of feeding crows becomes a way of being known and keeping company with grief.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant and gently wry, finding emotional weight in small, tangible things: a button, a tin, a key that fits no lock. Harold’s grief for Ruth is present without taking over the story; the crow’s visits give his long mornings a form of connection that does not demand ownership or constant care. The story invites the reader to value uncertain, modest bonds—and lets its tenderness rest in the unresolved question of what the gifts mean.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds retirement, bereavement, and the dignity of an unplanned routine. It makes the crow’s odd offerings and Harold’s ledger into objects of attention, while repeatedly contrasting companionship with possession: the crow “did not need him.” Its moral emphasis is on the comfort of being recognized without being needed, and on allowing wonder to remain unexplained.

## Evidence line
> It is being known by a bird.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the recurring attention to ordinary objects, restrained grief, and bonds that leave room for uncertainty gives this sample a distinct and coherent emotional signature.
