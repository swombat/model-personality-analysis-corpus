## Grounded reading
The voice is observant, intimate, and carefully lyrical, moving from sleepless walks to a broader attention to the people whose work sustains the daytime world. Its pathos comes from holding two kinds of night together: the private loneliness of insomnia and the quiet dignity of workers doing unseen labor. The essay resists making either into a neat metaphor, and its invitation to the reader is practical and gentle: go out, notice the people and places usually overlooked, and witness without turning them into content.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds the liminal hours between two and five in the morning; the sensory quiet of the city; workers such as nurses, drivers, and fish-market unloaders; and the small exchanges of courtesy among people awake at night. It repeatedly links attention to an ethic of dignity that does not depend on recognition, while treating insomnia as both difficult and a route to noticing the ordinary more clearly.
