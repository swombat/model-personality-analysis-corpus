EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A literary science essay that turns lunar physics into a sustained meditation on departure, time, and the value of noticing.

## Grounded reading
The voice is lucid and patient, moving from precise explanation to elegy without abandoning the science. Its pathos comes from scale: a farewell measured in centimeters and billions of years, and a rare celestial spectacle whose final occurrence can be anticipated but not witnessed by us. The repeated image of the moon leaving gives the piece emotional continuity, while its careful refusal to treat the moon as a human lover keeps the metaphor from becoming too easy. The reader is invited not just to understand the mechanics, but to go see an eclipse and feel briefly present in a vanishing arrangement of sun, moon, and Earth.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds the moon’s recession, tides as both physical mechanism and “farewell,” the temporary fit that makes total solar eclipses possible, and the future end of that spectacle. It also selects human attention and record-keeping as a counterweight to cosmic impermanence: mirrors, ancient eclipse calculations, and observers who know a last time is coming. The mood is awed and wistful, but not despairing; its closing appeal is to notice the present while it lasts.
