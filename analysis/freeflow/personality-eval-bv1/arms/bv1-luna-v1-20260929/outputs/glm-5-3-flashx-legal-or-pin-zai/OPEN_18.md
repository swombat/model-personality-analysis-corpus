## Grounded reading
The voice is intimate and assured, moving between archival detail and the felt experience of lying awake. Its pathos comes from recasting a dreaded symptom as a possible inheritance: the watch becomes a lost room of human life, not merely an old sleep schedule. The closing invitation is modest and practical—to meet wakefulness without immediately treating it as failure—and the essay leaves room for the alarm and the difficulty to remain real.

## What the model chose to foreground
It foregrounds the night as a place people once inhabited, the “third” state between waking and sleeping, and electric light as a force that narrowed human experience. Its moral emphasis is gentle rather than accusatory: modern expectations about sleep may turn some ordinary waking into distress, and a different response may sometimes be possible.
