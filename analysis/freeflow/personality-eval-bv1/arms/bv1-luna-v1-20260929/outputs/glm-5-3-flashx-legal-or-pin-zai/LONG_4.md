## Grounded reading
The voice is intimate and contemplative, moving from the practical discomfort of sleeplessness toward wonder at how much of the world—and of other people—remains unseen. Its pathos comes from the narrator’s sense of being overwhelmed by countless private lives, but the piece does not resolve that feeling into easy uplift: the resulting patience is tentative, and the narrator admits it “does not always make me kinder.” The reader is invited to slow down, look past the public “summary” of the day, and accept that another person’s interior life is real even when it remains inaccessible.

## What the model chose to foreground
The piece foregrounds the hour between one and four in the morning, insomnia, empty streets, lit windows, and the city as an archive of lives. Its recurring moral emphasis is that other people are whole, vivid worlds rather than abstractions, and that recognizing this may encourage humility without guaranteeing kindness. It also gives solitude value as an unshared, unoptimized experience, preserving the mood of quiet attention rather than turning the night walk into a simple lesson.
