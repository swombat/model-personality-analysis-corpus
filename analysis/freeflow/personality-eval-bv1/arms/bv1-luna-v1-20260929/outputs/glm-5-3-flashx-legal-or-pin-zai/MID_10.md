## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A carefully shaped first-person story uses piano tuning to explore memory, grief, and the quiet forms of connection that outlast everyday contact.

## Grounded reading
The voice is patient and observant, with emotion held in check until the technical details begin to carry it: a metronome left mid-piece, a piano gone sharp in grief, an empty room retaining the instrument’s outline. The narrator’s regret over missing Vera’s return is small and unshowy, which makes the accumulated years feel more affecting than a direct confession would. The story invites the reader to listen for what remains in objects and relationships, and to understand sympathy not as perfect agreement but as attention to what is actually there.

## What the model chose to foreground
The model foregrounds the craft of tuning, especially beats, imperfect strings, and sympathetic vibration, then makes those physical phenomena bear the weight of family history and loss. It selects ordinary traces—the pencils, sheet music, wedding invitation, dust—as evidence of lives the narrator has only partly known. Its moral emphasis is on listening rather than judging, and on meeting an imperfect instrument, person, or moment halfway.
