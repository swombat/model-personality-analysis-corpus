## Grounded reading
The voice is lyrical and quietly elegiac, drawn to the idea that modern life can erase not only customs but whole forms of consciousness. The essay’s pathos gathers around the “watch”: a once-sanctioned interval of wakefulness that has become something people treat as failure. It avoids simple nostalgia, acknowledging the dangers of old nights while insisting that loss deserves attention. The invitation is to meet a nighttime waking with curiosity rather than self-criticism, and perhaps to leave the phone untouched long enough to feel the hour differently.

## What the model chose to foreground
The model foregrounds first and second sleep, darkness, electric light, and the “room in the mind” that may have disappeared with older rhythms of life. It makes the transformation from customary wakefulness to medicalized insomnia its central moral contrast, while resisting a return-to-the-past argument. The recurring images of a painted-over door, a walled-up room, and a country that is difficult to enter give the essay’s interest in lost inner experience a concrete, melancholic shape.
