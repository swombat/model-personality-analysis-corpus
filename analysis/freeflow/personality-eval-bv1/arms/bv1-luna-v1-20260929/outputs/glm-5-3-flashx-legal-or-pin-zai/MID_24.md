## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW. A lyrical, closely composed essay turns light pollution into a vivid meditation on what modern life has erased.

## Grounded reading
The voice is assured and elegiac, but not simply nostalgic: it acknowledges the safety and convenience of artificial light while asking readers to recognize darkness as habitat, bodily condition, and shared inheritance. Its pathos gathers around concrete losses—the hatchlings crawling inland, birds circling lit towers, children who have never seen a star—before widening into a sense of cosmic scale. The closing invitation is direct and physical: go somewhere unlit, turn from the windows, and look up.

## What the model chose to foreground
The model foregrounds the night sky and the many forms of life and human meaning that depend on darkness: sleep, navigation, migration, memory, wonder, and consolation. It repeatedly frames darkness not as emptiness or defect but as a living environment and a “country” humans have abandoned. The moral claim is measured rather than absolutist: preserve darkness as a neighbor without rejecting the benefits of light.

## Evidence line
> It has been waiting the whole time, the way dark things wait — without moving, without dimming, without asking to be seen.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the essay’s distinctive “unlit country” metaphor recurs across history, biology, and personal appeal, giving its emotional and moral concerns unusually strong coherence.
