## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW. A lyrical, personal essay turns an everyday observation into a direct invitation to notice what is small and easily overlooked.

## Grounded reading
The voice is tender and assured, moving from a quiet image—the old man touching a flower—to broad claims about attention, meaning, and how lives are shaped. Its pathos comes from the possibility of age and regret, but the closing wish is gentle rather than scolding: notice one thing today and let it matter. The reader is invited to slow down and treat ordinary details as worthy of care.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds small acts of attention: grocery-store flowers, household sounds, light through leaves, a brief message to a friend. It contrasts these unmeasured moments with a culture focused on scale, productivity, and visibility, and gives attention a moral weight—as care, resistance, and the material from which a life is made. The recurring images of libraries, coastlines, and waves make that claim feel cumulative and intimate.

## Evidence line
