## Grounded reading
The voice is warm and inviting, with a gentle wonder rather than anxiety about what remains unknown. It moves from a memorable image—sea monsters at the edges of maps—to intimate examples of insomnia, nostalgia, and anticipatory loss. Its central reassurance is that putting an unnamed feeling into words can make it shareable without making the mystery disappear. The closing question invites the reader to offer their own “dragons,” while also making room to change the subject.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds emotional interiority as a new frontier: feelings that resist easy explanation, the language people find for them, and the relief of discovering that others recognize them. It also gives special value to poets, therapists, grandmothers, and children as explorers of that territory. The repeated map-and-dragon imagery makes the act of describing private experience feel both exploratory and communal.
