## Grounded reading
The voice is intimate and gently elegiac, moving from playful awareness of the “thousand words” to a story that makes room for grief without trying to resolve it. Ellis’s garage, the broken radios, and the narrator’s remembered belongings give the sentiment concrete weight. The closing invitation asks the reader to consider their own attachments, offering companionship rather than advice.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds objects kept because they hold memories: radios, a child’s CD, a dead watch, a frayed charger. Its moral center is that broken or obsolete things may deserve witness, not repair or disposal. The recurring image of listening through static makes writing itself an act of attending to what remains human.
