## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW. A lyrical, highly structured essay uses a recurring library metaphor to turn unfinished parts of life into an invitation toward self-compassion and deliberate choice.

## Grounded reading
The voice is intimate and gently persuasive, speaking to the reader as a fellow work in progress rather than an authority delivering a lesson. Its pathos comes from recognizable unfinished things—the unrehearsed apology, the dusty guitar, the door behind which sit the risks never taken—and from treating regret as something that can be revisited without shame. The essay also resists easy reassurance: it distinguishes dormant hopes from burdensome obligations and argues that some things deserve conscious abandonment. Its invitation is to look at one’s unfinished life with more kindness, then decide what to reopen and what to release.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds incompletion as both a source of mental weight and a condition of possibility. It repeatedly returns to books, shelves, drafts, open loops, cracks, and blank pages, using those objects to connect personal regret with learning, creativity, love, and growth. Its moral emphasis is not “finish everything,” but tend what still matters and release what no longer does.

## Evidence line
> The question is not "How do I finish everything?" That way lies madness and burnout.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the recurring imagery, clear emotional arc, and specific distinction between unfinished things worth tending and those worth abandoning make this a strongly shaped sample.
