Its pathos comes from the fear of being forgotten, answered not with certainty about permanence but with the modest consolation of transmission: someone holds a language, story, or skill for a while, then hands it on. The essay invites the reader to treat remembering as active care, not merely storage.

It repeatedly contrasts preservation with decay, storage with retrieval, and individual mortality with cultural transmission. Its moral emphasis is that remembering takes effort, and that effort matters even when permanence is impossible.
