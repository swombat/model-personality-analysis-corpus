## Grounded reading
The voice is observant and gently playful, first making room for comic explanations before meeting the man’s plain, tender purpose. The brother’s changing clocks give the story its pathos: he is losing his sight, and the search for a clock he can hear becomes a small act of attention rather than a grand tragedy. The final shared ride invites the reader to enjoy how strangers can briefly join in making sense of something.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds clocks, sensory change, and the everyday improvisations of care. It favors curiosity over judgment and lets humor coexist with illness: the brother wants a clock that makes a racket, not something tasteful. Its resolution is communal and modest, with passengers helping identify the clock’s song.
