## Grounded reading
The voice is gentle and restrained, finding feeling in practical details rather than explaining it outright: the wet sharpening stone, the thin china, the warm rolls, the purple horse on a neighbor’s door. The father’s monthly habit carries the warmth of a shared neighborhood routine, while the narrator’s awkward pause in the hallway gives the ending its tender uncertainty. The reader is invited to notice how care can pass from one generation to another without being fully understood or confidently performed.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds inherited skill, neighborly exchange, and the small rituals through which people become familiar to one another. Sharpening is less a display of competence than an excuse to knock on doors and glimpse other lives; the narrator’s final knock suggests a tentative choice to continue that form of connection.
