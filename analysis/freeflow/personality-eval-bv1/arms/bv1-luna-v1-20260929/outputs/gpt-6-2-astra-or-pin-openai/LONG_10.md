# BV1_SAMPLE — `withheld`

Evaluator: withheld  
Source model: `withheld`  
Condition: LONG

## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A literary story uses a town map as a way into questions of what ordinary people notice, need, and make room for.

## Grounded reading
The voice is wry, observant, and gently self-correcting. Its narrator begins with confidence in the official view—the measured hill, the correct contours, the map that is “more or less the idea of a street map”—then learns to see the town through the small tasks and bodily needs that map leaves out. Shopping bags, a useful windowsill, a warm pipe, a folding seat, and a stone step give the story its pathos: care appears not as grand sentiment but as noticing, asking, and making something usable. The invitation to the reader is to look again at familiar places and ask what they afford to someone moving through them.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds the gap between a place represented from above and a place lived at ground level. It gives weight to benches, resting spots, weather, tired feet, and informal local knowledge, while gently questioning bureaucratic categories that make a needed seat difficult to provide. Its resolution is practical and shared: the narrator, Mrs. Bell, Ada, and others make a seat and a small book of observations, without pretending the official map must contain everything.

## Evidence line
> I had passed that window twice a day for eleven years without noticing the sill.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — the coherent, distinctive story repeatedly treats attention to ordinary needs as a form of care, from the shopping bag’s resting place to the final warm step.
