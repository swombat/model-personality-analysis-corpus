## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A carefully shaped first-person literary story follows a surveyor whose measurements keep opening onto the human meanings they cannot contain.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant, dryly funny, and tender without insisting on sentiment. Its pathos gathers through small, concrete losses: trousers folded after a husband’s death, a tooth found in a swimming pool, a geranium outliving Kit, and the narrator’s inability to recover her voice. Measuring is both his trade and the story’s governing metaphor: precise figures coexist with personal distances that resist precision. The repeated notebooks invite the reader to notice how places become meaningful through the people who use them, while the ending passes that attention to Martin, whose written measurement is simply that they sat until their feet got wet.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds ordinary objects that carry memory—the cabbage, teaspoon, pool tooth, geranium, drain—and the gap between official measurements and lived experience. It gives weight to grief, local community, and small acts of companionship, while resisting a neat triumph: the baths close despite the notebook, and the narrator’s memories of Kit remain incomplete. The recurring attention to what a place means to different people makes listening, rather than persuasion or rescue, the story’s quiet moral center.
