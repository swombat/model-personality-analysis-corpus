## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A quiet, carefully shaped family story uses a stopped town clock and a father learning to swim to explore work, aging, and belated discovery.

## Grounded reading
The voice is wry and tender, letting humor—two icing hands eaten off a cake, a dachshund that never jumps, an unexpectedly accurate splash—keep the story from becoming solemn. Its pathos gathers around the narrator’s realization that years of shared life have left much about the father unknown. The reader is invited to notice how a person’s identity can outlast a job, and how affection may arrive through small, newly observed details rather than grand declarations.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story centers on invisible routine and what happens when it ends: the father’s thirty-one years winding the clock, the town’s awkward response to its stopping, and his own new routine at the pool. It gives moral weight to trying something late in life and to the narrator’s belated curiosity, while favoring modest particulars—the bakery window, battered shins, a penciled note—over public ideas of legacy.

## Evidence line
> It struck me that I knew very little about what he had been doing all those years.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The sample’s distinctive humor, recurring focus on ordinary acts of care, and emotionally earned shift from public legacy to private attention make these choices unusually legible.
