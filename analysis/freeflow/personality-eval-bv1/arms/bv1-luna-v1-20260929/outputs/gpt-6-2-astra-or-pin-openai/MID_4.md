## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A carefully shaped literary short story about a family’s handmade model town and the people who reclaim pieces of it.

## Grounded reading
The voice is tender and wry, attentive to the odd practical details through which affection becomes visible: a roof darkened with orange peel, a mayor who cannot leave his post, a matchstick keeping an office window open. Grief arrives without grand declarations; it is carried by the mother’s request for her basement back and by the small, unexpectedly personal claims people make on the town. The final exchange invites the reader to hold both truths at once: the model cannot preserve the original smell, but memory can make it present again.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds the imperfect translation of lived experience into art, the father’s precise but humane rules, and the way ordinary places gather private histories. Its recurring objects—the orange, the miniature buildings, the swimming pool, the station—make inheritance feel material and communal. The moral emphasis is less on preserving the town intact than on letting people carry away what mattered to them.
