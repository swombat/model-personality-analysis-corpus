## Grounded reading
The voice is gently deadpan: the impossible moon is handled with the calm procedures of a library, from checking the boy’s card to finding a computer category. The story’s tenderness stays modest and practical. The boy wants a nightlight for his sister; Mara finds him a pear-shaped lamp, while the moon’s ascent is left to its own strange logic. The ending invites the reader to keep a little room for questions that cannot be filed neatly away.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds libraries, rules, borrowing, lost property, and the moon as an object that can be returned. Its mood is playful and kindly rather than grandly fantastical; its moral emphasis is on generosity toward a child’s imaginative wish and on not erasing a “good question.”
