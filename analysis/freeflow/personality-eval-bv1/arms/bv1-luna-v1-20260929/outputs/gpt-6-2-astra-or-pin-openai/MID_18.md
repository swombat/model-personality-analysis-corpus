## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW. A quiet, crafted meditation uses lost objects and family keepsakes to connect ordinary details with memory and attachment.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant, gently funny, and emotionally restrained. Its tenderness shows in small acts—turning Daniel’s sweatshirt collar out of sight, remembering the grater’s squeak, rubbing the scarf’s shiny patch—rather than in declarations of grief or sentiment. The reader is invited to recognize how objects carry private histories, and how care can mean preserving a thing without making it solemn.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample repeatedly foregrounds lost and kept objects: pool belongings, six cheese graters, and a scarf recovered from a train. It attends to the imperfect clues that make things identifiable—the sock “that hurts,” a name traced twice, a burn that is really a shiny patch—and to the practical, sometimes awkward rituals of deciding what deserves to be kept. Its moral emphasis is modest: attachment need not be grand to matter.

## Evidence line
> This strikes me as about the right amount of ceremony.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The recurring object-and-memory structure, dry humor, and resistance to grand ceremony make for a distinctive, coherent voice, though those qualities are closely woven into this particular narrative.
