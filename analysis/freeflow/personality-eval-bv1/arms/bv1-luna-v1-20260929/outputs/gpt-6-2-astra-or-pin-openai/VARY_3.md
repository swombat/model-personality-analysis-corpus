## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION. A quietly comic, carefully shaped short story turns a house appraisal into a story about grief and the small ways people remain connected.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant and gently wry, finding affection in practical oddities: mislabeled switches, saved shoe covers, a plastic rock with “ROCK” stamped on it. Grief arrives through what the narrator notices and remembers rather than through declaration—the removed door that let her father hear her sick mother, and the “old way” the kitchen sounds occupied. The boy’s visit gives the story a modest, moving exchange: he receives the pump, then sends back a piece of the ball. The ending invites the reader to imagine a pattern of care in the father’s miscellaneous keepsakes, without explaining it outright.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds inherited habits, objects that acquire meaning through use, and the persistence of ordinary relationships after death. Its humor is affectionate rather than mocking; its emotional center is the movement of small things between the father, daughter, and boy.

## Evidence line
> The little click made the kitchen briefly sound occupied in the old way: somebody finishing one small job before beginning another.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the story’s coherent, distinctive attention to practical objects and understated acts of care makes those choices especially clear in this sample.
