## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW. A literary domestic vignette uses recurring objects and quiet humor to explore family habits, change, and the limits of what the narrator can know.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant and gently comic, finding feeling in precise domestic details rather than announcing it. The clock’s shifting position and the fading circles on the wall give shape to time passing, while the narrator’s forgotten question and reluctance to ask about June’s plaster foot register a tender awareness that a familiar place now belongs to other people. The reader is invited to notice the small oddities and accommodations through which people share a life, and to accept that not everything needs to be explained.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds retirement, household negotiation, objects that retain traces of their former places, and the handoff of a family home to its next occupants. Clocks, wall marks, curtains, records, a sewing room, pears, and a plaster foot carry the mood: affectionate, lightly amused, and attentive to both continuity and change. Its moral emphasis is quiet rather than declared—care can be practical and particular, while another person’s life remains partly private.

## Evidence line
