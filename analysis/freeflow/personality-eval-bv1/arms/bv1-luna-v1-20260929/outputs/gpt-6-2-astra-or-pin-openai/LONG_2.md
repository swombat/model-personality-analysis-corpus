## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A tightly shaped literary narrative follows a daughter’s visit to her father after his cataract surgery.

## Grounded reading
The voice is wry and observant, finding humor in small specifics—the dolphins’ “private satisfaction,” a washer kept “in case I need a washer”—without using jokes to dodge feeling. Grief for the mother stays understated, emerging through a pink building, a remembered phone call, and the father’s regret about what he may not have asked. The story invites the reader to notice how care can take the form of practical details and shared attention, and how connection can begin when neither person knows what comes next.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds sight and the limits of seeing: the father’s newly clear vision, the daughter’s attention to what she had overlooked, and the uncertainty of what the mother could see near the end of her life. Ordinary objects—shower curtains, oranges, a brass washer, a miniature house—carry affection and memory. Its moral emphasis is gentle rather than declarative: paying attention, making room for another person’s interests, and staying curious can bring people closer.
