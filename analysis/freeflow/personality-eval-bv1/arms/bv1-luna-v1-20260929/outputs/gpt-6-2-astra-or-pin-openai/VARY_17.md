## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A quiet, finely observed family narrative uses mending and pocketed objects to explore how care passes between generations.

## Grounded reading
The voice is tender and wry without becoming sentimental. Its emotional weight comes through small acts: a mother pointing to the seam ripper while on the phone, a grandmother polishing a forgotten chestnut, a daughter choosing yellow thread for a black coat. The narrator is drawn to what gets carried, lost, kept, and handed on, and finds meaning in ordinary changes of mind—“I didn’t then”—rather than in grand declarations. The reader is invited to notice that repair need not hide its history: the final yellow pucker remains visible, and the stitches do not have to be perfect to hold.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds inherited habits of care, private pockets and their small contents, the imperfect recovery of what has been discarded, and the way memory attaches to ordinary objects. Its mood is affectionate and lightly comic, especially around the lost stones and the stone falling through a hole in the narrator’s pocket. The moral emphasis is gentle: attention matters, people change their minds, and a visible repair can be enough.

## Evidence line
> I don’t know why I’d expected to find the chestnut.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The recurring objects and the closing return to mending make this a coherent, distinctive expression of care through material detail.
