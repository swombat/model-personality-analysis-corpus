## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A literary family vignette uses a recurring household object to explore inheritance, loss, and the open-ended usefulness of things.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant, gently comic, and affectionate without making grief explicit or grand. Its emotional force comes through practical details—the rigid elastic bands, the frog-hat fitting, the candle that drowns in a clementine peel—and the way ordinary acts of sorting become ways of staying in relation to the mother. The reader is invited to see “usefulness” less as a property of an object than as a willingness to keep looking, imagining, and making room for what comes next.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds a mother’s habits of saving and improvising, the siblings’ shared memories, and small objects that gather new meanings across generations. Its moral emphasis is on resisting premature judgments: a thing may have another use, a person may need company, and an imperfect solution—a ribbon tied around a toy train—can still bring delight. The mood remains tender and lightly playful, with loss carried through domestic ritual rather than directly explained.

## Evidence line
