## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION. A literary short story with a sustained narrator’s voice and a carefully developed arc around place-names, memory, and a father’s changing recollections.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant, dryly funny, and tender without becoming sentimental. Its pathos gathers through small acts of attention: a father correcting a detail on a card, a daughter choosing to sit with him over old routes, and a private memory of his mother emerging through the story of a kitchen. The narrator’s practical work of collecting names becomes an invitation to notice how ordinary language holds relationships and local history, even when the official record cannot hold them.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds unofficial names and the people who use them; the gap between maps and lived experience; memory, aging, and family reticence; and the ethics of recording what people say. Its recurring objects—the index cards, route cards, canvas pocket, and pencils—make preservation feel provisional and communal rather than authoritative. The ending returns attention to an everyday scene, suggesting that a place’s meaning can reside in a name as long as the people using it.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — the recurring attention to vernacular language, memory, and modest acts of care is distinctive and sustained throughout this coherent story.
