## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A compact literary vignette turns a found leaf into a quiet story about memory, loss, and what remains unsaid.

## Grounded reading
The voice is restrained and observant, letting small physical details carry emotional weight: the wet cyclist, the fading leaf, the kitchen window. Its pathos comes through understatement rather than explanation, especially in the exchange about the father and the narrator’s wait for more. The reader is invited to sit with an ambiguous keepsake without demanding that it resolve into a complete account.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds forgotten objects kept between the pages of ordinary life, and the difficulty of sorting them into neat categories. It links a rainy Thursday, a leaf, and a family’s hard decisions, while leaving the father’s absence largely unexplained. The closing “Lots of people had Thursdays” preserves both the intimacy of the memory and the mother’s refusal—or inability—to make it wholly personal.

## Evidence line
> My mother, who had been making hard decisions all morning about lamps and dishes, held it up against the kitchen window.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — the carefully repeated attention to overlooked objects and the understated emotional turn make this sample distinctive and coherent.
