## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A quiet literary short story builds around a family’s attempt to preserve childhood height marks as they leave their old house.

## Grounded reading
The voice is restrained and observant, letting practical gestures carry the emotion: the pry bar, the duck-handled brush, the orange eaten over the sink. Its pathos comes from the family’s imperfect care for one another—especially the mother’s admission that she quietly adjusted the measurements when one child was having a bad year. The ending offers a gentle invitation to see inheritance not as a possession to take away, but as a small act of welcome that can be repeated for someone new.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds family memory, sibling rivalry, and the quiet accommodations parents make for children in pain. Objects hold that history: the marked doorframe, the concealed strip of wallpaper, the brush, and the mother’s pencil. The final gesture extends the family ritual to the next child, suggesting that what is passed on is less the house itself than the habit of making room for someone.

## Evidence line
> What they did not sell by the foot was the afternoon I overtook him.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the story’s recurring objects, carefully restrained emotion, and resonant final gesture make its chosen literary sensibility distinct in this sample.
