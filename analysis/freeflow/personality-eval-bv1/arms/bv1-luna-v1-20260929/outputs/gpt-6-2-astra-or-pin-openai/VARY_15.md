Evaluator: withheld

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A literary short story built around a mother’s memory loss, family rituals, and the small acts that keep affection present.

## Grounded reading
The voice is intimate, observant, and quietly funny, letting grief and tenderness surface through practical details rather than explanation. The narrator’s attention to the pencil, the old rice cooker, the cat note, and the doughnuts gives ordinary objects the weight of shared history. The story invites the reader to notice that care can run both ways: the mother is recording what she might lose, while the narrator discovers that their time together is still adding something to the record.

## What the model chose to foreground
The piece foregrounds memory, aging, and the uneven ways family members know one another. Food and household objects—rice, eggs, doughnuts, index cards, a saucer—become vessels for inherited habits and intimate history. Its moral emphasis is modest rather than grand: making an effort for oneself, and treating time together as something still being made.

## Evidence line
> I washed it just for me.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — the recurring domestic details and the understated turn from loss toward shared creation make this a distinctive, carefully shaped piece rather than a generic sentimental story.
