## Grounded reading
The voice is observant, warm, and gently comic, finding emotional weight in practical details: a scraper and apron, a sign rewritten as “THINGS,” a loose note trembling in wooden trays. Its pathos is quiet rather than tragic; the aunt’s death is felt through her notebook and the places she made for other people. The narrator invites the reader to notice how a room can help people feel less alone, even from a seat where nobody can see them.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds attentive looking and listening, humble acts of hospitality, lost objects, and the gradual creation of community from an underused place. Its recurring attention to seats, signs, sounds, and small things makes the moral claim understated but clear: belonging can begin with making room for others, and ordinary details can carry that welcome forward.
