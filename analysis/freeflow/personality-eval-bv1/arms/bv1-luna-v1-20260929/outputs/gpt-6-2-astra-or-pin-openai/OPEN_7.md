## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A quiet miniature story turns an ordinary lost mitten into a small meditation on care and connection.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant and gently wry, attentive to the human gestures around an object as much as to the object itself. Its tenderness stays modest: the narrator imagines anonymous people protecting the mitten, then lets the girl’s cheek-pat deliver the emotional turn without spelling out its meaning. The reader is invited to notice how small acts of care can link strangers—and how a child can make a loss feel newly useful.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds a red mitten, repeated acts of tending, and the quiet continuity between unknown passersby, a parent, and a child. Its moral emphasis is on care expressed through attention rather than grand declarations; its mood is affectionate, lightly playful, and unsentimental.

## Evidence line
> I like that there are at least two people in this story besides the child: the one who lifted the mitten out of the slush, and the one who stopped to brush it clean.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The vignette is coherent and distinctive in its attention to small, unclaimed acts of kindness, though that evidence is concentrated in one compact scene.
