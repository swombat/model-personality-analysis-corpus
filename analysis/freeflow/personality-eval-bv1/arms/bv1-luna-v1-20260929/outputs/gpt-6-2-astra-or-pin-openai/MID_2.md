# BV1_SAMPLE — `withheld`

Evaluator: withheld  
Source model: `withheld`  
Condition: MID

## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A quietly comic, carefully shaped story turns an inherited drawer into a meditation on grief, memory, and the meanings objects acquire through use.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant, gently funny, and emotionally restrained. It finds feeling in small physical details—the upside-down brass fish, the “GOOD BAG,” keys that feel like appointments—without insisting that each object unlock a grand revelation. Grief appears as curiosity and ongoing care: the narrator keeps the drawer, shares its contents, and accepts that some of its history remains unknown. The story invites the reader to value the ordinary ways family memories persist, while respecting the parts of another person’s life that cannot be recovered.

## What the model chose to foreground
The piece foregrounds inheritance not as a transfer of valuable possessions but as an accumulation of modest, ambiguous things. It gives weight to practical kindness and new connections, from finding Salma a washer to passing along the pressure-cooker instructions. Its moral emphasis is on living with uncertainty and leaving room for private experience rather than forcing a neat story onto the dead.

## Evidence line
> There is a courtesy in leaving him there with his private reaction, rather than furnishing him with the one that makes the best anecdote.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The sustained, distinctive attention to humble objects, unresolved meanings, and humane restraint makes these choices unusually clear throughout the piece.
