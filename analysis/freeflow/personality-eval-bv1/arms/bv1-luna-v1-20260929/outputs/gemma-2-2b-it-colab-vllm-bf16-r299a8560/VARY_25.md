## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical first-person meditation turns an attic’s neglected objects into a story about memory and self-redefinition.

## Grounded reading
The voice is dreamy and intimate, lingering over tactile details—the smoothed teacup rim, faded sunflowers, lavender, strawberries and vanilla—to make the past feel sensuous but unreachable. Its pathos comes from imagining a former occupant while recognizing that the narrator is inventing meanings for her things. The reader is invited to do the same: treat imagination not as theft, but as a way to make a life from fragments.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds forgotten domestic objects, an absent girl, dreams, and the narrator’s responsibility for turning traces into a story. Its mood moves from dusty melancholy toward imaginative renewal; the final emphasis is less on recovering the past accurately than on weaving it into a new identity.

## Evidence line
> The house was my exile, yet novel.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the recurring objects and explicit shift from observing forgotten things to making them part of the self give the sample a coherent, distinctive imaginative preoccupation.
