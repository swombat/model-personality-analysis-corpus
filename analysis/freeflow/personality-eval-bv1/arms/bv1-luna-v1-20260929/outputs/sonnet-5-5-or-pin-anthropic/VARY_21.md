GENRE_FICTION — A lyrical, gently magical short story about an obsolete civic ritual becoming a practice of communal attention.

## Grounded reading
The voice is warm, patient, and wistful, finding dignity in a task that appears useless. Teodor’s nightly walk turns maintenance into a form of presence: first for the lamps, then for neighbors who begin to show up and notice one another. The story invites the reader to treat care as something that can matter even when it is not technically needed. Its magic stays quiet and metaphorical, resolved not by restoring the old lamps but by preserving the shared ritual around them.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds overlooked labor, inherited custom, and the difference between efficiency and meaning. Its recurring objects—the brass pole, the lamps, and the gray control box—carry a moral emphasis on attention: things and people seem to flourish when someone keeps showing up for them. The ending favors communal continuity over technological nostalgia; the new system works, but the neighbors keep walking together.
