## Grounded reading
The voice is patient, admiring, and quietly elegiac. It moves from the craft of lenses and towers to the less visible labor of keepers, then uses the lighthouse to think about care that does not demand recognition. The pathos is restrained: vanished keepers, dangerous work, and ships saved without anyone knowing are treated with tenderness rather than melodrama. The reader is invited to value reliable, modest help—to be clear about danger without taking over another person’s choices.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay selects lighthouses as both engineering achievements and moral objects: glass, clockwork, stone, and human attention working for strangers at a distance. It repeatedly favors constancy over novelty, practical help over control, and unrecognized service over public credit. Its central image is not simply the beacon, but a system of care whose success may be invisible because disaster never happens.
