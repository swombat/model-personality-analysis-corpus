## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A carefully shaped literary story turns a widow’s habit of measuring her house into a way to recover both her husband’s presence and her own.

## Grounded reading
The voice is patient and tender, attentive to small physical details: a dented tape measure, worn floorboards, the light on a notebook. Its pathos comes from letting grief appear first as practical activity, then as an “exact map of absence,” before the measurements reveal a shared life rather than only a loss. The story invites the reader to notice how ordinary routes and repeated gestures can hold intimacy—and how a person left behind may find a way to belong to the same home again.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds domestic space, bereavement, memory, and the material traces of long companionship. Measuring becomes both a form of mourning and a means of recognizing Marguerite’s own life within the marriage. The ending chooses modest consolation: not escape or closure, but a renewed claim on “here,” expressed through the porch and the next act of measuring.

## Evidence line
> The whole house was stitched together with places where two lives had brushed past each other, carrying things, saying *excuse me* and *love you* and *did you see my glasses*.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the recurring measurements, domestic objects, and red-and-blue map imagery make the story’s attention to ordinary intimacy unusually coherent and distinctive.
