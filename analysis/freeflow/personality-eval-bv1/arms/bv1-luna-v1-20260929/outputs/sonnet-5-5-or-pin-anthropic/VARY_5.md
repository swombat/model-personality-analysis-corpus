## Grounded reading
The voice is wry and observant, using small comic details—the unimpressed cat, the “good tuna”—to keep grief from becoming grand or sentimental. Its central ache is regret over an unanswered voicemail, made tangible through the grandmother’s measurements and the phone fourteen steps away. The story invites the reader to notice how practical habits can carry love, and how vague intentions can disguise avoidable distance.

## What the model chose to foreground
The model foregrounds a notebook of measurements, household routes, a phone, and the ordinary labor of reaching people. It makes care legible through distance and repeated acts of counting, then turns inherited grief into a decision to call the living. The moral emphasis is direct: love can be postponed by keeping its demands vague.
