## Grounded reading
The voice is patient and attentive, using the precision of Ines’s craft to make room for feelings that are difficult to name directly. The story’s pathos gathers around Dorothea’s discovery that a piano restored to pitch no longer carries the sound she has associated with her dead husband. Its central metaphor—that tuning is a fair sharing of imperfection—lets the story honor both the comfort of memory and the value of meeting the present. The reader is invited to listen closely, without treating either correctness or sentiment as the whole truth.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds the piano as both a working instrument and a vessel of memory: dust, broken strings, tuning tools, a hymn, and one final A connect physical repair with mourning. It gives moral weight to compromise and to the small deviations by which people accompany one another. Its resolution favors accepting the piano’s new pitch while preserving the past in a shared song, rather than restoring the old sound.
