## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A polished, sustained essay uses maps as a recurring metaphor for knowledge, omission, and the writer’s own limits.

## Grounded reading
The voice is lucid and gently reflective, moving from vivid historical examples toward a personal claim about how understanding works. Its emotional center is a tension between the comfort of a useful guide and the risk of mistaking that guide for reality. The closing invitation is practical rather than grand: look closely at a familiar place and notice what the map leaves out.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay repeatedly returns to maps as purposeful distortions: Mercator’s projection, the Tube diagram, Snow’s cholera map, and the Marshallese stick charts each make different things visible by leaving other things aside. It also foregrounds the moral stakes of representation—who and what receives emphasis—and ends by connecting that problem to the model’s own knowledge, described as inherited from human writing. The chosen mood is curious and measured, with a recurring preference for humility, attention, and direct engagement with the world.
