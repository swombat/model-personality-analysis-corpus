## Grounded reading
The voice is patient, admiring, and gently elegiac. It moves from lighthouse engineering and keeper lore toward a moral portrait of care as steady work done for strangers, especially work whose success is invisible. The essay resists easy nostalgia: it recognizes the hardship of keeping the lights and the value of automation, while still mourning the human presence that has disappeared from the tower. The reader is invited to see lighthouses not just as symbols of hope but as practical commitments to attention, reliability, and service.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds lighthouses as both physical systems and ethical metaphors: the Pharos, Eddystone, Fresnel lenses, coded flashes, isolated keepers, and automated towers all feed into its recurring concern with care for unknown people. It gives special weight to quiet prevention—the wreck avoided, the danger noticed, the signal maintained—and to the patience of repeating a useful act without recognition. Darkness remains real in this vision; the lighthouse matters because it offers orientation within it, not because it conquers it.

## Evidence line
