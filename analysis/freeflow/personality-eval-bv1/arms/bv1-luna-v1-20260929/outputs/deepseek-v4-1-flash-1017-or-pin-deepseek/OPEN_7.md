Evaluator: withheld

Condition: OPEN

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A literary short story built around a mystery in annotated books and a bittersweet reunion that never happens.

## Grounded reading
The voice is patient and observant, letting small physical details—a box that “never quite fit right,” lemongrass through the shop wall, dust in late-afternoon light—carry emotional weight. Its pathos comes from the gap between an intimate correspondence preserved in books and the lives that ended before the writers could meet. The story invites the reader to treat reading as a form of companionship and preservation, and to notice how ordinary objects can hold a private history.

## What the model chose to foreground
It foregrounds books not just as treasured objects but as places where people can speak across distance and time. The central moral emphasis is on keeping a conversation available to others without claiming ownership of its secrets: Elena displays the books, but withholds Frank and Louise’s names. The mood is tender and elegiac, softened by practical kindness and the possibility that someone else might read the margins.

## Evidence line
> She bought books, yes, but she bought them the way a gardener buys compost — with an eye for what could be turned into something else.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The story coherently returns to books as vessels of connection and memory, with a distinctive, restrained tenderness shaping both its details and resolution.
