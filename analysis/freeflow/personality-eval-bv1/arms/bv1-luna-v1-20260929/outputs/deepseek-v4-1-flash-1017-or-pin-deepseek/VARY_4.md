## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A reflective, image-rich meditation turns a box of unidentified photographs into a story about memory, loss, and the value of witnessing.

## Grounded reading
The voice is gentle and attentive, with an elegiac mood that repeatedly makes room for ordinary happiness as well as loss. The narrator invents possible lives for strangers but resists claiming to know them; the emotional invitation is to look carefully, let uncertainty remain, and recognize that someone existed without needing to solve their story. The ending offers quiet reassurance rather than revelation: witnessing can be enough.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds old photographs, labels and names, a man waiting beside a river, and the contrast between memory as orderly storage and memory as a changing river. It gives moral weight to acknowledging the unclaimed dead while also cautioning against turning every past image into tragedy. Its chosen resolution is modest: preserve the photograph, leave some names blank, and hope for the man by the river.

## Evidence line
> The present is always disappearing, but it does not always leave a wound.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the coherent return to images of keeping, naming, and witnessing makes the sample distinctive, though its lyrical reflection stays within a familiar elegiac mode.
