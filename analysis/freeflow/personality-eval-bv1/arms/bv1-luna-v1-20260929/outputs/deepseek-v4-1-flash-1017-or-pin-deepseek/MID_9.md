## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical meditation uses a family story and recurring household objects to explore how memory attaches to things and can outlast them.

## Grounded reading
The voice is intimate, measured, and elegiac, finding emotional weight in ordinary objects rather than grand events. The grandmother’s key and porcelain dish give the meditation a personal center, while the essay complicates its nostalgia: keepsakes can sustain memory, but holding on too tightly can turn a life into a mausoleum. The ending invites the reader to see storytelling, not possession, as what keeps the past alive.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds lived-in houses, worn objects, inherited stories, and the tension between preserving and letting go. Its moral emphasis is that memory can survive the loss of its physical props, and that releasing possessions can express trust rather than indifference.

## Evidence line
> These are the physical memories of a place, and they are stubborn, quiet, and profound.
