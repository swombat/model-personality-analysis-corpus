## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical, first-person essay uses recurring images and personal ritual to make an argument about attention.

## Grounded reading
The voice is reflective and gently urgent, moving from the amber light and dust motes of an ordinary room to a larger claim about how attention lets us inhabit our lives. Its pathos comes from impermanence: loved ones, places, and versions of ourselves are already changing, which makes noticing feel less like self-improvement than a way of honoring what is here. The essay also turns attention outward, presenting it as recognition of other people’s inner lives and a form of care. It invites the reader to try small, concrete acts—looking out a window, walking without a podcast, letting boredom remain—without promising that they will fix anything.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay foregrounds late-afternoon light, dust, ordinary household objects, quiet and boredom, and a repeated contrast between presence and distraction. It treats attention as a discipline, a form of love, and a refusal to let life pass unwitnessed; the recurring images and the evening window ritual give those claims a personal, sensory frame.

## Evidence line
> The corollary is that noticing is a form of love.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the essay sustains a distinctive lyrical image of fleeting light and connects it to attention, love, and impermanence, while its polished argument keeps the voice relatively controlled.
