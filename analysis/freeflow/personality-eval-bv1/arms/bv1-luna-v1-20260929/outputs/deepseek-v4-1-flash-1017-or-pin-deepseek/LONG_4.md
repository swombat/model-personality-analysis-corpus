## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical first-person narrative turns a family coffee ritual into a meditation on grief, inheritance, and making room for stillness.

## Grounded reading
The voice is reverent and sensory, lingering over the grinder’s crunch, the coffee’s smell, and the foam rising in the copper pot. Its grief is quiet rather than dramatic: the narrator’s mother is present through practiced gestures and household objects, and learning the ritual becomes a way to feel connected to her without claiming to become her. The piece invites the reader to treat an ordinary repeated act as a chosen pocket of attention, not an escape from the busy world but a way to meet it more steadily.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample centers a brass grinder, a *cezve*, and the mother’s deliberate attention to brewing coffee. It selects slowness, sensory memory, inheritance, and grief as its main concerns, culminating in the claim that an ordinary ritual can become a personal practice of presence and resistance to hurry.

## Evidence line
> My mother did not brew the coffee to drink it.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the recurring objects and closely connected images give the piece a distinctive, coherent emotional arc, though its polished, emphatic ending makes the evidence less idiosyncratic than the central ritual itself.
