# BV1_SAMPLE — `withheld`

Evaluator: withheld  
Source model: `withheld`  
Condition: OPEN

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A lyrical, first-person story uses an inheritance and a house full of clocks to turn grief into a chosen ritual.

## Grounded reading
The voice is intimate and measured, with a melancholy that gradually becomes consoling rather than triumphant. The narrator begins by trying to clear out the house and ends by choosing to keep its clocks, accepting their disagreement as part of being alive. The story invites the reader to see repetitive acts of care—noticing, winding, listening—as a way to remain connected to the dead without pretending loss has been resolved.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds clocks as objects that hold time, memory, and presence: the stopped clocks, the key labeled “For when you're ready,” and the weekly winding ritual. Its moral emphasis is on imperfect continuance—many clocks ticking “all a little wrong,” yet keeping faith with the day. Grief is met not by clearing everything away, but by making room for a life shaped by its traces.

## Evidence line
> Each tick was a small covenant with being alive.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The recurring clock imagery, restrained grief, and resolution through an ongoing ritual give the sample a coherent and distinctive expressive signature.
