## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical essay turns the everyday practice of noticing into an argument about attention, care, and responsibility.

## Grounded reading
The voice is warm, assured, and playfully observant, finding significance in humble details: a crooked fence, a spoon against a bowl, dust in sunlight, a pigeon “who means to renovate.” Its pathos comes from the fear that speed and distraction leave other people and ordinary life unregistered. The reader is invited not to escape daily obligations, but to inhabit them more attentively and treat attention as a quiet form of generosity.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay repeatedly foregrounds attention as both perception and ethical practice: noticing the textures of cities and routines, recognizing one’s own impatience, and seeing other people as full lives rather than “small data points.” It contrasts measured, optimized modern life with unscripted encounters—children studying a leaf, swallows returning, a colleague gone quiet—and favors modest witness over spectacle or grand declarations. The mood is gently exhortative, with humor and sensory detail keeping its moral claims from becoming stern.

## Evidence line
> Noticing is the first inch of responsibility.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the piece is unusually coherent and distinctive in its recurring images and moral emphasis, especially its linking of close observation with generosity.
