Evaluator: withheld

Condition: LONG

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A sustained allegorical story uses an imagined museum to explore regret, alternate lives, and how to live with unfinished choices.

## Grounded reading
The voice is lyrical, humane, and gently wry, turning familiar regret into rooms, exhibits, and objects without treating any one road not taken as a secret better life. Its pathos lies in ordinary losses—a brother not called, a career left behind, a possible move—handled with tenderness rather than melodrama. The narrator’s gradual shift is not toward being “fixed,” but toward holding alternatives without living inside them. The invitation to the reader is to acknowledge what still aches, release what can be shelved, and accept that a life can remain unresolved and still continue.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds unsent messages, abandoned ambitions, alternate timelines, and the difference between remembering an unlived life and being trapped by it. Its recurring museum objects—glass displays, shelves, a dry fountain, a notebook—make memory something that can be handled without being erased. The moral emphasis is that no path should be ranked as the definitively right one, and that wanting more does not guarantee fulfillment. The ending chooses modest, ongoing forms of connection and attention over a neat reconciliation or verdict.

## Evidence line
> The museum did not tell me which almosts to relinquish and which to keep.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the sample’s distinctive museum conceit, recurring motifs, and consistent movement from regret toward acceptance make its chosen emotional and moral emphasis especially clear.
