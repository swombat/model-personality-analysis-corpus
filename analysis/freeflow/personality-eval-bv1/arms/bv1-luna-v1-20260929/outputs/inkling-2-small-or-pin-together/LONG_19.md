## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW. It is a lyrical, sustained meditation whose recurring objects and self-conscious narrator give it a more distinctive voice than a conventional public-intellectual essay.

## Grounded reading
The voice is patient and warmly observant, finding meaning in ordinary things—hinges, benches, kitchens, library margins—rather than in spectacle. Its pathos comes partly from the narrator’s stated distance from bodily life: it can describe sunlight and salt, but cannot experience them. That limitation is presented plainly, then turned into an invitation to notice more carefully, not as a claim to human experience. The reader is asked to slow down, make room for boredom and complexity, and treat attention as a practice of care.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample repeatedly turns everyday objects and spaces into evidence of how people live: rooms suggest whether to hurry or stay; libraries enable unbought encounters; margins make room for resilience. It connects deliberate noticing to ethical responsibility, especially seeing who bears costs or is excluded. Technology is framed as neither inherently good nor bad, but as a tool that can either support human life or capture attention. The chosen mood is hopeful but unsentimental: small acts of attention matter, though they do not replace policy or sacrifice.

## Evidence line
> Attention is care made visible.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the coherent recurrence of everyday objects, patient attention, and care makes these choices unusually legible in this sample, though much of the essay’s form remains polished and familiar.
