## Grounded reading
The voice is lyrical and gently exhortative, moving from small scenes—a child dropping a spoon, noticing a bird, keeping an unanswered question—to claims about culture and attention. Its pathos comes from the sense that adult life and technology can narrow wonder, while exhaustion and grief can make curiosity difficult; the essay treats that narrowing with sympathy rather than blame. It invites the reader to protect space for unhurried inquiry and to meet both the world and other people with more attention.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay foregrounds curiosity as more than intelligence or information retrieval: it presents curiosity as contact with the world, a form of care, and a check on certainty. Repeated ordinary objects and scenes—the spoon, ant trails, streetlights, birds, rain—make noticing concrete. It also emphasizes the social conditions that sustain or suppress inquiry, from schools and workplaces to libraries, safety, sleep, and silence.
