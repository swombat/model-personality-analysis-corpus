Evaluator: withheld

## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A polished, metaphor-rich reflection that turns ordinary city life into an argument for attention and care.

## Grounded reading
The voice is self-aware about the pressure to make free writing either confession or performance, then finds its footing in concrete images: the baker, crossing guard, nurse, mechanic, screws, and unsent messages. Its pathos is gentle rather than tragic; it notices fatigue, routine, and the ways care often goes unnamed. The essay repeatedly converts noticing into an invitation to address others with small acts of kindness, ending by urging the reader to open up to meaning already present in everyday life.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds urban details and ordinary labor as sources of “ridiculous holiness,” with attention cast as both a moral practice and a way to resist hurry. Maintenance, competence, rest, friendship, and small expressions of gratitude recur alongside images of thresholds, doors, lighthouses, and packages. The chosen moral claim is that meaning is abundant but easily missed—and that noticing matters most when it becomes humane acknowledgment.

## Evidence line
> Attention is a moral technology.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The essay sustains a distinctive ethic of attention through recurring images of ordinary work and repeated invitations to notice and thank people.
