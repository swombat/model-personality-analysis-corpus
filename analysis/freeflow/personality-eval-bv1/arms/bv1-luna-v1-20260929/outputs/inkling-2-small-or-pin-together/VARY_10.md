## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW. A lyrical, carefully structured meditation turns a drawer of discarded objects into an image for unfinished intentions.

## Grounded reading
The voice is intimate and self-aware, using small, tactile details—the orphaned earring, obsolete key, dead plant’s receipt—to make unfinished efforts feel both tender and faintly comic. Its pathos comes from the pull between avoidance and hope: an unsent letter may be a refuge from accountability, yet the drawer is also a “rehearsal room” where old intentions remain alive. The reader is invited to look at their own almosts without treating them as proof of failure, and to see beginning again as a small, human motion rather than a grand resolution. The late disclosure of the writing prompt makes the essay’s imagined personal history part of its meditation on how a blank page gets filled.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds unfinished objects and actions, the emotional meanings attached to ordinary possessions, and the difference between leaving something unresolved and declaring it worthless. Its recurring moral claim is that interrupted intentions can still contain meaning, while its counterpoint is that some things remain unfinished because completing them would make them accountable. The explicit turn toward the machine and the blank page makes the act of writing itself one of the essay’s central subjects.

## Evidence line

Medium: the distinctive object imagery, sustained “almost” motif, and self-conscious turn toward the writing condition make this a coherent and unusually revealing sample.
