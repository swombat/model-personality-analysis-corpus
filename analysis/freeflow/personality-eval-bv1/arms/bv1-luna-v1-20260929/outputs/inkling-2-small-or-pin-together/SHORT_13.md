## Grounded reading
The voice is warm, observant, and gently playful: buses have “sighing bodies,” pigeons become briefly philosophical, and the baker’s smell suggests “tomorrow being prepared.” Its pathos lies in the smallness and brevity of the morning’s promise, not in dramatic hardship. The passage invites the reader to notice a world before its routines and certainties take over.

## What the model chose to foreground
The model foregrounds dawn, urban routines, bread, and brief glimpses of people and animals beginning their day. It treats morning as a temporary “truce” and a shared chance to choose between quarreling, hurrying, noticing, or making toast. That emphasis makes attention to ordinary life feel like a quiet moral choice.
