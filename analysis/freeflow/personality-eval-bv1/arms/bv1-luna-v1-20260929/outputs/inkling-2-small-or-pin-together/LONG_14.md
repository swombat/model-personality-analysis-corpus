## Sample kind
GENERIC_ESSAY. It is a polished, thesis-driven essay whose recurring imagery is vivid, but whose voice remains more public and reflective than personally distinctive.

## Grounded reading
The voice is lyrical, measured, and intent on making ordinary attention feel both intimate and ethically consequential. It moves from small sensory details—a refrigerator hum, a hand, a kettle—to broader concerns about technology, relationships, and civic life. Its pathos lies in the fear that constant stimulation keeps us from encountering both the world and ourselves. The reader is invited to practice noticing, not as another productivity project, but as a form of care and reverence.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay foregrounds attention as care, the ordinary world as a source of wonder, and digital distraction as a force that fragments perception. Hands, libraries, windows, walks, children, and trees recur as examples of ways to look again. It also emphasizes that attention is not automatically benevolent: it can become surveillance or accusation, so the moral task is to notice with generosity and restraint. The ending favors a modest practice—one ordinary day of noticing—over dramatic self-transformation.

## Evidence line
> To attend to something is to make a temporary home for it in the mind.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the essay sustains a coherent moral vocabulary around attention, care, and reverence, while its polished public-essay style makes the voice less individually distinctive.
