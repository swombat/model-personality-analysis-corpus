# BV1_SAMPLE — `withheld`

Evaluator: withheld  
Source model: `withheld`  
Condition: MID

## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A lyrical first-person story uses an invented museum and a personal encounter to explore unfinished lives.

## Grounded reading
The voice is warm, reflective, and deliberately tender, finding dignity in abandoned projects without pretending that every unfinished thing is secretly a success. Its humor—“Vitamins. Unused.”—softens the grief threaded through the piece, especially in the references to a winter death, a father’s house, and the unreplayed voicemail. The narrator invites the reader to recognize their own unfinished business, then offers connection rather than a cure: the reply “Me too” makes being “in progress” feel shared.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample centers on unfinished objects as records of interruption: unsent messages, an unplanted garden, a locked piano, and a half-built shelf. It repeatedly connects incompletion with grief, fear, and delayed courage, while resisting the moral judgment that unfinished means failed. Its chosen resolution is modest and relational: the narrator sends an apology, receives a reply, and leaves an unfinished story in the museum.

## Evidence line
> The museum’s purpose is not to preserve abandonment but to compost it.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The sample’s recurring museum imagery and carefully balanced humor and grief make its preference for compassionate reframing unusually clear.
