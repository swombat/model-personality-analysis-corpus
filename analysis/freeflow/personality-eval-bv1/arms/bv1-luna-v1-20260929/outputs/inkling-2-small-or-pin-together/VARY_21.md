## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW. A lyrical, first-person meditation turns a night-shift diner into a setting for an argument about attention and care.

## Grounded reading
The voice is tender and observant, with a knack for making ordinary details carry emotional weight: one ice cube in orange juice, a crooked parking-lot light, flour on a cuff. Its pathos comes less from dramatic revelation than from small signs of loneliness and grief, met with restraint. The narrator also checks the impulse to turn noticing into ownership or self-congratulation. The invitation to the reader is practical: witness people without claiming their stories, then do the smallest decent thing available.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds night workers, regulars, and the private burdens visible in mundane routines. Its recurring objects—the diner table, tea, napkin, streetlight, compass—make attention feel like a form of orientation and companionship. The central moral claim is that noticing creates responsibility, not special status; care means staying present without interrogation or possession.

## Evidence line
> People believe comfort is softness. Often it is simply witness without interrogation.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the sustained imagery and repeated emphasis on restrained, attentive care make this a distinctive sample with a clear moral center.
