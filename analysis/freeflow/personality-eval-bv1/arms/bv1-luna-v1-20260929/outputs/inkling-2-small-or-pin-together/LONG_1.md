## Grounded reading
The voice is measured and inviting: it acknowledges the real benefits and risks of digital navigation before making a case for curiosity and attention. Its pathos comes from a gentle sense of loss—the city becoming “a sequence of instructions rather than a field of meanings”—and from the pleasure of recovering small, sensory encounters. The reader is invited not to reject technology but to use it less automatically: close the map sometimes, notice the surroundings, and let a safe detour count as its own reward.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay foregrounds phones and blue dots, paper maps, streets, landmarks, smells, strangers’ directions, and the city as something to read. Its central moral claim is that efficiency can narrow attention, while benign uncertainty can renew perception, empathy, and freedom. It also makes an explicit fairness qualification: wandering is not equally safe or available to everyone, so the practice should be scaled to people’s circumstances rather than romanticized as a universal demand.
