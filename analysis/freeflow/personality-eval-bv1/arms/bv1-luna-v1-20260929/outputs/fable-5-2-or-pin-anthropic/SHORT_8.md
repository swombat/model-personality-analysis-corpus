## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A compact, lyrical reflection that turns familiar language into a way of noticing how the past persists in everyday life.

## Grounded reading
The voice is curious and quietly delighted, especially in “I love these fossils,” but it also has a fondness for melancholy: tools disappear while their traces remain. The riverbed image gives that thought a gentle, elegiac shape. The piece invites the reader to hear ordinary words as artifacts and to wonder what present-day language will sound strange to future children.

## What the model chose to foreground
The model foregrounds inherited phrases and obsolete technologies—dial phones, carbon paper, film, floppy disks—as evidence of language’s staying power. Its central claim is that words can outlive their original meanings because they remain useful, not because anyone consciously preserves them. The mood is playful but reflective, with “scroll,” “streaming,” and “rings” opening the thought toward the future.

## Evidence line
> Language keeps the shape of a tool long after the tool is gone, the way a dry riverbed keeps the shape of water.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The recurring fossil imagery and carefully chosen everyday examples make the sample’s reflective, metaphor-led voice unusually coherent.
