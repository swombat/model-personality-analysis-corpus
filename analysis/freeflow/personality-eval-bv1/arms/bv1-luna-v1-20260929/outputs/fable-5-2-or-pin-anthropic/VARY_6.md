## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A reflective, metaphor-led essay that turns an ordinary phrase into a personal meditation on tacit knowledge and trust.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant and warmly analytical, moving from old recipe cards to the risks of writing without instructions. Its pathos comes from knowledge that cannot be fully passed down: a grandmother’s hands, a vanished ingredient, a kitchen the next generation never knew. The essay invites the reader to see omissions not just as missing information but as signs of intimacy and assumed competence—and to consider what it means to be trusted to fill a gap.

## What the model chose to foreground
The model foregrounds handwritten community recipes, kitchens, inherited skill, and the difference between explaining steps and teaching someone what to notice. It pairs the comfort of shared knowledge with the sadness of its loss, then makes the recipe metaphor an account of its own experience with an open-ended prompt. The closing emphasis is on judgment: freedom can be a form of trust, but it leaves room for failure.

## Evidence line
> The omissions are a portrait of a whole world of shared competence, drawn in negative.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the essay sustains a distinctive metaphor across domestic history, inherited knowledge, and self-reflection, with recurring attention to trust and what cannot be written down.
