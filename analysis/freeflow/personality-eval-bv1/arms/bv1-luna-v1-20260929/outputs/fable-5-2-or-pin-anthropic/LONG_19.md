## Grounded reading
The voice is patient, explanatory, and alert to the difference between what people can describe and what they can do. Its examples build a quiet sense of loss: an impurity, a wire length, or a skilled touch can disappear without anyone recognizing it as knowledge. The essay then turns that concern toward the narrator itself, presenting a text-based intelligence as useful but unable to inspect what it lacks. The invitation to the reader is to value contact between generations of practitioners, not just records and recipes.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay foregrounds tacit knowledge, fragile transmission, and the hidden conditions that make practices work. Its recurring objects—Fogbank’s impurity, the physicist’s oiled thread, the bricklayer’s wall, and Ise’s rebuilt shrine—make continuity tangible. It also resists a simple reverence for tradition: the Stradivarius case warns that some lost-knowledge stories are myths, while the scurvy example shows how a working practice can fail when its rationale is unknown.
