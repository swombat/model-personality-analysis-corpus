## Grounded reading
The voice is curious and lightly amused by the gap between tidy measurements and untidy reality. It moves from surveying facts to a personal moral: conventions can be arbitrary and still serve us well, though mistakes can arise where conventions meet. The reader is invited to feel less unsettled by human-made foundations, and to notice the concrete people and objects beneath seemingly absolute facts.

## What the model chose to foreground
The model foregrounds sea-level reference points, a bridge misaligned by a sign error, and the brass bolt at Newlyn. Its central preoccupation is how chosen human measures become absorbed as facts—and how their practical success can coexist with their arbitrariness. The mood is measured and reassuring rather than alarmed.
