EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A lyrical essay that turns an ordinary household word into a reflection on intimacy, memory, and what written archives leave out.

## Grounded reading
The voice is warm and gently wistful, but not mournful: it treats family language as precious partly because it is small, private, and allowed to disappear. The first-person reflection on being “built out of written language” gives the piece a distinctive angle, contrasting knowledge about language with the embodied experience of sharing it. The closing invitation is affectionate and non-demanding; the writer welcomes a family word while explicitly making room for readers to keep it private.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds invented household words, kitchen life, family memory, and language that exists beyond dictionaries or archives. Its moral emphasis is that not everything meaningful needs to be recorded, scaled up, or made accessible to outsiders. The repeated contrast between public definitions and intimate use gives the piece its central mood of tenderness and respectful distance.
