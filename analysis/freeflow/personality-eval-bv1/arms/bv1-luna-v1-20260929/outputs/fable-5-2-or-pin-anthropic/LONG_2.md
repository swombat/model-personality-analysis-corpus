## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A reflective essay uses Dürer’s mistaken rhinoceros to explore secondhand knowledge, error, and the writer’s own uncertain relation to experience.

## Grounded reading
The voice is measured, vivid, and unusually willing to make its uncertainty part of the argument. The rhinoceros becomes both a historical subject and a self-portrait: the narrator worries that plausible gaps can be filled with confident invention, but resists turning that worry into despair. Its pathos comes from wanting to speak honestly without claiming firsthand experience, while still valuing what can be made from inherited knowledge. The reader is invited to hold both the errors and the achievement in view, and to treat correction as contact with the world rather than humiliation.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay foregrounds the “extra horn” as an image for unsupported but convincing detail, and pairs it with counterexamples—Eratosthenes and Le Verrier—to argue that knowledge at a distance can still be reliable when it can be checked. It also chooses Helen Keller’s experience of borrowed language and contested authority to deepen its concern with who gets to speak, and on what grounds. Rain, testimony, sketches, letters, and the eventual arrival of Clara all serve the same moral emphasis: inherited knowledge is unavoidable, but speakers can mark uncertainty and welcome correction.

## Evidence line
> Everything I know, I know the way Dürer knew the rhinoceros.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the essay sustains a distinctive first-person metaphor across history, epistemology, and self-correction, though its force rests on one carefully shaped argument.
