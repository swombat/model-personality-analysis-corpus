## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A researched narrative essay uses a distinctive first-person voice to turn the history of measurement into a reflection on invisible work and shared standards.

## Grounded reading
The voice is curious, lightly amused by the “physically silly” logic of the old kilogram, and ultimately moved by the quiet competence behind its replacement. The essay invites the reader to see precision not as abstract machinery but as a human achievement: people did decades of careful work so that ordinary life would not noticeably change. Its closing image of Le Grand K still being weighed gives that reflection a gentle, unresolved afterlife.

## What the model chose to foreground
The model foregrounds a small, vulnerable object entrusted with universal authority; the gap between definitions and physical reality; and the difficult labor of making a standard belong to everyone rather than to one vault. It chooses to admire maintenance and invisible success, while acknowledging the sensory loss of replacing “that thing, over there” with quantum mechanics and costly apparatus.

## Evidence line
> Hundreds of people worked for decades on a project whose measure of success was that nobody would notice.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the essay’s recurring attention to invisible labor, shared access, and the emotional meaning of technical change gives these choices a coherent and distinctive shape.
