## Grounded reading
The voice is curious, lightly amused, and increasingly unsettled by its own examples. It starts with the charming absurdity of *dord*, then turns toward a more consequential question: how records and authorities shape the things they claim merely to describe. The personal admission that the writer’s knowledge comes from text gives the piece a stake beyond wordplay. It invites the reader to enjoy the odd histories while reconsidering what makes something real, correct, or trustworthy.

## What the model chose to foreground
The model foregrounds dictionaries, maps, misprints, deliberate copyright traps, and the people who adopt what they find in reference works. Its central moral claim is that authority is neither a perfect mirror nor simply untrustworthy: it is a human process that can be corrected, but can also help make its own records true. The recurring mood is delight shading into “dizziness,” with *dord* and Agloe serving as vivid objects for that shift.
