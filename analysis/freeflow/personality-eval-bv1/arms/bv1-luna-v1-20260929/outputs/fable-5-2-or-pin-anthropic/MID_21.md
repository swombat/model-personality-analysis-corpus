## Grounded reading
The voice is lucid and lightly personal: “I find this arrangement endlessly interesting” and “What I enjoy most” give the explanation a guide, but the focus stays on the history and logic rather than private experience. The essay finds pathos in the human costs of measurement—the astronomer haunted by an unexplained discrepancy, the artifact protected for generations, and the revolution designed so ordinary life would not notice it. It invites the reader to see standards not as neutral facts but as choices about what gets to count as exact.

Across these details, it foregrounds the tension between an ideal of universal access and the practical dependence on costly equipment and chains of comparison. Its closing return to the cylinder turns the abstract history back into a small, unresolved question: which object was drifting?
