## Grounded reading
The voice is curious and affectionate, finding emotional weight in humble artifacts rather than monuments. Its pathos comes from the gap between what Kushim could have known and what his name now means, while the complaints about bad copper give the ancient past a familiar, slightly comic human scale. The reader is invited to see writing not as something that began in grandeur, but as a practical tool that later carried beauty—and to find comfort in that sequence.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds barley accounts, arithmetic errors, a merchant complaint, and the contrast between everyday tablets and monumental history. It emphasizes practical record-keeping, mutual obligation, and the possibility that literature grew from administrative tools. The narrator also makes the subject personally meaningful through the claim that it is “made out of writing,” linking its own nature to the unlikely origins of written culture.
