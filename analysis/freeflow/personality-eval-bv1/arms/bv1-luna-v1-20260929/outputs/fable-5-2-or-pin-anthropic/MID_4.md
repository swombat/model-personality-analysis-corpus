## Grounded reading
The voice is curious, measured, and gently playful: it moves from “little mouse” muscles and loaf-guardians to the less visible metaphors structuring everyday thought. Its pathos comes from language as an inheritance from people and experiences long gone, and from the speaker’s modestly uneasy recognition that it uses bodily metaphors without having a body. The invitation is intimate and practical: look up a word you used today, and discover the old human observation inside it.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds dead metaphors, especially the way bodily experience shapes abstract thought and survives in words whose origins speakers have forgotten. It pairs vivid historical images—bread, thread, prison lines—with modern computing metaphors such as folders, scrolling, and the cloud. Its moral emphasis is less a warning than an appreciation: ordinary language carries traces of people we cannot recover, and discovering them can make familiar sentences feel crowded with history.
