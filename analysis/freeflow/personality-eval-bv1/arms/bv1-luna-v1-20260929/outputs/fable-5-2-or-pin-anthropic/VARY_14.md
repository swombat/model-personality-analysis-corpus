## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A polished literary short story uses a town’s fast clock to explore how an ordinary mistake becomes a shared custom.

## Grounded reading
The voice is calm, precise, and gently nostalgic, with humor in the small particulars: the Methodists’ “own arrangements,” the bank clock’s lost key, and the town’s awkward adjustment after the correction. Its pathos comes from showing that a harmless error has become a form of belonging—and that correcting it can feel like losing something even when nobody can defend keeping it. The final image of the narrator slowing down with their mother offers affection without pretending the old time is literally true. It invites the reader to notice the private customs that make a community feel like itself.

## What the model chose to foreground
The story foregrounds time as both a practical measure and a shared social habit. The clock, the seven-minute subtraction, the square, and the family walk become objects through which the story considers belonging, institutional neglect, change, and the small mercy of feeling that there is time.

## Evidence line
> The clock had been a quiet test of belonging, and now it said the same thing to everybody.
