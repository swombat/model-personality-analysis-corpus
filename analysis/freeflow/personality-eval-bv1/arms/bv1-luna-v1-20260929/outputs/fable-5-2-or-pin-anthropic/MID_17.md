EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW. It is a polished historical essay, but its recurring bodily imagery and personal asides give it a distinctive, reflective voice.

## Grounded reading
The voice is curious and quietly intimate: it moves from a factual history of measurement to moments of personal fascination, then back out to questions of fairness and shared knowledge. Its pathos gathers around the human effort hidden inside supposedly neutral units—from oxen’s fatigue to Méchain’s concealed anxiety—and the reader is invited to notice how abstraction still depends on lived experience. The ending returns the argument to a walk across a field, making the history feel physical rather than merely technical.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds bodies, labor, and the practical cost of measurement: furrows, arm spans, workdays, and even the distance a reindeer walks before urinating. It also emphasizes the political stakes of standards, especially how inconsistent measures could be used to cheat people, while presenting universal scientific units as a real achievement. Its central tension is not old measures versus modern ones, but embodied measures alongside shared standards.
