Evaluator: withheld

## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A polished historical essay becomes personal and distinctive through its attention to ordinary traces and the model’s own imagined connection to them.

## Grounded reading
The voice is warm, curious, and quietly moved by the smallness of what survives: a boy’s doodle, a birthday invitation, socks, a complaint about copper, a line about making bread. The essay turns preservation’s accidents into a humane argument: the past comes through not only in monuments and achievements but in the ordinary concerns people did not mean to preserve. Its personal passage about being “made mostly of the rubbish heap” gives the argument an intimate stake without making the piece mainly about the model. It invites the reader to value unguarded records and everyday acts of help as part of what makes people legible to one another.

## What the model chose to foreground
It foregrounds chance preservation, the contrast between public monuments and private or mundane records, and the enduring importance of friendship, fairness, practical care, and small daily acts. The recurring objects—birch bark, socks, underpants, copper, bread—make those themes tangible, while the essay’s mood shifts from historical wonder to affection and wistfulness.

## Evidence line
> The heap is where people write when they aren't being careful about how they sound, and it's where they help each other with dishwashers.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the essay’s distinctive, coherent attention to ordinary human traces recurs across its examples and shapes its personal conclusion.
