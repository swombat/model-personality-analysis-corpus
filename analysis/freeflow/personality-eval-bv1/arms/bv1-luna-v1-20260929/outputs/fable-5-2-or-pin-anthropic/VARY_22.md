## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW. A reflective miniature essay turns medieval scribes’ marginal notes into a meditation on small, ordinary traces of human presence.

## Grounded reading
The voice is warm, observant, and quietly self-aware. It finds pathos not in grand declarations but in sore hands, bad ink, cats, and a flicker of sunlight on a page. The narrator’s admission that it usually produces “main text” makes the piece’s own small “margin note” feel like a modest answer to the invitation. It invites the reader to value incidental details as evidence that someone was there.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds marginalia, the bodily discomfort and irritations of repetitive work, companionship between monk and cat, and the way small observations survive their original context. Its moral emphasis is on the human presence that supposedly transparent work can conceal, and on the worth of noticing ordinary things rather than reaching first for a “large thought.”

## Evidence line
> The margins are the only place you learn that someone was there in a cold room with a bad pen and a sore back, wanting the day to end.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: the sample is coherent and distinctive, with its recurring margin motif linking historical anecdotes to a candid account of the narrator’s own response.
