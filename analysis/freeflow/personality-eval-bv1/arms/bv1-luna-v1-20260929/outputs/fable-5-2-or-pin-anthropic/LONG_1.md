## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A reflective essay with a distinctive recurring metaphor and an unusually direct turn toward the speaker’s own text-bound knowledge.

## Grounded reading
The voice is patient, curious, and fond of complicating a clean takeaway. It starts with the annoyance of “cook until done,” then uses recipes, bicycles, lasers, steel, and medical checklists to explore what written instructions can and cannot carry. Its pathos is quiet: knowledge can vanish with a generation, and even expertise may not know which of its habits matter. The speaker’s admission that it has never touched dough gives the argument personal stakes without making the essay a confession. The invitation is to notice where instructions direct attention, while accepting that some understanding arrives only through practice and feedback.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay foregrounds tacit knowledge, the gap between instructions and lived practice, and the need for writing and hands-on experience to correct each other. It repeatedly chooses concrete, memorable details—the length of a wire, grease from a nose or ear, an impurity left in a material, dough compared to an earlobe—to make invisible knowledge tangible. It also resists romanticizing either side: skilled practice can preserve useless rituals, while checklists and written records can prevent errors and outlast practitioners.
