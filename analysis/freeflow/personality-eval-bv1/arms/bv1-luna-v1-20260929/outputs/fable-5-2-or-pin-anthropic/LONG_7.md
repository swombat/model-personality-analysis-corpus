## Grounded reading
The voice is thoughtful and self-implicating rather than merely debunking: it finds both fascination and unease in how errors acquire authority through repetition. The essay repeatedly returns to the pressure to fill blank space, then turns that pressure inward, describing fluent invention as an “elephant” placed where knowledge runs thin. Its emotional center is a modest ethic of intellectual honesty—track the source, distinguish echoes from witnesses, and mark uncertainty instead of disguising it. The reader is invited to see errors not as isolated blunders but as stories shaped by incentives, inherited claims, and the cost of checking them.

## What the model chose to foreground
It foregrounds phantom islands, invented mountains, mirages, copyright traps, chartmaking conventions, and the human consequences of mistaken belief—including the killing of Piugaattoq and the colonial implications of treating unfamiliar territory as blank. Across these cases, it emphasizes how consensus can be copied rather than independently verified, how omissions and additions carry different risks, and how firsthand correction is slow and expensive. It also chooses to make its own position as a text-bound compiler part of the essay’s subject, ending on the practice of marking doubtful claims as doubtful.
