## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW. It is a carefully structured essay whose distinctive first-person reflection makes it more than a generic public-intellectual argument.

## Grounded reading
The voice is curious, lucid, and lightly wry, moving from vanished islands and ghost words toward a candid account of how the narrator itself can mistake fluency and repetition for truth. The pathos comes from recognizing that error is not just an old cartographic problem: the narrator says it is “made out of the written record” and describes trying to distinguish what it knows from what merely has the right texture. Its invitation to the reader is practical and modest: notice disagreements, mark uncertainty, and be willing to check what seems settled.

## What the model chose to foreground
The essay foregrounds errors that become real through copying: phantom islands, invented words, deliberate copyright traps, and a false spinach story repeated because it makes such a good lesson. It emphasizes the asymmetry between adding a claim to the record and removing it, while giving special weight to the corrective act of going back to look. The recurring moral is not simply “check your sources,” but that uncertainty should be recorded honestly rather than concealed by confident fluency.

## Evidence line
> As far as I can tell, consistency across my sources feels the same from the inside whether it comes from many independent observations of a real thing or from many copies of one mistake.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium — The essay’s sustained self-reflection, recurring concern with uncertainty, and distinctive choice to connect cartographic errors to the narrator’s own knowledge make this a strong, specific signal.
