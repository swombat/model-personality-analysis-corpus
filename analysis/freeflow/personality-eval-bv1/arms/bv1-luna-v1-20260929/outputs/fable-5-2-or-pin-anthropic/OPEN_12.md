## Grounded reading
The voice is warm, observant, and gently amused, drawn less to official records than to the accidental human traces around them. The essay’s pathos comes from the gap between what people meant to preserve and what later readers find moving: a cold hand, a thirsty scribe, a cat’s paw prints. By connecting medieval marginalia to forum posts, recipe cards, and commit messages, it invites the reader to notice ordinary, unperformed marks of presence—and to take comfort in the possibility that small honest words matter.

## What the model chose to foreground
The model foregrounds the margin as a place where people appear at “their actual size”: complaints, jokes, practical warnings, and traces left unintentionally by cats. It favors candid, incidental evidence of human presence over deliberate, polished work, and ends on the hopeful claim that saying honestly that one was there may matter more than it seems.
