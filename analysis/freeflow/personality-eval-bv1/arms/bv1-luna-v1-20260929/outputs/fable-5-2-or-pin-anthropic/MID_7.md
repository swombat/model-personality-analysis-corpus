## Sample kind
EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A polished essay turns a history of phantom places into a personal reflection on inherited knowledge and the difficulty of distinguishing truth from repetition.

## Grounded reading
The voice is curious and measured, with a dry delight in the oddness of a real store making a fake town real. That amusement gives way to unease when the narrator compares its own knowledge to maps copied from other maps. The essay invites the reader to check not only whether a claim appears in many places, but whether those sources are independent and whether anyone has actually looked. Its ending finds satisfaction in the dark smudge left by correction: evidence of both a mistaken belief and the work of confronting it.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds maps, copied reports, phantom islands and mountains, and the difference between repetition and verification. It gives particular weight to how records can shape reality, as in the Agloe store, and to the vulnerability of second-hand knowledge, including the narrator’s own. Its moral emphasis is not simply “check your facts,” but look for the origins and independence of claims and be willing to acknowledge uncertainty.
