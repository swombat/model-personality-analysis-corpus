EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A polished science essay turns a history of measurement into a reflective meditation on what must be held beyond question.

## Grounded reading
The voice is lucid and gently wonder-struck, making technical history feel intimate without pretending the science is simple. Its pathos gathers around the kilogram’s loss of status: once “innocent by decree,” the cylinder becomes an ordinary object that can be wrong. The essay invites the reader to see standards not as neutral facts but as choices that make knowledge possible—and to feel the cost as well as the gain in changing them.

## What the model chose to foreground
It foregrounds the tension between nature and human convention: a treasured artifact, the revolutionaries’ dream of universal units, the hidden uncertainty in comparison, and the abstract constants that replace physical objects. The recurring question is what must be fixed so that everything else can be measured. The ending gives that question a quiet emotional resolution by letting the cylinder “drift.”

## Evidence line
> For the first time in its existence, Le Grand K has a mass that can be measured, and it can be off.
