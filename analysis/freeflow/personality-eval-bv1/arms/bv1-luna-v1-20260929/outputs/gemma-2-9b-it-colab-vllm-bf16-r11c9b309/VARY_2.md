## Sample kind
GENRE_FICTION — A lyrical, melodramatic story of an aging keeper whose grief and sense of duty are bound to the lighthouse he must leave behind.

## Grounded reading
The voice makes the lighthouse more than a setting: Silas calls it “her,” and its presence becomes a vessel for his solitude, his work, and his memories of Grace. Sensory details—the salt air, gulls, foghorn, and lavender perfume—make grief feel embedded in the landscape. The ending offers consolation through release rather than reunion: the reader is invited to feel the weight of Silas’s attachment, then accept that his service and mourning can end.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds bereavement, aging, duty, and the difficulty of letting go. Its recurring images of sea, storm, beacon, and sentinel link Grace’s death to the lighthouse’s eventual failure, while the rescue and final image turn loss toward peace and completed service.

## Evidence line
> She was the lighthouse, the limestone monolith that had been his solitude, his solace, his burden for fifty years.

## Confidence for persistent model-level pattern
Medium: The story coherently returns to grief, service, and release, with the personified lighthouse and sea-bound imagery giving those choices a distinct, sustained shape.
