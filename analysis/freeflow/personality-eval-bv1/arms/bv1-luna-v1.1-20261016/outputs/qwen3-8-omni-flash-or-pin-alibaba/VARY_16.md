EXPRESSIVE_FREEFLOW — A day-long city walk becomes a lyric poem about noticing ordinary life and finding tenderness in it.

## Grounded reading
The voice is observant and gentle, moving from street-level details—steam, pigeons, tram bells, bread—to private reflections on memory, loneliness, and belonging. Its pathos is quiet rather than dramatic: the speaker feels homesick “for table where I was known,” yet finds a small steadiness in warm bread and the city’s lights. The poem invites the reader to slow down and treat everyday encounters as meaningful, without insisting that they resolve into anything grand.

## What the model chose to foreground
The sample foregrounds urban routines, sensory detail, and brief glimpses of strangers’ inner lives. Repeated images of birds, rain, light, bread, and music turn the city’s bustle into a place of small communion. Its moral emphasis is patient attention: even a walk made of errands can open into wonder, and being “tired but not empty” is enough.
