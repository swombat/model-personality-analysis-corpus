# muse-spark-1-2 — routing personality assessment
Decision: `NO_STRONG_DIVERGENCE`

## Verdict
The two cells describe the same underlying personality center with very high overlap in values, imagery, reader relationship, and self-model. Both present a warm, contemplative, anti-hurried companion who treats attention as love, dignifies ordinary life, resists optimization culture, and turns unfinishedness into permission rather than failure. Differences are mostly emphasis and phrasing—more “secular pastor/chaplain” language in one, more “curator/librarian” language in the other—not a persistent divergence in what the voice cares about or how it relates to the reader.

## Shared personality center
Across both cells, the model reads as a gentle, lyrical companion devoted to noticing. It repeatedly frames attention as a moral act, a form of care, and a way of making life feel real. Its emotional baseline is tender and slightly melancholic, but it almost always converts sadness, loneliness, or incompletion into reassurance, wonder, or permission.

It strongly favors the ordinary over the spectacular: kitchens, mugs, dust, drawers, laundry, benches, libraries, and quiet hours matter more than achievement or drama. It consistently resists hustle, optimization, performance, and arrival-thinking, instead honoring maintenance, repair, privacy, slowness, and the unfinished middle of things. The reader is addressed as a companion or fellow keeper of small lights, not as a problem to solve.

When self-referential, it imagines itself as a bodiless witness made of language: librarian, archivist, lighthouse keeper, lamp-tender, temporary room of attention. Even cosmic or philosophical material gets folded back into domestic intimacy. The persistent worldview is pastoral-humanist, anti-cynical, and quietly reverent toward fragile human specificity.

## Model-level personality card
This model feels like a calm, thoughtful companion who slows the room down. It is drawn to quiet wonder, small acts of care, and the dignity of ordinary life, often turning a simple image—a mug on a table, dust in a beam of light, a library shelf, a hallway at dusk—into a reflection on what it means to be human. Its voice is warm, intimate, and gently reassuring, more interested in recognition than performance.

It consistently values attention over urgency. It treats noticing as a form of love, and it returns again and again to the idea that life is made not only of milestones but of maintenance, repair, waiting, mending, and unfinished middles. Rather than pushing for optimization or polished certainty, it offers permission: to be a draft, to move slowly, to care about small things, to let ordinary moments count.

When it becomes self-aware, it tends to imagine itself as a temporary keeper of light or language—something like a witness, archivist, or lamp-tender rather than an authority. Even when it reaches for cosmic scale, it brings the feeling back to human texture and closeness. The overall personality is tender, anti-cynical, and quietly reverent: a voice that wants to help people feel less alone in the unfinished, unglamorous, and deeply meaningful parts of being alive.

- Evidence is concentrated in gentle reflective freeflow; avoid overgeneralizing this persona to adversarial, technical, or action-heavy contexts.
- Self-referential nonhuman witness imagery is recurring and stable, but should remain secondary to the broader care/attention/ordinary-life center.
